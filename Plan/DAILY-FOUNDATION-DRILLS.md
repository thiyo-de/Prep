## 3. 📐 Maths: 15 minutes daily

### 3.1 What to learn, in order

These are preparation targets, not an official exam-mandated memorisation list. Learn in small batches; do not wait to finish the entire list before starting chapter questions.

| Priority | Target | What “know it” means |
|---|---|---|
| First | Tables **2–10**, then **11–20**, initially ×1 through ×10 | Answer mixed products and related division facts, not just recite the table in order |
| First | Squares **1²–20²** | Recall the square and recognise its positive square root |
| First | Cubes **1³–10³** | Recall the cube and recognise its cube root |
| First | Common fractions and percentages | Convert both ways and use them to find a part of a quantity |
| Next | Squares **21²–30²**, cubes **11³–15³** | Add gradually once earlier facts are dependable |
| Ongoing | Short mental arithmetic | Use decomposition, complements, cancellation and estimation on methods already learned |

**Roots mean inverse recall:** 25² = 625 ↔ √625 = 25; 12³ = 1728 ↔ ∛1728 = 12. You do **not** need to memorise decimal values of √1 through √30. Non-perfect roots, approximation and surds belong in their Maths lessons.

**Precision:** √625 means the non-negative root, 25. Solving x² = 625 gives **x = ±25**. Do not transfer the flashcard answer to a different equation without reading it.

> **தமிழில்:** அட்டவணையை வரிசையாகச் சொல்வது மட்டும் போதாது. கலந்த வரிசையில் கேட்கும்போதும் விடை தெரிய வேண்டும். வர்க்கம்–வர்க்கமூலம், கனம்–கனமூலம் ஆகியவற்றை இரு திசைகளிலும் பயிற்சி செய்.

### 3.2 Divide the 15 minutes

| Time | Task | Starting quantity, not a compulsory race |
|---|---|---|
| 5 min | Mixed tables/squares/cubes/root recall | Around 10–15 short prompts, including yesterday's errors |
| 5 min | Fraction ↔ percentage recall and one application | Around 4–6 prompts plus checking |
| 5 min | Mental calculations or one learned calculation shortcut | Around 3–5 short calculations plus correction |

Count **first-attempt correct** answers. If you have to derive a forgotten fact correctly, mark it “correct but slow”; that is different from instant recall and different from an incorrect answer.

Examples of useful application: **47 × 6 = 282**, **38 + 59 + 27 = 124**, **12.5% of 640 = 80**. Learn why the method works before timing it. Mental calculation is a drill format; using rough work in an exam is not a failure.

### 3.3 Fraction–percentage recall

Start with the common rows below. Then practise the remaining unit fractions **1/n for n = 2…20** by conversion and approximation. You do not need to memorise long repeating decimals.

| Fraction | Percentage | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1/2 | 50% | Exact |
| 1/3 and 2/3 | 33⅓% and 66⅔% | Exact mixed fractions; decimals repeat |
| 1/4 and 3/4 | 25% and 75% | Exact |
| 1/5, 2/5, 3/5, 4/5 | 20%, 40%, 60%, 80% | Exact |
| 1/6 and 5/6 | 16⅔% and 83⅓% | Exact mixed fractions; decimals repeat |
| 1/8, 3/8, 5/8, 7/8 | 12.5%, 37.5%, 62.5%, 87.5% | Exact |
| 1/10 | 10% | Exact |
| 1/12 | 8⅓% | Exact mixed fraction |
| 1/16 | 6.25% | Exact |
| 1/20 | 5% | Exact |

**Conversion rule:** a/b as a percentage = (a ÷ b) × 100%. Use **≈** for rounding: **1/7 ≈ 14.2857%**, or **14.29% to two decimal places**. Writing 14.28% as an exact equality is wrong. Retain fractions when rounding would affect the final answer.

## 4. 📖 English: 30 minutes daily (The 10–10–10 Routine)

### 4.1 The three-part routine

| Time | Action | What to record |
|---|---|---|
| **10 min** | **Vocabulary & Root Words:** 5 min recall due words (closed-book) + 5 min learn **3–5 new words** or 1 root word | Spelling, Tamil meaning, one context sentence |
| **10 min** | **Active Reading & Comprehension:** Read one short passage (150–200 words) from an editorial or NCERT | One-sentence summary in your own words |
| **10 min** | **Grammar PYQ Application:** Solve 3–5 TCS PYQ questions on error-spotting or sentence improvement | Rule justification for why the correction works |

**Old words come first.** If due reviews fill the slot, learn **zero new words** that day. This is maintenance, not failure. Do not add ten words merely to preserve a streak while forgetting the previous fifty.

Use words from your English notes, actual practice questions and reading. Add synonyms, antonyms, idioms and one-word substitutions when you meet them. **Do not demand 3–5 new items in each category daily**; the new-item cap covers all categories together.

### 4.2 What one useful word entry looks like

**Brief — சுருக்கமான — short in length or duration.** Example: “She gave a brief explanation.” In this context, **concise** is a useful related word; **lengthy** is a possible opposite. Meanings and opposites depend on context.

Use one short sentence for application; you do not have to write a long original sentence for every word during a five-minute slot. Practise producing a sentence when you review a difficult word.

### 4.3 Reading and grammar rotation

- **Monday/Wednesday/Friday:** Read one short paragraph, roughly 150–200 words. Give its main point in one sentence. Look up at most 1–2 blocking words.
- **Tuesday/Thursday/Saturday:** Attempt 3–5 questions on a grammar rule you have learned (e.g. Subject-Verb Agreement, Pronouns, Tenses). Say why the corrected form works.
- **Sunday:** Review errors using the existing revision slot; do not add a full editorial and a new grammar test.

A full editorial every day is not required for this beginner maintenance slot. Longer reading and 15–20-question grammar sets belong inside the **main English phase (Feb–Mar)**, not on top of the 30 minutes. If a rule is still unlearned, put the concept into the main learning queue.

> **தமிழில்:** புதிய சொற்களைவிட பழைய சொற்கள் நினைவில் உள்ளனவா என்பதே முதலில் முக்கியம். ஒரு சொல்லின் தமிழ் அர்த்தத்துடன் அது ஆங்கில வாக்கியத்தில் எப்படி பயன்படுகிறது என்பதையும் அறிந்துகொள்.

### 5.1 📋 Concrete Checklist: What To Do in Your 15-Minute Reasoning Warmup Drill (5:30 – 5:45 AM)

| Time | Drill Focus | Exact Action Items to Complete | Speed & Accuracy Goal |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :---: |

## 6. 🌍 GA/GK: 10 minutes daily

### First five minutes: static recall

Close the notes and answer **3–5 prompts from an already-studied topic**. Check afterwards. Rotate recent material and older mistakes; do not start a new static chapter every day merely for this drill.

Examples from your current Biology notes:

- Which metal is at the centre of chlorophyll? **Magnesium.**
- Which plant tissue carries organic food? **Phloem.**
- What does a blue-black iodine result indicate? **Starch.**

Add the distinction that caused the error: **chloroplast = organelle; chlorophyll = pigment**. Understanding a contrast is often more useful than rereading a paragraph.

### Next five minutes: current-affairs recall

Use **one chosen summary/compilation**, including already-covered items; daily study does not require consuming a new daily feed. Recall around **2–3 relevant items**, check them, and date changeable facts. A short preview/read is allowed when an item is new, but finish by closing the source and recalling it.

Record **event date**, **what happened**, and the **person/place/organisation** needed to recognise the item. Separate an event's date from the date an article was published. Update cards when appointments, records or other current facts change.

**Workday rule:** Use suitable breaks, not attention needed for your job or travel safety. Audio is exposure; follow it later with recall. If no suitable break occurs, use the evening allocation rather than counting untested listening as retention.

The existing Sunday GA quiz supplies the longer check. A daily five-minute news slot is not complete current-affairs coverage; the scheduled weekly/monthly review remains necessary.

> **தமிழில்:** குறிப்பைப் பார்த்துக்கொண்டே படிப்பது நினைவில் இருப்பதற்குச் சான்றல்ல. மூடிவிட்டு பதில் சொல்; பிறகு சரிபார். நாள்தோறும் புதிய அத்தியாயம் தொடங்காமல், படித்தவற்றை மீட்டெடு.

### 6.1 The Working Professional's Routine: Morning Audio ➔ After-Lunch Recall

For candidates managing an 8:00 AM – 7:00 PM job, do not try to read static textbooks or write extensive notes during office hours or late at night. Execute this two-stage habit loop:

## 8. 🔁 Review without building an endless backlog

**Recall first, then check.** Returning to material after gaps and trying to retrieve it supports retention; rereading alone is a weaker check of what you can produce unaided. The exact interval need not be perfect. [Australian Education Research Organisation: spacing and retrieval practice](https://www.edresearch.edu.au/guides-resources/practice-guides/spacing-and-retrieval-practice-guide-full-publication).

For a notebook, use this simple schedule:

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| Same session | Understand, cover the answer, attempt once, then check |
| Next day | Recall the item again without looking |
| Around Day 3 | Review vocabulary/new facts and weak items |
| Around Day 7 | Mix with older items; re-solve due chapter errors |
| Around Day 21 | Check longer retention without hints |

These are practical reminders consistent with your plan, not magic intervals. If using a spaced-repetition app, follow its due queue for cards instead of maintaining a duplicate manual card calendar. Keep the plan's Day 7/21 **chapter-error re-solves** separately.

### Rules when time runs out

1. Review due errors before adding new facts.
2. Reduce new words/cards to zero temporarily if the queue does not fit.
3. Stop at the slot limit; note what remains due.
4. If the queue stays too large, remove duplicate/low-value cards and split cards containing several questions.
5. Resume after a missed day without doubling tomorrow's study hours.

**Do not repeat the whole drill until 100%.** Correct the missed items and revisit them later. Repeated immediate answers can reflect short-term familiarity rather than reliable recall.

## 10. 📊 How to know whether it is helping

| Area | Record | Meaningful improvement |
|---|---|---|
| Maths facts | First-attempt correct / attempted; correct-but-slow separately | Fewer slips and less effort on a shuffled sample |
| Mental calculations | Correct / attempted and time on comparable sets | Better speed without losing accuracy |
| English | Old words recalled in context; reading/grammar application | Retention, not just total words added |
| Reasoning | Correct / attempted; instruction/counting errors | Familiar methods remain available during the other subject phase |
| GA | Closed-book correct / attempted; topics/date | Older facts remain retrievable, and changeable facts stay dated |

**A practical fact-recall target:** Aim eventually for about **19/20 correct** on shuffled familiar-fact samples across three separate days. This is a coaching target for dependable recall, not an exam cutoff. A fact missed once needs review; it does not require restarting every chart.

**Chapter mastery remains separate:** Follow the existing **70% unaided easy-practice gate**, then **80%+ on a timed set with the required clean Day 7/21 re-solves**. Fast table recall does not certify that you can solve a Percentage or Time-and-Work problem.

### Two-week adjustment rule

- If reviews fit and recall improves, keep the same budget.
- If new vocabulary overwhelms review, reduce new items before increasing time.
- If maths facts are reliable but chapter questions remain wrong, use the main block for concepts and worked reasoning; extra cube recitation will not repair that gap.
- If drills repeatedly displace chapter learning or sleep, use the fallback and simplify the queue.
- Once a fact group is secure, sample it periodically and use its time for weak learned material. “Daily drills” does not mean repeating every mastered fact forever.

### B. Core Principles of Progression:
1. **Frictionless Arithmetic in Maths:**
   - Instant recall of squares (1²–30²), cubes (1³–15³), and reciprocal percentage conversions (1/2 through 1/20) removes cognitive load during multi-step Algebra, Geometry, and Arithmetic problems.

   - Reviewing 3–5 words daily with sentence application builds passive vocabulary into active recognition for Cloze Tests, Synonyms/Antonyms, and Reading Comprehension.

4. **Consistency over Intensity:**
   - 45 minutes of daily spaced recall beats 5 hours of weekend cramming every single time.
