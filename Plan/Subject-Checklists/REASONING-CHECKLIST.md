Begin with instructions, letters, numbers and simple diagrams. Practise straightforward examples before combined conditions and timed sets. In syllogisms and condition-based puzzles, follow the supplied statements without adding real-world assumptions. Word analogy and classification may require vocabulary and background facts. There is no universal seconds-per-puzzle rule. Verbal and non-verbal chapters with similar names use different question formats.

## Four pillars — necessary foundations for solving questions

These are core learning requirements, not optional extras. Build the relevant skills alongside each chapter. Understanding a method alone is insufficient when the question also needs an unfamiliar word, fact or calculation skill. Repair that prerequisite before harder practice. You do not need to memorise every possible fact before starting easy questions, and these foundations do not guarantee every answer.

| Pillar | What to learn or demonstrate |
|---|---|
| Concepts | Explain the relationship or method, why the answer fits and why the alternatives fail. Learn the standard method before shortcuts. |
| Vocabulary | Understand unfamiliar words in context, including instructions such as only, except, neither, at least and at most. |
| Background knowledge | Learn factual relationships and category features. For unfamiliar tools or instruments, use a picture and a simple explanation of their purpose. |
| Practice: PYQs + tests | Progress from easy independent questions to untimed PYQs and timed sets; review wrong, skipped, slow and guessed answers. |

Word analogy and classification draw heavily on all four. Number series needs calculation foundations; figure-based chapters need visual concepts and practice. Do not add a factual memorisation task to every chapter.

Keep vocabulary and factual learning inside the existing lesson/correction time. Keep short relationship notes in the same notebook, with the source/page for unfamiliar facts. Use the existing English and GK notes where they cover the same material. Revision applies to all four pillars through the existing 7/21-day reviews.

**One knowledge record:** unfamiliar word/item → simple meaning → relationship/category → example or picture → source/page. Recall the relationship without looking, then apply it to a fresh question. Learn both what the items are and how they are related; memorising an answer option is insufficient.

### Error labels — repair the specific gap

| Label | What went wrong | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| Concept | I did not understand the relationship or method. | Revisit one explanation and solve easy examples independently. |
| Vocabulary | I did not understand a word or instruction. | Learn its contextual meaning and use it in an example. |
| Knowledge | I understood the words but did not know the fact. | Check a reference, record the relationship and recall it without notes. |
| Execution | I knew the method and facts but made a reading, calculation or time-management error, or guessed. | Record the specific cause and retry; use a short timed set when speed is the issue. |

These labels refine the existing error notebook; they do not require a second log. Label the underlying gap behind a guess; a correct guess is not evidence of mastery. Repair the identified gap within the current blocks and keep the other subjects moving.

## Common study rule — use for every topic

1. Understand the meaning through your course lesson and worked examples; define unfamiliar terms first.
2. Recognise the topic in a question, sentence or diagram and distinguish its main types.
3. Learn the relevant rules, formulas and exceptions. Keep short notes: meaning, method, one example and a mistake to avoid.
4. Solve an easy set independently. Reach **70% accuracy without help** before harder questions; otherwise repair and retest in existing practice time.
5. Attempt relevant chapter PYQs before reading solutions, then progress to harder and timed sets. Foundations need easy exercises first, not advanced PYQs.
6. Log wrong, skipped, slow and guessed questions with the error labels above. Re-solve errors without notes after **7 and 21 days**; use a fresh sample when there were no errors. Failed reviews return to repair.

**Tick a subtopic after learning it and applying it independently.** A tick is coverage evidence, not timed mastery. Record helped answers separately. Use one error notebook; the short record below each chapter points to it. For tests, record correct/wrong/skipped, duration and net marks in that notebook. Follow the [existing Red/Amber/Green rules](README.md#status-rules-from-the-current-plan).

**Order:** foundations → chapter basics → applications → harder PYQs and timed work within that chapter. The book codes stay unchanged; their numbers are not the learning order. Dates and time allocations remain in the master plan.
