## 2. 🧠 Movement first | இயக்கத்தைப் புரிந்துகொள்வோம்

### 2.1 Essential words

| English term | Tamil meaning | Simple meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Movement | இயக்கம் | Change in position of the body or a body part. |
| Locomotion | இடப்பெயர்ச்சி | Movement taking the organism from one place to another. |
| Vertebrate | முதுகெலும்புள்ள விலங்கு | Animal with a vertebral column: fish, snake, bird, human. |
| Invertebrate | முதுகெலும்பற்ற விலங்கு | Animal without a vertebral column: earthworm, snail, cockroach. |
| Skeleton | எலும்புக்கூடு / ஆதாரச் சட்டகம் | Supporting framework; not every animal's support is made of bones. |
| Joint / articulation | மூட்டு | A junction between bones; movement depends on joint type. |
| Contraction | தசைச் சுருக்கம் | Muscle develops tension; it often shortens, but not always. |
| Relaxation | தசைத் தளர்வு | Reduction of active muscle tension. |
| Voluntary | விருப்பத்திற்குட்பட்ட | Generally controlled consciously, such as choosing to lift a hand. |
| Involuntary | தன்னிச்சையான | Occurs without needing conscious control, such as heartbeat. |

### 2.2 Movement and locomotion

Blinking, chewing and turning your head are movements. Walking from your desk to the door is locomotion. **All locomotion is movement; not every movement is locomotion.**

Animals change place to obtain food, find shelter or mates and escape threats or unsuitable conditions. Legs, wings, fins, a muscular foot, cilia and flagella can help different organisms move.

| Situation | Movement? | Locomotion of the organism? |
|---|---|---|
| Eyelids blink while you sit | Yes | No |
| Heart beats | Yes | No |
| A bird flies to another tree | Yes | Yes |
| A fish swims across a pond | Yes | Yes |
| A snail crawls along a leaf | Yes | Yes |

> **தமிழில்:** உடலின் ஒரு பகுதி அசைவது இயக்கம். உயிரினம் ஓர் இடத்திலிருந்து மற்றோர் இடத்திற்குச் செல்வது இடப்பெயர்ச்சி. கண் இமைத்தல் இயக்கம்; நடந்து இடம் மாறுதல் இடப்பெயர்ச்சி.

<!-- separate learning boxes -->

> **⚠️ Exam trap: Energy and control**
>
> Active biological movement, including locomotion, needs energy. Locomotion is **not defined by conscious choice**: a single-celled organism does not need human-like voluntary control. The PDF's claim that locomotion need not require energy is unsuitable as a biological rule. Passive transport by wind or water is a different situation.

Breathing is normally automatic, but you can briefly alter its rhythm voluntarily. Therefore, “voluntary or involuntary?” needs the particular activity and context; do not put every movement into an absolute everyday label.

### 2.3 Three movement types highlighted in the PDF

| Type | Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Amoeboid | Temporary cytoplasmic extensions called **pseudopodia**, meaning false feet | Amoeba; certain white blood cells |
| Ciliary | Coordinated beating of short, hair-like **cilia** | Paramecium; cilia moving mucus in the respiratory tract |
| Muscular | Contraction of muscle cells | Walking, chewing, pumping blood |

**Supplement:** A flagellum is a longer, whip-like structure used in movement, for example the human sperm tail. The PDF's three headings are not a claim that no other movement mechanism exists.

> **தமிழில்:** அமீபா தற்காலிகப் போலிக்கால்களால் நகர்கிறது. பாரமீசியம் குறுஇழைகளால் நகர்கிறது. மனிதனின் நடப்பதில் தசைகள் செயல்படுகின்றன. எல்லா இயக்கங்களும் தசைகளால் மட்டுமே நடைபெறுவதில்லை.

**Correction:** Do not memorise “amoeboid and ciliary movements both occur in the lymphatic system.” White blood cells provide the amoeboid example; respiratory epithelium provides the ciliary example. [NCERT chapter reproduced by IIT Kanpur SATHEE](https://sathee.iitk.ac.in/sathee-neet/ncert-books/neet-ncert-books/neet-nb-bio-11/bio-11-chapter-20-locomotion-and-movement/).

**Stop check:** Give one movement without locomotion and one locomotion without legs. Explain why neither requires a bone-and-leg model.

## 3. 🐾 How animals move | விலங்குகளின் இடப்பெயர்ச்சி

### 3.1 Earthworm | மண்புழு

An earthworm has a segmented body and no bony backbone. Its body-wall muscles change the shape of its segments. Tiny bristles called **setae** grip the ground; mucus helps movement across the surface.

> **🔬 Process map: One earthworm crawling cycle**
>
> 1. Grip the ground with the rear part of the body.
> 2. Extend the front segments forward; circular muscle action makes them longer and thinner.
> 3. Anchor the extended front with setae.
> 4. Release the rear grip and shorten the body with longitudinal muscle action, pulling the rear forward.
> 5. Repeat these coordinated waves along the segmented body.

**Why it works:** Some parts grip while others move. Extension without anchoring would produce much less forward progress. Its fluid-filled body helps provide support: a **hydrostatic skeleton** is a useful supporting idea, not a bony skeleton.

> **தமிழில்:** மண்புழு உடலின் ஒரு பகுதியைச் சிறு முட்களால் தரையில் பிடித்துக்கொள்கிறது. மற்ற பகுதியை நீட்டி, பின்னர் சுருக்கி முன்னேறுகிறது. முதுகெலும்பு இல்லாததால் நகர முடியாது என்பது தவறு.

### 3.2 Cockroach | கரப்பான் பூச்சி

The cockroach is an insect with **three pairs of jointed legs: six legs altogether**. Muscles attached inside its chitinous **exoskeleton** move those legs.

Typical winged adults have **two pairs of wings**, but flight ability differs among species and sexes. Do not conclude that every cockroach flies well. Growth requires shedding the old outer covering, called **moulting**.

### 3.3 Birds | பறவைகள்

Bird forelimbs are modified into **wings**. Hindlimbs and claws assist walking, grasping and perching. A streamlined body, feathers and a light but strong skeleton assist flight; many bones are air-filled, but **not every bird bone is hollow**.

Powerful flight muscles attach to the breastbone, often to its projecting **keel**. The breastbone does not turn into a muscle.

| Flight word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Flapping | Active wing strokes help generate aerodynamic forces. |
| Gliding | Spread wings support travel through the air without continuous flapping; height is generally lost in still air. |
| Soaring | Rising air allows height to be maintained or gained with little flapping. |

> **தமிழில்:** பறவையின் முன்னங்கால்கள் இறக்கைகளாக மாறியுள்ளன. மார்பெலும்பில் வலுவான பறக்கும் தசைகள் இணைகின்றன. மார்பெலும்பே தசையாக மாறுவதில்லை.

### 3.4 Snake | பாம்பு

A snake is a **vertebrate** despite lacking ordinary walking legs. Numerous vertebrae, ribs and coordinated muscles make the body flexible. During familiar lateral slithering, bends push against the surroundings and help move the body forward.

Snakes can also use other locomotion patterns; some swim. “Every snake always moves by one identical looping pattern” is too absolute.

### 3.5 Fish | மீன்

Body and tail muscles produce waves that push against water. The streamlined body reduces resistance. Fins help with propulsion, balance, steering and braking, with their contributions varying between fish.

| Fin group | Usual examples | Main recognition point |
|---|---|---|
| Paired fins | Pectoral and pelvic | Usually **two pairs**, not two fins in total. |
| Median/unpaired fins | Dorsal, anal and caudal | More than one unpaired fin can occur. |
| Caudal fin | Tail fin | Major propulsive role in many fish; also contributes to manoeuvring. |

> **⚠️ Exam trap:** The PDF emphasises the tail when changing direction. Learn the fuller relation: body/tail action provides thrust, while several fins contribute to control. “Tail only steers” and “fish has only one unpaired fin” are not general rules.

### 3.6 Snail | நத்தை

A snail moves using wave-like contractions of a broad **muscular foot**, helped by mucus. The foot is part of its soft body; the shell is protective and is not its locomotor organ.

### 3.7 One-glance animal comparison

| Animal | Backbone? | Main movement equipment |
|---|---|---|
| Earthworm | No | Body-wall muscles + setae + fluid-supported body |
| Cockroach | No | Six jointed legs; wings in winged adults |
| Bird | Yes | Wings for flight; feet/claws for walking and perching |
| Snake | Yes | Flexible vertebral column, ribs and body muscles |
| Fish | Yes | Body/tail muscles and fins |
| Snail | No | Muscular foot and mucus |

> **📌 Remember:** No legs does not mean no backbone. A snake has a backbone; an earthworm does not. No backbone does not mean no supporting structure: a cockroach has an exoskeleton.

**Stop check:** Explain “earthworm - setae,” “snail - foot” and “cockroach - six legs” without mixing up the three animals.

## 4. 🦴 Skeleton and supporting tissues | எலும்புக்கூடும் இணைப்புத் திசுக்களும்

### 4.1 What the skeleton does

The skeleton gives support and shape, protects organs and provides levers and attachment points for movement. Bone stores minerals, especially calcium and phosphorus. **Red bone marrow makes blood cells**, including red cells, white cells and platelets.

Bones are living organs, not dead sticks. Bone tissue contains cells, a collagen framework and a mineral component. **Calcium phosphate** is the useful exam association for hardness; collagen contributes tensile strength and toughness.

> **தமிழில்:** எலும்புக்கூடு உடலைத் தாங்கி வடிவம் அளிக்கிறது; உறுப்புகளைப் பாதுகாக்கிறது. தசைகளுடன் சேர்ந்து இயக்கத்திற்கு உதவுகிறது. எலும்பு மஜ்ஜை இரத்த அணுக்களை உருவாக்குகிறது; எலும்புகள் தாதுக்களையும் சேமிக்கின்றன.

### 4.2 Four structures you must not confuse

| Structure | What it does | Clear example |
|---|---|---|
| Bone - எலும்பு | Rigid living support; protection and leverage | Femur supports the thigh. |
| Cartilage - குருத்தெலும்பு | Firm but flexible support; smooth cushioning at many joint surfaces | External ear, nose and articular surfaces |
| Ligament - எலும்பிணை நார் | Connects bone to bone and stabilises a joint | Ligaments around the knee |
| Tendon - தசைநார் | Transmits muscle pull to bone | Tendon attaching a muscle to a limb bone |

> **📌 Remember:** **Ligament = bone to bone. Tendon = muscle to bone.** Cartilage cushions; a joint is the meeting place. A tissue and the place where it acts are different answers.

Tendons contain abundant collagen fibres, traditionally called **white fibres**. They resist stretching, but “completely inelastic” and “made only of collagen fibres” are oversimplifications: tendon also contains cells, matrix and smaller amounts of other components. Use the functional distinction before a vague elasticity option.

Cartilage does **not all disappear in adulthood**. Adult joint surfaces, parts of the nose and external ear still contain it.

### 4.3 Exoskeleton and endoskeleton

| Support type | Position | Reliable examples |
|---|---|---|
| Exoskeleton - வெளிச்சட்டகம் | Outside the soft body tissues | Chitinous covering of cockroach; hard covering of crab/shrimp |
| Endoskeleton - அகச்சட்டகம் | Inside the body | Human, cow, whale and dinosaur skeletons |
| Hydrostatic support - திரவ ஆதாரம் | Fluid pressure works with muscles | Earthworm |

The source also groups scales, feathers and the tortoise shell under external coverings. These are not all equivalent to an insect's exoskeleton. A turtle/tortoise shell is integrated with the body and includes skeletal elements associated with ribs and vertebrae; it is not a detachable coat.

### 4.4 Bone shape: classify by form, not size alone

| Type | Main feature | Safe examples |
|---|---|---|
| Long | Longer than wide; shaft and ends | Femur, humerus, radius, ulna, tibia, fibula; metacarpals, metatarsals, phalanges |
| Short | Roughly cube-like | Carpals and tarsals |
| Flat | Thin, often curved, protective or broad muscle attachment surface | Ribs, sternum, scapula; many cranial bones |
| Irregular | Complex form outside the above groups | Vertebrae, hip bones, several skull bones |
| Sesamoid | Develops within a tendon | Patella, the kneecap |

The clavicle is classed as a long bone despite its unusual shape. A finger phalanx is a long bone even though it is much smaller than the femur. **Vertebrae are irregular, not short bones.** The hip bone belongs with irregular bones; do not confuse it with the flat scapula. [OpenStax: bone classification](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/6-2-bone-classification).

### 4.5 The count: 206 = 80 + 126

The conventional adult human count is **206 bones**. Individual variation exists. Newborn counts are approximate because separate elements later fuse: references commonly give **about 270-300**. The PDF itself uses both 270 and approximately 300 in different questions; neither is a universal exact newborn count.

> **🧠 Concept map: Two skeleton divisions**
>
> - **Axial - அச்சு எலும்புக்கூடு:** skull and associated bones, vertebral column, thoracic cage; **80** adult bones.
> - **Appendicular - இணை உறுப்புகளின் எலும்புக்கூடு:** girdles and upper/lower limbs; **126** adult bones.
> - **Total:** 80 + 126 = **206**.

| Axial parts | Number | Appendicular parts | Number |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Skull: cranium 8 + face 14 | 22 | Two clavicles + two scapulae | 4 |
| Middle-ear ossicles: 3 each side | 6 | Two upper limbs: 30 each | 60 |
| Hyoid | 1 | Two hip bones | 2 |
| Adult vertebral column | 26 | Two lower limbs: 30 each | 60 |
| Ribs 24 + sternum 1 | 25 | **Appendicular total** | **126** |
| **Axial total** | **80** | **Whole skeleton** | **206** |

Check the arithmetic: 22 + 6 + 1 + 26 + 25 = 80. [OpenStax: skeletal divisions](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/7-1-divisions-of-the-skeletal-system).

**Stop check:** Cover the table and rebuild the two totals. If you remember 206 but cannot place the scapula or sternum, repair the grouping before moving to timed questions.

## 5. 🧩 Meet the bones | முக்கிய எலும்புகள்

### 5.1 Skull, ear and hyoid

The usual skull count is **22 = 8 cranial + 14 facial bones**. The cranium encloses the brain. The mandible is the lower jaw and the freely movable bone among these 22; most other skull connections are sutures.

| Cranial bones | Count | Facial bones | Count |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Frontal | 1 | Maxillae | 2 |
| Parietal | 2 | Zygomatic | 2 |
| Temporal | 2 | Nasal | 2 |
| Occipital | 1 | Lacrimal | 2 |
| Sphenoid | 1 | Palatine | 2 |
| Ethmoid | 1 | Inferior nasal conchae | 2 |
| **Cranial total** | **8** | Vomer + mandible | **1 + 1** |
| | | **Facial total** | **14** |

**Recognition supplement:** Learn the totals first. These names help with close matching questions; there is no need to learn detailed skull openings in this chapter.

Each **middle ear** has three tiny ossicles: **malleus, incus and stapes**. Both ears together have six. The **stapes** is the smallest human bone. These are middle-ear bones, not inner-ear bones.

The **hyoid** lies in the neck and supports structures involved in tongue movement and swallowing. Counting 22 skull bones + 6 ossicles + 1 hyoid gives **29 skull-associated bones**, not a replacement for the strict 22-bone skull count.

> **⚠️ Exam trap:** The PDF's “forehead - 8” means **cranium - 8**. The frontal bone forming the forehead counts as one in the adult. “Only the mandible moves” refers to the usual 22 skull bones; the ear ossicles also transmit movement.

### 5.2 Vertebral column | முதுகெலும்புத் தொடர்

**Vertebra** means one backbone bone; **vertebrae** is plural. The vertebral column supports the head and trunk and encloses/protects the spinal cord.

| Region | Typical developmental vertebrae | Adult counting |
|---|---:|---|
| Cervical - neck | 7 | 7 separate vertebrae |
| Thoracic - chest | 12 | 12 separate vertebrae |
| Lumbar - lower back | 5 | 5 separate vertebrae |
| Sacral | 5 | Fused as **one sacrum** |
| Coccygeal | Usually 4 | Fused as **one coccyx** |
| **Total** | **Usually 33** | **26 bones = 24 + 1 + 1** |

**Atlas = C1; axis = C2.** Rotation between atlas and axis helps turn the head to signal “no.” Nodding “yes” mainly involves the joint between the occipital bone and atlas. These are not the same joint.

Intervertebral discs between most adjacent vertebral bodies contain fibrocartilage and help distribute loads. Facet joints between vertebral processes are a different, synovial joint connection. One region can contain more than one joint type.

> **தமிழில்:** முதுகெலும்புத் தொடர் எலும்புகளால் ஆனது. அதன் உள்ளே பாதுகாக்கப்படும் தண்டுவடம் நரம்புத் திசுவால் ஆனது. **முதுகெலும்பு ≠ தண்டுவடம்.** வளர்ச்சியின்போது சில எலும்புகள் இணைவதால் 33 கூறுகள் பெரியவரில் 26 எலும்புகளாகக் கணக்கிடப்படுகின்றன.

### 5.3 Ribs and sternum | விலா எலும்புகளும் மார்பெலும்பும்

The thoracic cage includes the **sternum**, ribs and thoracic vertebrae. Sternum means the flat breastbone at the front; it does not mean the entire rib cage.

| Ribs | Pairs | Front attachment |
|---|---:|---|
| True ribs: 1-7 | 7 | Attach to sternum through their own costal cartilage. |
| Vertebrochondral ribs: 8-10 | 3 | Attach indirectly through cartilage joining the rib above. |
| Floating ribs: 11-12 | 2 | No attachment to sternum at the front. |

**False ribs** can mean ribs **8-12**, with floating ribs as a subgroup. Some classroom tables list “false 3 pairs + floating 2 pairs” separately. Check which convention is being used.

There are **12 pairs = 24 ribs**. All attach posteriorly to thoracic vertebrae. Ribs help protect the heart and lungs and contribute to protection of upper abdominal organs. Intercostal muscles and the diaphragm help breathing; bones themselves do not contract.

### 5.4 Shoulder and pelvic girdles

| Girdle | Components | Limb connection |
|---|---|---|
| Pectoral / shoulder - தோள்பட்டை வளையம் | A clavicle and scapula on each side: **4 bones total** | Humeral head fits into the scapula's **glenoid cavity**. |
| Pelvic / hip - இடுப்பு வளையம் | Right and left hip bones: **2 bones total** | Femoral head fits into the **acetabulum** of the hip bone. |

Each adult hip bone forms from **ilium + ischium + pubis**. The **bony pelvis** includes the two hip bones, sacrum and coccyx. The **sacrum**, not the pelvic girdle, is formed from five fused sacral vertebrae. [OpenStax: pelvic girdle and pelvis](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/8-3-the-pelvic-girdle-and-pelvis).

The shoulder girdle has its bony connection to the axial skeleton at the **clavicle-sternum joint**, not a direct scapula-vertebral connection. The pelvic girdle transfers weight through the sacrum and hip joints.

> **தமிழில்:** தோள்பட்டை வளையம் - கிளாவிக்கிள் மற்றும் ஸ்கேபுலா. இடுப்பு வளையம் - இரண்டு இடுப்பு எலும்புகள். ஐந்து முதுகெலும்புகள் இணைந்து உருவாகுவது சாக்ரம்; அது முழு இடுப்பு வளையம் அல்ல.

### 5.5 Upper limb: count from shoulder towards fingers

| Part | Bones | Count per upper limb |
|---|---|---:|
| Arm | Humerus | 1 |
| Forearm | Radius + ulna | 2 |
| Wrist | Carpals | 8 |
| Palm | Metacarpals | 5 |
| Digits | Phalanges | 14 |
| **Total, excluding shoulder girdle** | **1 + 2 + 8 + 5 + 14** | **30** |

With palms facing forward in anatomical position, **radius is on the thumb side; ulna on the little-finger side**. The thumb has two phalanges; each other finger has three: 2 + (4 × 3) = 14.

**Hand including wrist = 27 bones**, whereas the entire upper limb = 30. The wrist joint, thumb base and elbow are not all hinge joints.

### 5.6 Lower limb: count from hip towards toes

| Part | Bones | Count per lower limb |
|---|---|---:|
| Thigh | Femur | 1 |
| Kneecap | Patella | 1 |
| Leg | Tibia + fibula | 2 |
| Ankle/hindfoot region | Tarsals | 7 |
| Mid/forefoot | Metatarsals | 5 |
| Toes | Phalanges | 14 |
| **Total, excluding pelvic girdle** | **1 + 1 + 2 + 7 + 5 + 14** | **30** |

**Tibia** is the larger, medial, main weight-bearing leg bone; **fibula** is lateral and more slender. **Talus** participates in the ankle joint; **calcaneus** forms the heel. The big toe has two phalanges; each other toe has three.

**Foot = 26 bones; whole lower limb = 30.** The patella supplies the extra bone that balances the difference between eight carpals and seven tarsals when comparing whole limbs.

## 6. 🔗 Joints: where bones meet | மூட்டுகள்

### 6.1 Two different ways to classify a joint

**Structural classification asks “what joins the bones?”** Functional classification asks **“how much movement is allowed?”** Keep these questions separate.

| Structural class | Construction | Usual example |
|---|---|---|
| Fibrous | Fibrous connective tissue joins bones; no synovial cavity | Skull sutures |
| Cartilaginous | Cartilage joins bones; no synovial cavity | Connections between adjacent vertebral bodies |
| Synovial | Articulating surfaces meet within a fluid-containing joint cavity | Shoulder, hip, elbow |

| Functional class | Meaning | Recognition example |
|---|---|---|
| Synarthrosis | Essentially immovable | Skull suture |
| Amphiarthrosis | Slightly movable | Intervertebral connection between bodies |
| Diarthrosis | Freely movable | Synovial joints |

Do not memorise “fibrous = always fixed” and “cartilaginous = always slightly movable” as exact identities. These structural classes include different functional behaviours. [OpenStax: classification of joints](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/9-1-classification-of-joints).

> **தமிழில்:** மூட்டு எதனால் இணைக்கப்பட்டுள்ளது என்பது **அமைப்பு வகைப்பாடு**. அது எவ்வளவு அசைகிறது என்பது **செயல்பாட்டு வகைப்பாடு**. Synovial என்பது அமைப்புப் பெயர்; diarthrosis என்பது அசைவைக் குறிக்கும் பெயர்.

### 6.2 Inside a synovial joint

| Part | Job |
|---|---|
| Articular cartilage | Covers the articulating bone surfaces; reduces friction and distributes load. |
| Synovial cavity | Space between the articulating surfaces containing synovial fluid. |
| Synovial fluid | Lubricates and helps nourish articular cartilage. |
| Synovial membrane | Inner lining of the capsule, except over articular cartilage; contributes to synovial fluid. |
| Fibrous capsule | Outer enclosure helping hold the joint together. |
| Ligaments | Reinforce and stabilise the joint. |

**Correction:** “Bones are not physically connected” is misleading. The bone surfaces are separated by the joint space, but capsule and ligaments connect and stabilise them. Cartilage covers the articulating surfaces, not every part of the cavity wall.

> **🔬 Process map: Producing a limb movement**
>
> 1. Nervous activity activates the appropriate skeletal muscles.
> 2. Muscles develop tension and transmit pull through tendons.
> 3. Bones act as levers around the joint.
> 4. Articular cartilage and synovial fluid reduce friction at the moving surfaces.
> 5. Ligaments and surrounding muscles help guide and stabilise the movement.

### 6.3 Movement words before joint names

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Flexion | Decreases the angle at a joint | Bending the elbow |
| Extension | Increases the angle / straightens | Straightening the elbow |
| Abduction | Away from the body's midline | Raising an arm sideways |
| Adduction | Towards the midline | Bringing the arm back towards the body |
| Rotation | Turning around an axis | Turning the head from side to side |
| Circumduction | A combined movement tracing a cone-like path | Moving an extended arm in a circle |

An **axis** is an imaginary line around which movement occurs. Uniaxial = one main axis; biaxial = two; multiaxial = several. A **plane joint** permits small sliding movements and is commonly called nonaxial. “Freely movable” does not mean unlimited movement.

### 6.4 Six synovial joint types

| Type | Shape and main movement | Reliable examples |
|---|---|---|
| Ball-and-socket | Rounded head in a cup; multiaxial movement including rotation | Shoulder and hip |
| Hinge | Hinge-like surface; mainly flexion/extension around one axis | Elbow; knee as a modified hinge; talocrural ankle joint |
| Pivot | Peg rotates within a ring; one main rotational axis | Atlas-axis; proximal radius-ulna |
| Condyloid / ellipsoid | Oval surface in an oval depression; biaxial | Radiocarpal wrist joint; knuckles; occiput-atlas |
| Saddle | Each surface is concave in one direction and convex in the other; biaxial | First carpometacarpal joint at thumb base |
| Plane / gliding | Relatively flat surfaces slide past each other | Intercarpal, intertarsal and vertebral facet joints |

The thumb-base saddle joint is between the **trapezium and first metacarpal**. The sternoclavicular joint is another saddle example; “shoulder joint” without qualification normally means the ball-and-socket glenohumeral joint. [OpenStax: synovial joint types](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology/pages/9-4-synovial-joints).

> **📌 Remember: Six anchors**
>
> - Shoulder/hip → ball-and-socket.
> - Elbow → hinge.
> - Atlas-axis → pivot.
> - Radiocarpal wrist → condyloid.
> - Thumb base → saddle.
> - Between carpals / vertebral facets → gliding.

<!-- separate learning boxes -->

> **தமிழில்:** தோள் மற்றும் இடுப்பு - பந்துக்கிண்ண மூட்டு. முழங்கை - கீல் மூட்டு. அட்லஸ்-ஆக்சிஸ் - சுழல் மூட்டு. மணிக்கட்டின் radiocarpal மூட்டு - நீள்வட்ட மூட்டு. கட்டைவிரல் அடிப்பகுதி - சேண மூட்டு. மணிக்கட்டு எலும்புகளுக்கிடையே - வழுக்கு மூட்டு.

### 6.5 Solve the close-option traps

**Wrist:** The radiocarpal joint is condyloid; joints **between carpal bones** are gliding. “Wrist” alone can be underspecified.

**Neck:** Nod “yes” mainly at occiput-atlas, a condyloid connection. Turn “no” mainly at atlas-axis, a pivot. Do not answer from the word “neck” alone.

**Knee:** Hinge is the usual elementary matching answer, but “modified hinge” is more precise because some rotation is possible when flexed.

**Rib-sternum:** The first sternocostal connection is cartilaginous; the second through seventh are synovial. The source's blanket “slightly movable cartilaginous joint” obscures this distinction. [OpenStax: cartilaginous joints](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/9-3-cartilaginous-joints).

**Planes and range:** Do not learn “gliding = unrestricted movement in three planes,” or “condyloid is always second greatest.” Plane joints allow limited sliding; actual range depends on the particular joint, capsule, ligaments and surroundings.

**Ear:** The source's “inner-ear saddle joint” is not a safe example. The ossicles are in the middle ear. Use the thumb-base example for saddle identification.

**Stop check:** Say the full location before naming a joint: “radiocarpal,” “intercarpal,” “atlas-axis.” If you cannot identify the bones, return to the six anchors.

## 7. 💪 Muscles and coordination | தசைகளும் ஒருங்கிணைப்பும்

### 7.1 How a muscle produces movement

Muscle develops tension. In the simple lifting action, it becomes shorter and thicker and pulls on its attachment. A muscle **pulls rather than actively pushing a bone** back in the opposite direction.

**Origin** usually means the relatively fixed attachment; **insertion** usually means the more mobile attachment. These are useful descriptions, not a rule that every muscle has exactly two attachment points or that one attachment can never move.

> **🔬 Process map: Bending and straightening the elbow**
>
> 1. To bend the elbow, activate the biceps and other elbow flexors.
> 2. Biceps tension pulls the forearm towards the upper arm.
> 3. In the basic opposing-pair model, triceps relaxes as biceps contracts.
> 4. To straighten, triceps contracts and supplies the opposite action.
> 5. Biceps relaxes in this simplified extension model.

Such opposing muscles are **antagonists**. Biceps is on the front of the upper arm; triceps is mainly at the back. “Bi-” and “tri-” refer to two and three heads of origin, not to two or three separate bones moved.

Real actions can include more than two muscles and simultaneous activation for stability. The source question “how many muscles move a bone?” intends the elementary **opposing pair**, not a universal anatomical count.

> **தமிழில்:** முழங்கையை மடக்கும்போது பைசெப்ஸ் சுருங்குகிறது; எளிய மாதிரியில் டிரைசெப்ஸ் தளர்கிறது. நீட்டும்போது டிரைசெப்ஸ் சுருங்குகிறது. எதிரெதிர் செயலைச் செய்யும் தசைகள் antagonistic muscles எனப்படும்.

### 7.2 Three muscle types

| Feature | Skeletal | Smooth | Cardiac |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tamil | எலும்புத் தசை | வரியற்ற / மென் தசை | இதயத் தசை |
| Stripes / striations | Present | Absent | Present |
| Typical cell shape | Long, cylindrical, unbranched | Spindle-shaped, tapering ends | Shorter, branched |
| Nuclei | Many; peripheral | One; central | Usually one, sometimes two; central |
| Control | Generally voluntary; also participates in reflexes | Involuntary | Involuntary |
| Main locations | Muscles moving the skeleton | Gut, vessels, bronchi, ureters, iris, arrector pili | Heart wall |
| Main role | Movement and posture | Movement/control within organs | Pumping blood |

**Supplement:** Cardiac cells connect through **intercalated discs**, helping coordinated activity. The heart has its own pacemaker activity; nerves alter its rate and force rather than consciously starting every beat.

> **⚠️ Exam trap:** **Striated does not automatically mean voluntary.** Both skeletal and cardiac muscles are striated. In some source questions, “striated muscle” is being used loosely to mean skeletal muscle; inspect the other clues.

Muscle tissue comparison checked against [OpenStax: muscle tissue and motion](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology/pages/4-4-muscle-tissue-and-motion?modal=KS).

### 7.3 Contraction does not always mean shortening

| Situation | Muscle behaviour | Recognition word |
|---|---|---|
| Lifting a weight | Active muscle shortens | Concentric |
| Holding the weight still | Develops tension without overall length change | Isometric |
| Slowly lowering the weight | Active muscle lengthens while controlling the load | Eccentric |

These are supporting explanations for the PDF's inaccurate “muscles cannot lengthen.” Learn the examples before the terminology. [OpenStax: control of muscle tension](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-4-nervous-system-control-of-muscle-tension).

Four properties appearing in the source options are **excitability** (respond to a stimulus), **contractility** (develop force), **extensibility** (can be stretched) and **elasticity** (return towards original form). Contractility is the direct force-generating property.

> **தமிழில்:** தசை விசை உருவாக்குவது எல்லா நேரமும் அதன் நீளம் குறைவதையே குறிக்காது. எடையை அசையாமல் பிடிக்கும்போதும் தசை வேலை செய்கிறது; மெதுவாகக் கீழிறக்கும்போது செயலில் இருக்கும் தசை நீளலாம்.

### 7.4 Iris, goosebumps and posture

| Example | Mechanism | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Pupil dilation | Radial iris muscle contracts; pupil widens | Radial does not constrict the pupil. |
| Pupil constriction | Circular iris muscle contracts; pupil narrows | Pupil is an opening; the iris contains the muscle. |
| Goosebumps | Small arrector pili smooth muscles contract | Not a voluntary skeletal-muscle action |
| Standing upright | Coordinated muscle activity maintains posture | No visible movement does not mean no muscle activity. |

The nervous, muscular and skeletal systems cooperate in walking, running and playing tennis. Muscles provide force; bones and joints provide the mechanical arrangement; nerves coordinate the activity.

**Stop check:** Explain why “involuntary + striated” is possible. Then explain how muscles can work even while a person holds an object still.

## 10. 📝 Worked models | வழிகாட்டும் எடுத்துக்காட்டுகள்

These **40 author-created models** teach eight topic groups, five models per group. Read the question, try it, then uncover the reasoning. They are not claimed as PYQs.

### 10.1 Movement and cellular mechanisms

**A1. A person blinks while seated. Movement or locomotion?**

1. The eyelid changes position.
2. The person does not change place.

**Answer:** Movement, without locomotion. **தமிழில்:** இமை அசைகிறது; மனிதர் இடம் மாறவில்லை. **Trap:** Do not require walking for something to count as movement.

**A2. A Paramecium swims using cilia. Is this muscular locomotion?**

1. The organism changes place, so it is locomotion.
2. The named mechanism is ciliary beating, not muscle contraction.

**Answer:** Ciliary locomotion. **தமிழில்:** குறுஇழைகளால் இடப்பெயர்ச்சி. **Trap:** Locomotion does not prove the presence of muscles or legs.

**A3. Which is correct: all movement is locomotion, or all locomotion is movement?**

1. Walking satisfies both definitions.
2. Blinking satisfies movement alone, disproving the first statement.

**Answer:** All locomotion is movement. **தமிழில்:** இடப்பெயர்ச்சி என்பது இயக்கத்தின் ஒரு வகை. **Trap:** Reverse the statement and test it with a counterexample.

**A4. A cell extends temporary projections and flows into them. Name the movement.**

1. Temporary projections are pseudopodia.
2. Pseudopodia identify amoeboid movement.

**Answer:** Amoeboid. **தமிழில்:** போலிக்கால்கள் உருவாகும் அமீபா வகை இயக்கம். **Trap:** A permanent hair-like cilium is a different structure.

**A5. Does energy-free locomotion follow from locomotion being involuntary?**

1. Conscious control and energy supply are different questions.
2. Active cellular processes still need energy without conscious control.

**Answer:** No. **தமிழில்:** விருப்பக் கட்டுப்பாடு இல்லாவிட்டாலும் உயிரியல் இயக்கத்திற்கு ஆற்றல் தேவை. **Trap:** “Involuntary” does not mean “unpowered.”

### 10.2 Animal locomotion

**B1. An earthworm extends its front but cannot grip the ground. Which missing aid matters?**

1. Extension alone does not securely anchor the body.
2. Setae provide the grip used during coordinated crawling.

**Answer:** Setae. **தமிழில்:** சிறு முட்கள் தரைப்பிடிப்பைத் தருகின்றன. **Trap:** Mucus helps movement but is not the same gripping structure.

**B2. A student counts three pairs of cockroach legs. How many legs?**

1. Each pair contains two legs.
2. Three pairs × two = six.

**Answer:** Six. **தமிழில்:** மூன்று ஜோடி = ஆறு கால்கள். **Trap:** Wings and antennae must not be included in the leg count.

**B3. A legless animal has many vertebrae and ribs. Snake or earthworm?**

1. Vertebrae establish that it is a vertebrate.
2. The snake fits; the earthworm has no vertebral column.

**Answer:** Snake. **தமிழில்:** கால்கள் இல்லாவிட்டாலும் பாம்பிற்கு முதுகெலும்பு உண்டு. **Trap:** Body shape does not determine vertebrate status.

**B4. A bird's breastbone becomes a flight muscle. Correct the sentence.**

1. Bone and muscle are different tissues.
2. Flight muscles attach to the breastbone/keel.

**Answer:** The breastbone provides attachment for flight muscles. **தமிழில்:** தசை மார்பெலும்பில் இணைகிறது. **Trap:** “Attaches to” does not mean “turns into.”

**B5. Match snail and fish to muscular foot and fins/body-tail action.**

1. Snail crawling uses the muscular foot and mucus.
2. Fish swimming uses coordinated body/tail movement and fins.

**Answer:** Snail → foot; fish → fins/body-tail action. **தமிழில்:** நத்தை - தசைக் கால்; மீன் - உடல், வால், துடுப்புகள். **Trap:** The snail's shell is protective, not its moving foot.

### 10.3 Supporting tissues and bone classes

**C1. Which connects a contracting calf muscle to a bone: tendon or ligament?**

1. Identify the two ends: muscle and bone.
2. A tendon transmits that muscle's pull.

**Answer:** Tendon. **தமிழில்:** தசை-எலும்பு இணைப்பு தசைநார். **Trap:** A ligament connects bone to bone.

**C2. A knee structure stabilises two bones without generating muscular force. What tissue is intended?**

1. The connection is bone to bone.
2. The stabilising fibrous band is a ligament.

**Answer:** Ligament. **தமிழில்:** எலும்பிணை நார் மூட்டை நிலைப்படுத்துகிறது. **Trap:** Cartilage covers/cushions surfaces; it is not the intended band.

**C3. A vertebra is small. Does that make it a short bone?**

1. Bone shape categories are not based simply on absolute size.
2. The vertebra has a complex irregular shape.

**Answer:** Irregular bone. **தமிழில்:** அளவு சிறியது என்பதால் short bone ஆகாது. **Trap:** Carpals/tarsals are the standard short-bone examples.

**C4. Which classification suits the patella?**

1. The patella is the kneecap.
2. It lies within a tendon, fitting the sesamoid category.

**Answer:** Sesamoid bone. **தமிழில்:** தசைநாருக்குள் உள்ள எள்ளுரு வகை எலும்பு. **Trap:** Its location near a long bone does not make it a long bone.

**C5. What produces blood cells: bone mineral or red bone marrow?**

1. Mineral gives bone important mechanical properties and storage capacity.
2. Blood-cell formation occurs in red marrow.

**Answer:** Red bone marrow. **தமிழில்:** சிவப்பு எலும்பு மஜ்ஜை இரத்த அணுக்களை உருவாக்குகிறது. **Trap:** The skeleton's functions occur through different components.

### 10.4 Axial skeleton and counting

**D1. Add the axial groups: 22 skull, 6 ossicles, 1 hyoid, 26 vertebral bones and 25 thoracic bones.**

1. Head-associated subtotal = 22 + 6 + 1 = 29.
2. Add 26 + 25 to obtain 80.

**Answer:** 80. **தமிழில்:** அச்சு எலும்புக்கூடு 80. **Trap:** Do not add the 29 again after already counting its three components.

**D2. Why can 33 and 26 both occur in backbone questions?**

1. The usual developmental count includes five sacral and four coccygeal segments separately.
2. Adult counting treats sacrum and coccyx as one bone each.

**Answer:** Different counting stages: 33 segments, 26 adult bones. **தமிழில்:** இணைந்த எலும்புகளைத் தனித்தனியாக எண்ணுகிறோமா என்பதைப் பார்க்கவும். **Trap:** Neither number refers to the spinal cord.

**D3. Which does not belong to the axial skeleton: sternum, rib or scapula?**

1. Sternum and ribs belong to the thoracic cage.
2. Scapula belongs to the appendicular shoulder girdle.

**Answer:** Scapula. **தமிழில்:** ஸ்கேபுலா தோள்பட்டை வளையத்தைச் சேர்ந்தது. **Trap:** If “spinal cord” is also an option, that is not a bone either; the question may be defective.

**D4. How many ribs lack anterior attachment to the sternum?**

1. Floating ribs are the 11th and 12th pairs.
2. Two pairs contain four individual ribs.

**Answer:** Four ribs / two pairs. **தமிழில்:** இரண்டு ஜோடி மிதக்கும் விலா எலும்புகள் = நான்கு. **Trap:** Read whether the question asks ribs or pairs.

**D5. A question calls the hyoid one of 22 skull bones. What is wrong?**

1. The strict 22 consists of eight cranial and fourteen facial bones.
2. Hyoid is a neck bone counted separately among associated axial structures.

**Answer:** Hyoid is not included in that 22. **தமிழில்:** ஹையாய்டு கழுத்தில் உள்ளது; 22 மண்டையோட்டு எலும்புகளுக்குள் சேராது. **Trap:** The associated total 29 uses a different scope.

### 10.5 Girdles and limbs

**E1. Five fused vertebrae form the pelvic girdle. Repair the identification.**

1. Five sacral vertebrae form the sacrum.
2. The pelvic girdle comprises the two hip bones.

**Answer:** Replace “pelvic girdle” with “sacrum.” **தமிழில்:** ஐந்து சாக்ரல் எலும்புகள் இணைந்து சாக்ரம் உருவாகிறது. **Trap:** Pelvis, pelvic girdle and sacrum are related but different.

**E2. Count one hand including wrist: eight carpals, five metacarpals, fourteen phalanges.**

1. Add 8 + 5 = 13.
2. Add 14 to obtain 27.

**Answer:** 27. **தமிழில்:** மணிக்கட்டு உட்பட ஒரு கையில் 27 எலும்புகள். **Trap:** Adding humerus, radius and ulna changes the scope to the entire upper limb.

**E3. Why does one lower limb also total 30 despite only seven tarsals?**

1. Femur + patella + tibia + fibula = 4.
2. Foot = 7 + 5 + 14 = 26; total 4 + 26 = 30.

**Answer:** The count includes the patella. **தமிழில்:** முழங்கால்சில்லையும் கணக்கில் சேரும். **Trap:** Do not omit patella when comparing upper and lower limbs.

**E4. Match glenoid cavity and acetabulum to the correct limb bones.**

1. Glenoid cavity is in the scapula at the shoulder.
2. Acetabulum is the hip socket.

**Answer:** Glenoid → humerus; acetabulum → femur. **தமிழில்:** தோள் குழி - ஹியூமரஸ்; இடுப்பு குழி - ஃபீமர். **Trap:** Both are sockets, but their locations differ.

**E5. In anatomical position, which forearm bone lies on the thumb side?**

1. Use palms facing forward, not an arbitrary hand rotation.
2. Radius lies laterally on the thumb side.

**Answer:** Radius. **தமிழில்:** கட்டைவிரல் பக்கம் ரேடியஸ். **Trap:** Ulna is on the little-finger side.

### 10.6 Joint identification

**F1. Is the thumb-base joint a hinge because the thumb bends?**

1. A single observed movement does not establish the joint's full capability.
2. The first carpometacarpal joint has saddle surfaces and biaxial movement.

**Answer:** Saddle. **தமிழில்:** கட்டைவிரல் அடிப்பகுதி சேண மூட்டு. **Trap:** Many joints allow flexion; not all are hinges.

**F2. One option says “wrist = condyloid,” another “between carpals = gliding.” Can both be right?**

1. Radiocarpal and intercarpal describe different connections.
2. Their surface arrangements differ.

**Answer:** Yes, with those precise locations. **தமிழில்:** ஒரே பகுதியில் வேறு மூட்டு வகைகள் இருக்கலாம். **Trap:** Do not erase anatomical qualifiers.

**F3. Turning the head sideways uses which named joint?**

1. Side-to-side rotation corresponds to the “no” action.
2. Atlas rotates around the axis at the atlantoaxial pivot connection.

**Answer:** Pivot, atlas-axis. **தமிழில்:** அட்லஸ்-ஆக்சிஸ் சுழல் மூட்டு. **Trap:** Nodding mainly involves occiput-atlas instead.

**F4. Which is a functional name: synovial or diarthrosis?**

1. Synovial describes structure: a fluid-containing cavity.
2. Diarthrosis describes freedom of movement.

**Answer:** Diarthrosis. **தமிழில்:** செயல்பாட்டு வகைப்பாட்டுப் பெயர் diarthrosis. **Trap:** A source may offer synovial as the intended answer even when it asks the wrong classification question.

**F5. Which fluid lubricates a synovial joint?**

1. Identify the joint cavity and its lining.
2. Synovial membrane contributes the lubricating synovial fluid.

**Answer:** Synovial fluid. **தமிழில்:** மூட்டுத் திரவம் உராய்வைக் குறைக்கிறது. **Trap:** Articular cartilage is a covering tissue, not the fluid.

### 10.7 Muscles and opposing actions

**G1. A muscle is branched, striped and involuntary. Identify it.**

1. Stripes alone allow skeletal or cardiac muscle.
2. Branching and involuntary action point to cardiac muscle.

**Answer:** Cardiac. **தமிழில்:** கிளைத்த, வரியுள்ள, தன்னிச்சையான தசை - இதயத் தசை. **Trap:** Do not equate all striped muscle with voluntary control.

**G2. Which pair correctly describes basic elbow flexion?**

1. Flexion means bending the elbow.
2. Biceps contracts; triceps relaxes in the elementary antagonistic model.

**Answer:** Biceps contracts, triceps relaxes. **தமிழில்:** மடக்கும்போது பைசெப்ஸ் சுருங்கும். **Trap:** Reverse the pair for the simplified extension action.

**G3. A person holds a weight stationary. Are the supporting muscles inactive?**

1. The weight still exerts a load.
2. Muscles develop tension to maintain the position.

**Answer:** No; isometric activity can hold it still. **தமிழில்:** அசைவில்லாமல் பிடிக்கும்போதும் தசை விசை உருவாகிறது. **Trap:** Visible motion is not the only evidence of muscle work.

**G4. Which iris muscles widen the pupil?**

1. Radial fibres pull outward when they contract.
2. Circular muscle contraction narrows the opening instead.

**Answer:** Radial muscles. **தமிழில்:** ஆரத் தசைகள் சுருங்கும்போது கண்பாவை விரிகிறது. **Trap:** The pupil itself is an opening, not a muscle.

**G5. Smooth muscle has no stripes. Does it therefore have no nucleus?**

1. Striations concern the arrangement of contractile structures.
2. Smooth muscle cells normally have one central nucleus.

**Answer:** No; one nucleus is present. **தமிழில்:** வரிகள் இல்லாவிட்டாலும் உட்கரு உள்ளது. **Trap:** Appearance and nucleus count are separate features.

### 10.8 Disorders and statement evaluation

**H1. Match osteoporosis and arthritis to bone fragility and joint disease.**

1. Osteoporosis reduces bone strength and raises fracture risk.
2. Arthritis refers to joint conditions involving inflammation or damage.

**Answer:** Osteoporosis → bone fragility; arthritis → joint disease. **தமிழில்:** எலும்பு வலிமைக் குறைவும் மூட்டு நோயும் வேறு. **Trap:** Nearby anatomical structures can have different disorders.

**H2. Which is not a skeletal disorder: rickets, osteoporosis or atherosclerosis?**

1. Rickets and osteoporosis affect bones.
2. Atherosclerosis concerns arterial plaque.

**Answer:** Atherosclerosis. **தமிழில்:** இது தமனிகளைப் பாதிக்கும் நிலை. **Trap:** Arthritis and atherosclerosis have different meanings despite similar-looking words.

**H3. Is oxygen shortage the only cause of every muscle cramp?**

1. A cramp is an involuntary muscle contraction/spasm.
2. Several mechanisms and contributing factors are possible.

**Answer:** No. **தமிழில்:** எல்லாத் தசைப்பிடிப்புகளுக்கும் ஒரே காரணம் இல்லை. **Trap:** A familiar classroom explanation should not be promoted into a universal medical rule.

**H4. Which is harder: the body's hardest bone or enamel? What does the question actually ask?**

1. If it asks hardest body substance, compare tissue/substance categories.
2. Tooth enamel is the conventional answer and is not itself a bone.

**Answer:** Tooth enamel for “hardest substance.” **தமிழில்:** மிகக் கடினமான உடற்பொருள் - பல் எனாமல். **Trap:** “Strongest bone” and “hardest substance” are different questions.

**H5. A key says only A is correct: A = skeletal fibres are cylindrical; B = they show light/dark bands. Accept it?**

1. Skeletal fibres are long, cylindrical and unbranched.
2. They also have striations, so B is true too.

**Answer:** No; both statements are correct. **தமிழில்:** இரு கூற்றுகளும் சரியானால் ஒற்றை விடை விசை தவறாக இருக்கலாம். **Trap:** Scientific checking comes before copying a marked option.
