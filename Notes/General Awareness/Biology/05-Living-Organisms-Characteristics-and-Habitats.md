## 2. 🧠 What is an organism? | உயிரினம் என்றால் என்ன?

An **organism** is an individual living entity. A bacterium is an organism; so are a mushroom, a mango tree, a fish and a human. An organism can be made of **one cell or many cells**.

### 2.1 Essential vocabulary

| English | Tamil | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Cell | செல் | Basic structural and functional unit of cellular life. |
| Unicellular | ஒருசெல் | One cell carries out the organism's life functions. |
| Multicellular | பலசெல் | Many cells form one organism, often with specialised roles. |
| Metabolism | வளர்சிதை மாற்றம் | The combined chemical reactions occurring in a living system. |
| Homeostasis | அகச்சூழல் சமநிலை பராமரிப்பு | Regulation that keeps internal conditions within suitable limits. |
| Stimulus / stimuli | தூண்டல் / தூண்டல்கள் | A detectable change that can produce a response. |
| Response | துலங்கல் / எதிர்வினை | The organism's reaction to a stimulus. |
| Habitat | வாழிடம் | The surroundings in which an organism normally lives. |
| Adaptation | தகவமைப்பு | A feature or behaviour that helps survival and reproduction in a particular environment. |
| Acclimatisation | சூழலுக்கேற்ப தற்காலிகச் சரிசெய்தல் | Adjustment within an individual's lifetime to changed conditions. |

**Bacteria** are unicellular. Many familiar protists, such as Amoeba, are unicellular. Humans and mango trees are multicellular. Fungi include unicellular forms such as yeast and multicellular forms such as mushrooms; do not label every fungus unicellular or every organism multicellular.

> **தமிழில்:** உயிரினம் என்பது தனித்த உயிருள்ள ஒன்று. அது ஒரே செல்லால் ஆனதாகவும் பல செல்களால் ஆனதாகவும் இருக்கலாம். கண்களுக்குத் தெரியாத பாக்டீரியாவும் ஓர் உயிரினமே.

### 2.2 From cell to organism

> **🔬 Process map: Levels of organisation in a complex multicellular organism**
>
> 1. **Cell:** a basic working unit, such as a muscle cell.
> 2. **Tissue:** related cells work together, as in muscle tissue.
> 3. **Organ:** several tissues form a structure with particular functions, such as the heart.
> 4. **Organ system:** organs cooperate, as in the circulatory system.
> 5. **Organism:** the integrated living individual.

**Plant example:** leaf cell → leaf tissue → leaf → shoot system → whole plant. A leaf is an **organ**, not an organ system.

> **⚠️ Exam trap:** This hierarchy describes organised multicellular bodies. A bacterium does not need tissues, organs or organ systems to count as an organism. **Cell → tissue → organ → organ system** is the order tested in PDF Q6.

### 2.3 The characteristics work together

| Characteristic | What it means | Familiar example |
|---|---|---|
| Cellular organisation | Living functions occur in an organised cellular system | A single bacterial cell; many specialised human cells |
| Metabolism | Chemical reactions build, maintain and supply the organism | Breaking down food molecules; making new cell material |
| Growth and development | Increase in size/cell material and changes towards maturity | Seedling becoming a mature plant |
| Response to stimuli | Detecting and reacting to changes | Mimosa leaves fold after touch. |
| Homeostasis | Regulating the internal environment | Responses that limit a rise in body temperature |
| Reproduction | Producing new individuals | Seed formation, cell division, birth |
| Adaptation and evolution | Heritable features can become established across generations | Water-conserving features of desert plants |

Nutrition, respiration, transport, excretion and movement are related life activities. The PDF presents both this broader characteristic list and the seven everyday life-process questions in Section 3; they are complementary ways of organising the chapter.

**Supplement: A single sign is insufficient.** A moving car is not alive. A crystal can increase in size by material accumulating on its surface. A dormant seed may show little visible activity but remain alive. Judge the living system as a whole, not movement or size increase alone.

**Recognition boundary:** Viruses are acellular and depend on host cells for replication; they do not have independent cellular metabolism. They are a boundary case, not an example of a normal unicellular organism. [OpenStax: properties and organisation of life](https://openstax.org/books/biology/pages/1-2-themes-and-concepts-of-biology).

### 2.4 Metabolism and ATP: understand the diagram

Food contains chemical energy. Cells transform that energy into forms they can use; **ATP = adenosine triphosphate**, a molecule involved in transferring energy for cellular work. Some energy is released as heat.

**Supplement:** **Catabolism** breaks larger molecules down; **anabolism** builds molecules. Both belong to metabolism. Metabolism is broader than digestion, and ATP is not another name for food or oxygen.

The PDF's metabolic diagram shows food input, energy use, heat and products such as carbon dioxide and water. Do not interpret the picture as showing that every metabolic reaction releases the same waste products.

### 2.5 Homeostasis: stable does not mean unchanging

Body conditions fluctuate. Homeostasis keeps them within a suitable working range by detecting changes and adjusting activity. Examples include regulation of body temperature, water balance and pH; pH describes how acidic or alkaline something is.

> **🔬 Process map: A simple negative-feedback example**
>
> 1. Body temperature rises above its usual regulated range.
> 2. Receptors detect the change.
> 3. A control centre coordinates a response.
> 4. Effectors act; for example, sweat glands produce sweat.
> 5. Evaporation removes heat, opposing the initial temperature rise.

This explains the PDF's **receptor → control centre → effector → correction** diagram. “Negative” means the response opposes the disturbance; it does not mean harmful. [OpenStax: homeostasis](https://openstax.org/books/biology/pages/33-3-homeostasis).

> **தமிழில்:** வெளிச்சூழல் மாறினாலும் உடலின் உள்ளே செயல்பாடுகளுக்குத் தேவையான வரம்பைத் தக்கவைத்தல் homeostasis. வெப்பம் உயரும்போது வியர்வை ஆவியாவது உடலின் வெப்பத்தை இழக்க உதவுகிறது. வெப்பநிலை ஒருபோதும் மாறாது என்பது இதன் பொருள் அல்ல.

**Stop check:** Explain why a cell is not a tissue, metabolism is not just digestion, and homeostasis is not the same as evolution.

## 3. 🌱 Life processes | உயிர்ச் செயல்பாடுகள்

### 3.1 Nutrition: do all organisms need food?

Living systems need **materials and energy** for maintenance, growth and repair. They do not all obtain them by eating another organism.

| Mode | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Autotrophic | Makes organic food from inorganic starting materials using an energy source | Green plants use light in photosynthesis. |
| Heterotrophic | Obtains organic nutrients from other organisms or organic matter | Animals, fungi and many bacteria |

For green plants: **carbon dioxide + water → sugars + oxygen**, using light energy and chlorophyll. This is a summary, not a fully balanced chemical equation. Sunlight is the energy source; it is not itself a food substance.

Animals depend on other organisms for food, directly or indirectly. Fungi generally absorb nutrients after external digestion. Not every plant photosynthesises, so “all plants always make all their own food” is too broad; plant-nutrition exceptions belong in the next chapter.

> **தமிழில்:** எல்லா உயிரினங்களுக்கும் பொருட்களும் ஆற்றலும் தேவை. ஆனால் எல்லாமே விலங்குகளைப் போல உணவை உண்ணுவதில்லை. பசுந்தாவரங்கள் ஒளிச்சேர்க்கையால் உணவு தயாரிக்கின்றன; விலங்குகளும் பூஞ்சைகளும் பிற உயிர்மப் பொருட்களைச் சார்ந்துள்ளன.

### 3.2 Growth and development

**Growth** is an increase in the organism's size, mass or cellular material. In multicellular organisms, cell division and enlargement contribute. A single cell can enlarge before dividing.

**Development** includes changes in form and function during the life cycle. A seedling becoming a flowering plant shows development as well as growth. The PDF uses a child becoming an adult, a pup becoming a dog and a chick becoming a hen or cock.

Many plants retain growing regions called **meristems**, allowing continued growth. Human height growth largely stops after maturity, while cell renewal and repair continue. Do not claim that every animal stops all growth at the same age or that every part of a plant grows forever.

> **⚠️ Exam trap:** A living adult is not “non-living” because height has stopped increasing. Reproduction and growth need to be interpreted over a life cycle, not demanded visibly from every individual at every moment.

### 3.3 Breathing, gas exchange and cellular respiration

| Process | What happens | Where to recognise it |
|---|---|---|
| Breathing / ventilation | Air is moved in and out | Inhalation and exhalation in humans |
| Gas exchange | Oxygen and carbon dioxide cross an exchange surface | Lungs, gills or moist skin |
| Cellular respiration | Cells release usable energy from food molecules | Living cells, including plant cells |

For **aerobic respiration**: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + released energy, captured partly in ATP. This is a word equation; it is not a claim that every energy-releasing pathway requires oxygen.

**Supplement:** Anaerobic pathways release energy without oxygen. Thus “all living organisms must breathe atmospheric oxygen” is wrong. Respiration in introductory chapters broadly concerns energy release; it should not be reduced to lung breathing. [NCERT Life Processes via IIT Kanpur SATHEE](https://sathee.iitk.ac.in/ncert-books/class-10/nb-science-10/sci-10-chapter-5-life-processes/).

| Organism | Main gas-exchange structure in the source | Important boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Human, cow, buffalo, dog, cat | Lungs | Nose is an air passage; lungs provide the main exchange surface. |
| Typical fish | Gills | Extract dissolved oxygen; do not split water molecules into oxygen. |
| Earthworm | Moist skin | No lungs are required for this gas-exchange method. |
| Plant | Stomata in leaves | Stems and roots also exchange gases; stomata are not lungs. |

**Plant respiration continues in day and night.** Photosynthesis requires suitable light. In sufficient light, photosynthesis can exceed respiration, so the plant shows net carbon-dioxide uptake and oxygen release. That does not mean respiration has stopped.

> **தமிழில்:** மூச்சை உள்ளிழுத்து வெளியேற்றுவது breathing. செல்களில் உணவிலிருந்து பயன்படும் ஆற்றலைப் பெறுவது respiration. தாவரங்கள் பகலிலும் இரவிலும் சுவாசிக்கின்றன; ஒளிச்சேர்க்கை நடைபெறும்போதும் சுவாசம் நிற்காது.

### 3.4 Stimulus and response

**Stimulus = the change detected. Response = what happens because of it.** A stimulus can be external or internal.

| PDF example | Stimulus | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Foot meets a thorn | Painful contact | Withdrawal of the foot |
| Seeing/thinking about food | Food-related cue | Salivation or feeding activity |
| Moving into bright light | Increased light | Blinking and pupil adjustment |
| A bird notices approach | Nearby movement | May fly away |
| Cockroach exposed at night | Light/change in surroundings | Often moves towards shelter |
| Touch-me-not, **Mimosa pudica** | Touch or mechanical disturbance | Leaflets fold |
| Some flowers | Light/dark cycle and related timing cues | Opening or closing, depending on species |

The PDF gives examples of some flowers closing after sunset and others blooming at night. **Not all flowers follow the same pattern.** Similarly, animals do not all respond identically to light.

**Supplement:** A shoot growing towards light is **phototropism**. Rapid Mimosa folding is a touch-triggered **nastic movement**, involving changes in **turgor**, the pressure of cell contents against the cell wall. It is not growth towards the touching object. Learn the contrast, not a long hormone chapter here.

> **தமிழில்:** முள் குத்துதல் தூண்டல்; காலை விலக்குவது துலங்கல். தொட்டாற்சிணுங்கியைத் தொடுதல் தூண்டல்; இலைகள் மடங்குவது துலங்கல். தாவரங்களுக்கு நரம்பு மண்டலம் இல்லாவிட்டாலும் அவை மாற்றங்களுக்கு எதிர்வினை காட்டுகின்றன.

### 3.5 Excretion, egestion and secretion

The PDF begins its waste discussion with unused food. For precise exam answers, separate these three processes.

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Excretion - கழிவுநீக்கம்** | Removal of metabolic waste products and excess substances | Carbon dioxide removed by lungs; urea removed mainly in urine |
| **Egestion - செரிக்காத உணவு வெளியேற்றம்** | Removal of undigested/unabsorbed food residue from the digestive tract | Passing undigested residue in faeces |
| **Secretion - சுரத்தல்** | Release of a substance by cells/glands, often for a useful function | Saliva or digestive enzymes |

Egestion is the term for removing undigested food, as distinguished in [NPTEL's digestion lesson](https://archive.nptel.ac.in/content/storage2/courses/122103039/module3/lec12/10.html). A secretion is not automatically waste. Sweat has a major cooling role as well as carrying some water, salts and small amounts of waste.

Plants also manage wastes. They can release gaseous products, store substances in vacuoles or tissues, and remove some stored substances through shedding parts. The PDF mentions storage in plant parts and release through secretions. Some stored/secreted compounds also have protective functions; do not classify every resin, gum or secretion as useless waste.

> **தமிழில்:** வளர்சிதை மாற்றத்தால் உருவான கழிவை நீக்குவது excretion. செரிக்காத உணவுப் பகுதியை வெளியேற்றுவது egestion. உமிழ்நீர் போன்ற பொருளைச் சுரப்பது secretion. மூன்றும் ஒரே பொருள் அல்ல.

### 3.6 Reproduction: continuation of a kind

Reproduction produces new individuals. It maintains a lineage across generations, but **every individual need not reproduce to be alive**. The PDF makes this qualification explicitly on p. 69.

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Sexual reproduction | Involves fusion of gametes in the standard process | Sperm and egg form a zygote. |
| Asexual reproduction | No fusion of gametes | Bacterial binary fission; vegetative propagation |
| Oviparous | Lays eggs that develop/hatch outside the mother's body | Hen, pigeon |
| Viviparous | Gives birth to live young | Human, cow, dog, cat |

Oviparous/viviparous describes how offspring develop and are delivered; it is **not** the same classification as sexual/asexual.

> **🔬 Process map: Human development shown in the source**
>
> 1. Sperm and egg fuse during fertilisation.
> 2. The resulting single cell is the **zygote**.
> 3. Cell division and differentiation produce an **embryo**.
> 4. Development continues through the **foetal stage**.
> 5. A baby is born.

The source Q7 abbreviates this to **zygote → embryo → baby**, which has the right relative order. The handwritten “human - eggs” on p. 10 must not be learned as “humans lay eggs”: human reproduction uses an egg cell, but humans are viviparous.

### 3.7 Plant reproduction and germination

Many plants produce seeds containing an embryo. **Germination** is the resumption of embryo growth into a seedling. The source's seed diagram shows the emerging root and shoot.

**Supplement:** Water, oxygen and a suitable temperature are typical requirements; light requirements vary between species. An ordinary dry viable seed is dormant or inactive, not automatically dead. Soil itself is not essential for the initial germination of every seed.

A potato piece with an **eye/bud** can develop into a new plant. This is **vegetative propagation** using a stem tuber. The potato is a modified stem, not a root, and an arbitrary piece without a viable bud is not equivalent to the source example.

> **தமிழில்:** விதை முளைத்தால் அதிலுள்ள கரு வளரத் தொடங்குகிறது. உருளைக்கிழங்கின் மொட்டு உள்ள பகுதியிலிருந்து புதிய செடி உருவாவது உடல இனப்பெருக்கம். விதை இல்லாமலும் சில தாவரங்கள் இனப்பெருக்கம் செய்கின்றன.

### 3.8 Movement and death

Most familiar animals change place; plants are usually rooted. Yet plant parts can move, and water, minerals and sugars move within plants. **Movement is broader than locomotion**, as explained in the [Body Movements note](04-Body-Movements.md).

An organism may eventually die without ever reproducing. Reproduction matters for continuation across generations; it is not a requirement that an individual must satisfy before being recognised as living. Dead material and naturally non-living material are also different: a dead leaf was once part of a living organism; a mineral grain was not.

**Stop check:** Explain breathing versus respiration, excretion versus egestion, and germination versus seed formation. Give one living thing that does not visibly move from place to place.

## 4. 🏡 Habitat and adaptation | வாழிடமும் தகவமைப்பும்

### 4.1 Habitat: more than an address

A habitat is where an organism normally lives and obtains the conditions and resources it needs. The source lists food, water, air and shelter. Interpret these biologically: a fish uses dissolved oxygen, and an anaerobic organism does not need atmospheric oxygen in the same way as a human.

Many kinds of organisms can share one habitat. A pond may support fish, frogs, plants, insects and microorganisms. Sharing a habitat does not make their needs, food or adaptations identical.

> **🧠 Concept map: Broad habitat groups**
>
> - **Terrestrial - நில வாழிடம்:** forests, grasslands, deserts, mountain regions and landward coastal areas.
> - **Aquatic - நீர் வாழிடம்:** ponds, lakes, rivers and oceans.
> - **Freshwater - நன்னீர்:** typical ponds, lakes and rivers.
> - **Marine - கடல்நீர்:** seas and oceans.
> - **Transition areas:** coasts and wetlands can include land-water boundaries rather than fitting a single simple box.

### 4.2 Biotic and abiotic components

| Component | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Biotic - உயிருள்ள கூறுகள் | Living organisms and their interactions | Plants, animals, fungi, bacteria |
| Abiotic - உயிரற்ற சுற்றுச்சூழல் கூறுகள் | Physical and chemical environmental factors | Light, temperature, water, air, minerals, salinity, soil conditions |

**Soil is not just lifeless dust.** Its mineral, water and physical conditions are abiotic; bacteria, fungi, roots and animals living in it are biotic. The source's short “soil = abiotic” label refers to the environmental medium, not everything present in a soil sample.

**Supplement:** **Habitat** describes where an organism lives. **Niche** describes its ecological role and resource use. An **ecosystem** includes organisms interacting with one another and the non-living environment. These distinctions support G11 ecosystem basics; a full ecology classification is not needed here.

### 4.3 Adaptation versus acclimatisation

| Term | Main scale | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Adaptation in the evolutionary sense | Inherited features become established in populations across generations | Cactus spines and water-storing stems |
| Acclimatisation | An individual adjusts during its lifetime, often reversibly | Physiological adjustment after moving to a higher altitude |
| Immediate response | A reaction to a stimulus | Pulling a foot away from a thorn |
| Homeostasis | Ongoing regulation of internal conditions | Temperature regulation |

The PDF's p. 3 arrow can suggest adaptation simply comes from acclimatisation. That is not a general rule. An individual's adjustment is not the same process as a population's evolutionary change. [OpenStax: homeostasis and acclimatisation](https://openstax.org/books/biology/pages/33-3-homeostasis).

> **🧠 Concept map: Kinds of useful adaptations**
>
> - **Structural - உடலமைப்பு:** cactus spines, camel feet, fish body shape.
> - **Physiological - உடற்செயலியல்:** water conservation and internal regulation.
> - **Behavioural - நடத்தை:** sheltering in a burrow or being active at cooler times.

An adaptation helps under particular conditions; it is not a guarantee of survival everywhere. Organisms do not deliberately decide to grow a required inherited feature. “Useful because…” explains a benefit, not conscious design.

> **தமிழில்:** வாழிடம் என்பது வாழும் சூழல். தகவமைப்பு என்பது அந்தச் சூழலில் வாழ உதவும் பண்பு. ஒரே உயிரினம் புதிய சூழலுக்கு தன் வாழ்நாளில் சரிசெய்துகொள்வது acclimatisation; பல தலைமுறைகளில் நிலைபெறும் மரபுவழிப் பண்புடன் அதை குழப்ப வேண்டாம்.

**Stop check:** In a pond, classify fish, algae, sunlight and water temperature. Explain why two species in that pond need not have the same adaptation.

## 5. 🌵 Desert adaptations | பாலைவனத் தகவமைப்புகள்

### 5.1 Begin with the challenge

Low water availability is central to a desert habitat. The PDF focuses on **hot deserts**, where exposed daytime conditions can be severe and nights are often cooler. Not every desert is hot; cold deserts also exist.

### 5.2 Camel: feature → benefit

| Feature in the text/diagram | Benefit | Avoid this confusion |
|---|---|---|
| Fat stored in the hump | Energy reserve when food is scarce | The hump is not a tank of drinking water. |
| Long legs | Raise the body away from hot ground | Not every desert animal has long legs. |
| Broad, padded feet | Spread weight and reduce sinking into loose sand | They are not webbed swimming feet. |
| Long/double eyelashes and thick eyebrows | Help shield the eyes from windblown sand | No need to memorise an invented exact lash count. |
| Hairy ears and closable nostrils | Reduce entry of dust/sand | Nostrils still have a breathing function. |
| Coat/fur | Helps insulate against environmental temperature extremes | Fur is not useful only in snowy habitats. |
| Sandy colour, as labelled in the source | Can blend with surroundings | Colour alone does not explain desert survival. |

The hump's **fat-storage** distinction is supported by the [Library of Congress camel explanation](https://www.loc.gov/everyday-mysteries/categories/zoology/item/how-much-water-does-a-camels-hump-hold/).

### 5.3 Smaller animals: change behaviour

Some desert rodents and snakes avoid intense daytime heat by sheltering in **burrows** and being active at cooler times, often at night. This is a behavioural strategy, not a claim that every desert snake or rodent is strictly nocturnal.

> **🔬 Process map: Reason out a burrowing adaptation**
>
> 1. Identify the challenge: strong daytime heat at the exposed surface.
> 2. Identify the behaviour: shelter underground during the hottest period.
> 3. Connect it to the benefit: reduced exposure to surface heat.
> 4. Check the boundary: this applies to the described animals, not every desert species.

### 5.4 Cactus: conserve and collect water

**Transpiration** is loss of water vapour from a plant, mainly through stomata. The aim is to reduce unnecessary water loss, not to stop every exchange with the environment.

| Feature | How it helps |
|---|---|
| Leaves reduced to spines in typical desert cacti | Reduces leaf surface available for water loss; can also deter herbivores. |
| Green stem | Carries out photosynthesis when leaves are highly reduced. |
| Thick, fleshy stem | Stores water. |
| Waxy outer layer | Limits water loss through the surface. |
| Extensive roots | Collect available water; arrangement depends on species and conditions. |

> **⚠️ Exam trap: Two root strategies**
>
> The PDF text on pp. 31-32 says desert plants have very deep roots, while its p. 33 cactus diagram labels **shallow, widespread roots**. Both strategies exist: deep roots can reach deeper water; shallow spreading roots rapidly collect brief rain near the surface. Do not memorise “all cacti have deep roots.” [National Park Service: cactus root adaptations](https://www.nps.gov/sagu/learn/nature/cacti-and-desert-succulents.htm).

**Supporting vocabulary:** **Xerophyte** means a plant adapted to dry conditions. Not every xerophyte is a cactus, and not every cactus lives in an exposed hot desert.

> **தமிழில்:** ஒட்டகத்தின் திமிலில் கொழுப்பு சேமிக்கப்படுகிறது. கள்ளியின் பசுந்தண்டு ஒளிச்சேர்க்கை செய்கிறது; சதைப்பற்றுள்ள தண்டு நீரைச் சேமிக்கிறது. சில பாலைவனத் தாவரங்களுக்கு ஆழமான வேர்களும் சிலவற்றிற்கு மேல்மட்டத்தில் பரந்த வேர்களும் உள்ளன.

**Stop check:** Match hump, padded feet, spines, green stem and waxy layer to five different functions. Do not answer “helps survival” without stating how.

## 6. 🏔️ Mountains and grasslands | மலைகளும் புல்வெளிகளும்

### 6.1 Mountain plants

The PDF's examples concern cold, windy mountain regions with possible winter snow. Conditions vary with height, latitude and local climate; not every mountain slope is permanently snowy.

Conifer examples pictured in the source are **pine, spruce, cedar and fir**. Many have a conical crown, sloping branches and narrow needle-like leaves. Shape and branch arrangement help rain and snow slide off; needle-like leaves and their surface features can also limit water loss.

**Conical crown** describes the tree's overall outline. It is not the same thing as a **reproductive cone**. Mountains also have trees and smaller plants with other forms, as the PDF itself notes.

### 6.2 Mountain animals

| Animal | Feature highlighted | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Yak | Long, dense hair | Insulation in cold conditions |
| Snow leopard | Thick fur, including around feet and toes | Reduces heat loss; protects paws from cold surfaces |
| Mountain goat | Strong, gripping hooves | Movement over steep rocky ground |

As elevation changes, surroundings and the organisms found there change. This does not mean an individual animal instantly acquires a new inherited adaptation merely by walking uphill.

> **தமிழில்:** யாக்கின் நீண்ட உரோமம் குளிரிலிருந்து காக்கிறது. பனிச்சிறுத்தையின் பாதங்களைச் சுற்றிய உரோமமும் வெப்ப இழப்பைக் குறைக்க உதவுகிறது. மலை ஆட்டின் குளம்புகள் பாறைச் சரிவுகளில் பிடிப்பைத் தருகின்றன.

### 6.3 Grassland predator and prey

A **predator** hunts other animals; **prey** is the animal being hunted. The source compares a lion with a deer. Their adaptations solve different problems in shared surroundings.

| Feature | Lion | Deer |
|---|---|---|
| Colour | Light brown/tawny colour can conceal it in dry grass | Camouflage can also help prey; it is not exclusive to predators. |
| Eyes | Forward-facing eyes support overlapping vision and judging prey distance | Side-facing eyes provide a broad view for detecting danger. |
| Feeding structures | Claws and teeth assist capturing/feeding on prey | Cheek teeth help grind plant material. |
| Other source features | Retractile claws, particularly emphasised on forelimbs | Long ears detect sounds; speed helps escape. |

The lion can occupy grassland and some woodland/forest habitats. Do not convert one habitat example into a claim that lions live only in grassland.

> **⚠️ Exam trap:** The source's “deer sees in all directions” means a **wide field of view**, not perfect vision without blind areas. Eye position is a useful comparison here, not an exception-free rule for identifying every predator and herbivore.

> **தமிழில்:** சிங்கத்தின் முன்னோக்கிய கண்கள் இரையின் தூரத்தைக் கணிக்க உதவுகின்றன. மானின் பக்கவாட்டுக் கண்கள் சுற்றியுள்ள ஆபத்தைப் பரந்த பகுதியில் கண்டறிய உதவுகின்றன. மானின் வேகம் இரையைப் பிடிக்க அல்ல; வேட்டையாடுபவரிடமிருந்து தப்பிக்க உதவும்.

**Stop check:** Explain why fur, hooves, eye position and body colour cannot all be assigned the same function.

## 7. 🌊 Aquatic habitats | நீர்வாழ் வாழிடங்கள்

### 7.1 Streamlining and movement

A **streamlined body** offers relatively low resistance when moving through a fluid. Many actively swimming fish and marine mammals have such a shape. Streamlining helps movement; it does not itself extract oxygen from water.

The PDF describes squid and octopus as exceptions that can alter their shape while moving. This needs qualification: **squid commonly have an elongated, streamlined body already**. Not all squid or octopuses live on the seabed. Octopuses often crawl with their arms and can also swim by jet propulsion. [Australian Museum: squid](https://australian.museum/learn/animals/molluscs/squid/); [Smithsonian: cephalopod movement](https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/invertebrates/octopuses-squids-and-relatives).

> **🔬 Process map: Basic jet propulsion**
>
> 1. Water enters the mantle cavity of the squid or octopus.
> 2. Muscular contraction forces water out through a funnel/siphon.
> 3. The animal moves in the opposite direction to the expelled water.
> 4. Body posture and, in squid, fins also help control movement.

This is supporting explanation for the source's shape discussion, not a separate fluid-mechanics chapter.

### 7.2 Gills versus lungs

| Animal group | Gas-exchange organ | Oxygen source |
|---|---|---|
| Typical fish | Gills | Oxygen dissolved in water |
| Squid and octopus | Gills | Oxygen dissolved in water |
| Whales and dolphins | Lungs | Atmospheric air taken in at the surface |

Whales and dolphins are **mammals**, not fish. A **blowhole** is a breathing opening on the upper head connected to the respiratory passages; lungs perform gas exchange. They hold their breath underwater and must return to the surface, with the opening exposed to air, to breathe. Dive duration varies; the source supplies no universal safe duration to memorise. [NOAA: dolphins breathe using lungs](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/dolphin.html).

> **தமிழில்:** மீன்களின் செவுள்கள் நீரில் கரைந்துள்ள ஆக்சிஜனைப் பெறுகின்றன. திமிங்கலமும் டால்பினும் நுரையீரலால் காற்றைச் சுவாசிக்கும் பாலூட்டிகள். தலையின் மேலுள்ள சுவாசத்துளை நுரையீரலுக்குச் செல்லும் வழி; அது செவுள் அல்ல.

### 7.3 Pond and lake plants: separate the growth forms

**Hydrophyte** means a plant adapted to aquatic or very wet conditions. Aquatic plants do not all have the same leaf position or root arrangement.

| Growth form | Arrangement | Example / recognition cue |
|---|---|---|
| Rooted floating-leaved | Rooted in sediment; leaves float at the surface | Water lily |
| Free-floating | Floats without attachment to the bottom | Duckweed; water hyacinth |
| Submerged | Most vegetative parts grow below the surface | Hydrilla, Vallisneria |
| Emergent | Rooted in shallow water; shoots extend into air | Cattail/reed-type plants |

These distinctions prevent the source's opening “plants have their roots fixed in soil below water” from being applied to **all** aquatic plants. [Minnesota DNR: where aquatic plants grow](https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/shorelandmgmt/apg/wheregrow.html).

### 7.4 Match the plant feature to the water problem

| Feature in the PDF | Benefit | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Long, light, flexible stalks/stems | Connect rooted plants to surface leaves and tolerate movement of water | Not every aquatic stem is a hollow tube. |
| Air spaces | Assist buoyancy and internal gas movement | A useful explanation, not a claim that every organ floats. |
| Narrow, ribbon-like submerged leaves | Bend in flowing water | Not the typical form of all floating leaves. |
| Finely divided submerged leaves | Let water pass with less mechanical stress | They are not easily destroyed simply because they are divided. |
| Roots anchoring rooted plants | Maintain position in sediment | Roots can also absorb nutrients; some aquatic plants lack roots. |

The PDF says aquatic roots mainly hold the plant in position. This is an introductory comparison, **not a universal loss of absorptive function**. Different aquatic plants obtain nutrients through different combinations of roots and exposed surfaces. [Plant Physiology review: aquatic root forms and functions](https://academic.oup.com/plphys/article/197/1/kiae586/7917389).

Submerged plants are classified by their principal growth form. Flowers or reproductive structures of some can reach the surface, so “every part always remains underwater” is too absolute outside the simple example in Q52.

> **தமிழில்:** நீர்த்தாவரங்கள் அனைத்தும் அடித்தள மண்ணில் வேரூன்றி இருப்பதில்லை. சில தனியாக மிதக்கின்றன; சில நீரில் மூழ்கியுள்ளன. நாடா போன்ற அல்லது நுண்ணிய பிரிவுள்ள இலைகள் நீரோட்டத்தைத் தாங்க உதவுகின்றன.

### 7.5 Frog: movement in water and on land

The source's pond frog is an **amphibian - இருவாழ்வி**. Strong hind legs assist jumping, while webbed feet help push against water during swimming. Many adult frogs use both land and water; their exact habitat and life cycle vary by species.

**Supplement:** A typical tadpole uses gills, whereas the adult frog uses lungs and moist skin for gas exchange. Do not infer that an adult frog must always breathe with fish-like gills because it enters water.

> **⚠️ Exam trap:** “Amphibian” is an animal classification, not a label for every animal that enters land and water. A crocodile is a reptile; a whale is a mammal.

**Stop check:** Explain why a fish and a dolphin can share an aquatic habitat but have different breathing structures. Then explain why a water lily and Hydrilla need not have the same leaf shape.

## 9. 📝 Worked models | வழிகாட்டும் எடுத்துக்காட்டுகள்

These **35 generated practice questions** cover seven teaching groups, with five models in each. Try each question before reading the two reasoning steps. These are guided learning, not evidence of your unaided score.

### 9.1 Organisms, organisation and internal regulation

**A1. A bacterium has no heart. Can it still be an organism?**

1. An organism can be unicellular.
2. Its one cell performs its life functions without a heart or organ systems.

**Answer:** Yes. **தமிழில்:** ஒரே செல் உயிர்ச் செயல்களைச் செய்யலாம். **Trap:** Human body organisation is not compulsory for all life.

**A2. Put these in order: organ, cell, organ system, tissue.**

1. Cells contribute to tissues; tissues form organs.
2. Organs cooperate in organ systems.

**Answer:** Cell → tissue → organ → organ system. **தமிழில்:** செல் → திசு → உறுப்பு → உறுப்பு மண்டலம். **Trap:** A leaf is an organ, not a tissue alone.

**A3. A body maintains its temperature within a suitable range. Name the process.**

1. The clue is regulation of an internal condition.
2. Homeostasis describes that regulation despite change.

**Answer:** Homeostasis. **தமிழில்:** அகச்சூழல் சமநிலை பராமரிப்பு. **Trap:** Metabolism is the wider set of chemical reactions, not the specific regulation term.

**A4. Breaking food molecules down and building new molecules belong to which umbrella process?**

1. Breakdown is catabolism; building is anabolism.
2. Both are included in metabolism.

**Answer:** Metabolism. **தமிழில்:** சிதைத்தலும் உருவாக்குதலும் வளர்சிதை மாற்றத்தில் அடங்கும். **Trap:** Do not reduce metabolism to digestion alone.

**A5. A crystal increases in size. Is that sufficient proof it is alive?**

1. Material can accumulate on a non-living object's surface.
2. Size increase does not establish cellular organisation or integrated life processes.

**Answer:** No. **தமிழில்:** அளவு அதிகரிப்பது மட்டும் உயிருள்ளதற்குச் சான்றல்ல. **Trap:** Apply several characteristics together.

### 9.2 Nutrition, growth and respiration

**B1. A green plant does not eat rice. Does that mean it has no nutritional requirement?**

1. Nutrition does not have to involve swallowing food.
2. The plant makes organic food using photosynthesis and takes up required materials.

**Answer:** No; it has nutritional needs. **தமிழில்:** உணவை உண்ணாவிட்டாலும் ஊட்டத் தேவைகள் உண்டு. **Trap:** Autotrophic does not mean independent of water, minerals or energy.

**B2. A mature adult stops growing taller. Is all biological activity over?**

1. Height growth is only one type of change.
2. Metabolism, cell renewal, repair and regulation continue.

**Answer:** No. **தமிழில்:** உயர வளர்ச்சி நின்றாலும் பராமரிப்புச் செயல்கள் தொடரும். **Trap:** Do not demand visible growth at every moment.

**B3. A plant is photosynthesising at noon. Has its cellular respiration stopped?**

1. Photosynthesis and respiration are different processes.
2. Plant cells still need usable energy in daylight.

**Answer:** No; both can occur. **தமிழில்:** பகலிலும் தாவரச் சுவாசம் நடக்கும். **Trap:** Net oxygen release does not prove zero oxygen use.

**B4. An earthworm has no lungs. How can it exchange respiratory gases?**

1. Lungs are one kind of exchange surface, not the only one.
2. Earthworms exchange gases through moist skin.

**Answer:** Through moist skin. **தமிழில்:** ஈரமான தோல் வழியாக வாயுப் பரிமாற்றம். **Trap:** No lungs does not mean no respiration.

**B5. Is moving air into the lungs the same event as releasing energy inside cells?**

1. Moving air is ventilation/breathing.
2. Cellular respiration releases usable energy from food molecules.

**Answer:** No; they are related but distinct. **தமிழில்:** மூச்சியக்கம் வேறு; செல்லின் ஆற்றல் பெறும் செயல் வேறு. **Trap:** Do not use the words as exact synonyms.

### 9.3 Stimuli, wastes and reproduction

**C1. A touch-me-not leaflet folds after touch. Identify stimulus and response.**

1. Touch is the detected change.
2. Folding is the resulting action.

**Answer:** Touch → stimulus; folding → response. **தமிழில்:** தொடுதல் தூண்டல்; மடிதல் துலங்கல். **Trap:** Do not reverse cause and reaction.

**C2. Match urea removal and undigested-food removal to excretion and egestion.**

1. Urea is a metabolic waste product.
2. Undigested residue is material left in the digestive tract.

**Answer:** Urea → excretion; residue → egestion. **தமிழில்:** வளர்சிதை கழிவு நீக்கம்; செரிக்காத உணவு வெளியேற்றம். **Trap:** Not every substance leaving the body has the same process label.

**C3. A gland releases saliva. Must this be waste excretion?**

1. Saliva has useful functions, including helping food processing.
2. Release by the gland is secretion.

**Answer:** No; secretion. **தமிழில்:** உமிழ்நீர் சுரத்தல் என்பது கழிவுநீக்கம் மட்டுமல்ல. **Trap:** A released product is not automatically useless.

**C4. Humans form egg cells. Does that make humans oviparous?**

1. Egg-cell formation is part of sexual reproduction.
2. Oviparous means laying eggs that develop/hatch outside the mother's body.

**Answer:** No; humans are viviparous. **தமிழில்:** அண்டசெல் இருப்பது முட்டையிடும் விலங்கு என்பதல்ல. **Trap:** Gamete type and mode of birth are different classifications.

**C5. A potato piece grows into a new plant. Which feature matters, and what kind of reproduction is this?**

1. The source piece contains a viable eye/bud.
2. Growth from the stem tuber is vegetative propagation without gamete fusion.

**Answer:** Bud; vegetative/asexual reproduction. **தமிழில்:** மொட்டுள்ள தண்டுக்கிழங்குப் பகுதி புதிய செடியைத் தருகிறது. **Trap:** Potato is not a root, and not every cut piece has a viable bud.

### 9.4 Habitat and adaptation reasoning

**D1. A pond contains fish, algae and bacteria. Can it be one habitat?**

1. A habitat can support several kinds of organism.
2. They may use different resources within it.

**Answer:** Yes. **தமிழில்:** ஒரே வாழிடத்தில் பல உயிரினங்கள் வாழலாம். **Trap:** One habitat does not mean one species.

**D2. Classify a soil bacterium and soil moisture.**

1. A bacterium is a living organism.
2. Moisture is a physical environmental condition.

**Answer:** Bacterium → biotic; moisture → abiotic. **தமிழில்:** பாக்டீரியா உயிருள்ள கூறு; ஈரப்பதம் உயிரற்ற சூழல் காரணி. **Trap:** Do not put everything in a soil sample under abiotic.

**D3. A person adjusts physiologically after moving to high altitude. Which term best fits?**

1. The change occurs within that individual's lifetime.
2. It does not by itself demonstrate inherited population change across generations.

**Answer:** Acclimatisation. **தமிழில்:** தனி உயிரினத்தின் சூழல்சார் சரிசெய்தல். **Trap:** It is not automatically evolutionary adaptation.

**D4. A cactus has spines; a rodent shelters in a burrow. Classify the two features.**

1. Spines are a bodily structure.
2. Sheltering is an action pattern.

**Answer:** Structural; behavioural. **தமிழில்:** உடலமைப்புத் தகவமைப்பு; நடத்தைத் தகவமைப்பு. **Trap:** Behaviour can be adaptive even without a visible new organ.

**D5. Two animals share a grassland. Must they have identical eyes, teeth and diets?**

1. Shared surroundings do not imply the same feeding role.
2. A predator and its prey face different tasks.

**Answer:** No. **தமிழில்:** ஒரே வாழிடம் இருந்தாலும் உணவும் பண்புகளும் வேறுபடலாம். **Trap:** Match a trait to the organism's activity, not just its habitat label.

### 9.5 Desert applications

**E1. What does a camel's hump principally store: drinking water or fat?**

1. The hump contains a reserve of fatty tissue.
2. That reserve can supply energy when food is scarce.

**Answer:** Fat. **தமிழில்:** திமிலில் கொழுப்பு சேமிக்கப்படுகிறது. **Trap:** Desert survival does not imply a water tank in the hump.

**E2. Why do broad camel feet help on sand?**

1. Body weight is distributed over a larger contact area.
2. This helps reduce sinking into loose sand.

**Answer:** Weight distribution and support. **தமிழில்:** அகன்ற பாதம் எடையைப் பரப்பி மணலில் புதைவதைக் குறைக்கும். **Trap:** Do not give the unrelated hump-storage explanation.

**E3. A cactus has reduced leaves but a green stem. Where does much photosynthesis occur?**

1. Its leaf area is greatly reduced.
2. The chlorophyll-containing stem performs photosynthesis.

**Answer:** Green stem. **தமிழில்:** பசுந்தண்டு ஒளிச்சேர்க்கை செய்கிறது. **Trap:** A broad-looking cactus pad can be a stem, not a leaf.

**E4. A cactus has shallow roots spreading widely. Does this prove it is poorly adapted to desert rainfall?**

1. Brief rain can wet the surface without reaching deep layers.
2. Spreading roots rapidly collect that near-surface water.

**Answer:** No; this can be an effective adaptation. **தமிழில்:** மேல்மட்ட மழைநீரை விரைவாகப் பெற பரந்த வேர்கள் உதவும். **Trap:** Deep roots are not the only successful strategy.

**E5. A desert rodent shelters during daytime and feeds at night. Which problem does this address?**

1. Exposed daytime conditions are hotter in the example.
2. Shelter and cooler-time activity reduce heat exposure.

**Answer:** Avoiding intense daytime heat. **தமிழில்:** பகல் வெப்பத்திலிருந்து தப்பும் நடத்தை. **Trap:** This does not mean all desert animals follow the same schedule.

### 9.6 Mountains and grasslands

**F1. Why can sloping branches help a tree in a snowy mountain habitat?**

1. Snow accumulating on branches adds load.
2. Sloping form helps snow slide off.

**Answer:** Reduced accumulation of snow. **தமிழில்:** பனி வழிந்து விழ உதவுகிறது. **Trap:** This is not a reason to claim every mountain tree is conical.

**F2. Match yak hair, snow-leopard foot fur and mountain-goat hooves to their benefits.**

1. Hair/fur provides insulation.
2. Hooves provide grip and support on rocky terrain.

**Answer:** Warmth; protection from cold surfaces; rocky-ground grip. **தமிழில்:** வெப்பப் பாதுகாப்பு; பாதப் பாதுகாப்பு; பாறைப் பிடிப்பு. **Trap:** Do not assign the goat's grip to long foot hair.

**F3. Why is a lion's light-brown coat useful in dry grass?**

1. Its colour can resemble the surrounding vegetation.
2. Reduced visibility can help it approach prey.

**Answer:** Camouflage. **தமிழில்:** சூழலுடன் கலந்து மறைய உதவும் நிறம். **Trap:** Camouflage can help prey too.

**F4. Why compare a lion's front-facing eyes with a deer's side-facing eyes?**

1. Overlapping forward vision supports distance judgement.
2. A broader lateral view helps detect approaching danger.

**Answer:** Different visual benefits for hunting and vigilance. **தமிழில்:** இரையின் தூரம் கணித்தல்; சுற்றியுள்ள ஆபத்தை அறிதல். **Trap:** Neither pattern is an absolute rule for every animal.

**F5. A statement says a deer's speed mainly helps it catch smaller prey. Is this the chapter's correct relation?**

1. The deer is the herbivorous prey example.
2. Its speed is linked to escaping predators.

**Answer:** No; escape is the intended relation. **தமிழில்:** மானின் வேகம் வேட்டையாடுபவரிடமிருந்து தப்பிக்க உதவும். **Trap:** Read whether the animal is hunter or hunted.

### 9.7 Aquatic applications

**G1. A dolphin surfaces and exposes its blowhole. Why?**

1. It has lungs and must obtain atmospheric air.
2. The blowhole is the breathing opening, not a gill.

**Answer:** To breathe air. **தமிழில்:** நுரையீரலுக்குக் காற்றைப் பெற மேலே வருகிறது. **Trap:** An aquatic habitat does not prove gill breathing.

**G2. Does streamlining explain a fish's ability to extract dissolved oxygen?**

1. Streamlining reduces resistance during swimming.
2. Gills provide the gas-exchange surface.

**Answer:** No; these are different structure-function relations. **தமிழில்:** உடல்வடிவம் நீந்த உதவும்; செவுள் வாயுப் பரிமாற்றத்திற்கு உதவும். **Trap:** Both are aquatic adaptations but have different jobs.

**G3. A submerged plant has narrow flexible leaves. How can that help in a current?**

1. Flexible leaves bend with water movement.
2. A narrow/divided form can reduce mechanical stress.

**Answer:** It tolerates flowing water with less damage. **தமிழில்:** நீரோட்டத்தில் வளைந்து சேதத்தைக் குறைக்க உதவும். **Trap:** Do not substitute cactus water-storage functions.

**G4. A floating plant is not rooted in the pond bottom. Must the observation be wrong?**

1. Aquatic plants include free-floating forms.
2. Bottom attachment is not universal.

**Answer:** No; it may be free-floating. **தமிழில்:** அடித்தளத்தில் வேரூன்றாமல் மிதக்கும் தாவரங்களும் உள்ளன. **Trap:** One rooted-water-lily diagram cannot describe every hydrophyte.

**G5. A frog swims with webbed feet. Does “amphibian” apply to a crocodile for the same reason?**

1. Swimming ability and habitat alone do not determine animal class.
2. Frog is an amphibian; crocodile is a reptile.

**Answer:** No. **தமிழில்:** நீரிலும் நிலத்திலும் இருப்பதாலேயே எல்லாம் இருவாழ்வி வகுப்பில் சேராது. **Trap:** Keep habitat labels separate from animal classification groups.
