## 1. 🧭 Your learning route | படிக்கும் வரிசை

**Start with this question:** A plant does not eat a plate of rice. How does it get the materials and energy needed to grow?

## 2. 🧠 Nutrition begins with materials and energy | ஊட்டச்சத்து அடிப்படை

### 2.1 Food, nutrient and nutrition

**Nutrition** is the process of obtaining and using nutrients. Organisms need materials and energy for growth, repair, maintenance and reproduction.

A **nutrient** is a substance needed for life processes. Familiar food components include carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins and minerals. Water is essential too. **Not every nutrient supplies energy**: water and mineral ions are not fuels like glucose.

Plants take in inorganic materials such as CO₂, water and mineral ions. They use these to build organic substances such as carbohydrates and proteins. **Organic** here refers to carbon-based biological compounds; it does not mean an “organic farming” label.

The PDF's examples of mineral ions include nitrate **NO₃⁻**, potassium **K⁺**, calcium **Ca²⁺** and zinc **Zn²⁺**. An ion is an electrically charged atom or group of atoms.

> **தமிழில்:** ஊட்டச்சத்து என்பது தேவையான சத்துகளைப் பெற்று பயன்படுத்தும் செயல். எல்லாச் சத்துகளும் ஆற்றல் தருவதில்லை. தண்ணீரும் தாது உப்புகளும் அவசியம்; ஆனால் அவை குளுக்கோஸ் போன்ற எரிபொருள் அல்ல.

### 2.2 Autotrophic and heterotrophic nutrition

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Autotroph — தற்சார்பு ஊட்ட உயிரி | Builds organic food using inorganic carbon, usually CO₂ | Green plant |
| Heterotroph — பிறசார்பு ஊட்ட உயிரி | Depends on organic substances made by other organisms | Human, mushroom |
| Photoautotroph | Uses light as its energy source | Green plant, many algae, cyanobacteria |
| Chemoautotroph | Uses energy from oxidation of inorganic substances to build food from CO₂ | Certain nitrifying, iron-oxidising and sulphur-oxidising bacteria |

**Word roots:** *auto* = self; *heteros* = other; *troph* relates to nourishment; *photo* = light. Autotrophs still require external materials and energy: “self-feeding” does not mean making matter from nothing.

**Recognition only:** The PDF mentions purple, red and green photosynthetic bacteria. Some purple and green bacteria perform photosynthesis that does not release oxygen. Colour alone does not establish an organism's nutrition type. Do not extend the green-plant equation to every photosynthetic bacterium.

**Correction:** Plants are not the only autotrophs, and most unicellular organisms cannot simply be assumed to be autotrophic. Amoeba and yeast are familiar counterexamples. Cyanobacteria are bacteria, historically called “blue-green algae.”

> **🧠 Concept map: Where food comes from**
>
> - **Autotrophic:** organic food built from inorganic materials — தானே உணவு தயாரித்தல்.
> - **Heterotrophic:** organic food obtained from other organisms — பிற உயிரிகளைச் சார்தல்.
> - **Within heterotrophy:** parasitic, saprotrophic and holozoic modes differ in how food is obtained.
> - **Holozoic:** taking food into the body and digesting it internally, as in humans.

### 2.3 The cell terms you need

| Term | Beginner meaning | Important distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Cell — செல் | Basic structural and functional unit of life | An organism can have one cell or many. |
| Cell membrane — செல் சவ்வு | Boundary controlling movement into and out of a cell | Different from a plant's outer cell wall. |
| Cytoplasm — சைட்டோபிளாசம் | Material inside the membrane, outside the nucleus in a typical plant cell | Many cell activities occur here. |
| Nucleus — உட்கரு | Contains most genetic material in a typical plant cell | Not necessarily central; bacteria lack a membrane-bound nucleus. |
| Chloroplast — பசுங்கணிகம் | Photosynthetic organelle in green plant cells | Chlorophyll is a pigment inside it. |

Most cells need magnification to be studied clearly, but “every cell is invisible without a microscope” is too absolute. Not every plant cell contains chloroplasts; ordinary underground root cells usually do not.

> **⚠️ Exam trap:** Cyanobacteria photosynthesise without chloroplasts. “Photosynthesis occurs in chloroplasts” describes green plant/algal cells, not every photosynthetic organism.

**Quick self-check:** Can you explain why a plant needs fertiliser even though it makes food? Fertiliser supplies mineral nutrients; photosynthesis supplies organic food. These needs are different.

## 3. ☀️ Photosynthesis: making food | ஒளிச்சேர்க்கை

### 3.1 Meaning, ingredients and equation

**Photosynthesis** uses light energy to make organic food. In green plants, chlorophyll absorbs light; carbon dioxide and water supply the materials used to build carbohydrates. Oxygen is released as a by-product.

**Word equation:** Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen, **with light and chlorophyll**.

**Balanced net equation:** **6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂**, with light energy and chlorophyll.

This is a summary of many reactions, not a single-step recipe. **CO₂ and water are raw materials; light is energy; chlorophyll captures light.** Do not list sunlight as a material substance.

> **🔬 Process map: From sunlight to food**
>
> 1. Chlorophyll absorbs light energy.
> 2. Light-dependent reactions convert that energy into chemical forms; water splitting releases oxygen.
> 3. The captured energy helps convert CO₂ into organic compounds.
> 4. The plant uses its sugars for respiration, growth, transport and storage.

> **💡 Why does a plant need both sunlight and CO₂?** Light supplies energy. CO₂ supplies carbon atoms. Energy cannot replace the material needed to build a sugar molecule.

> **தமிழில்:** ஒளிச்சேர்க்கையில் கார்பன் டைஆக்சைடும் நீரும் மூலப்பொருட்கள். ஒளி ஆற்றலை வழங்குகிறது; பச்சையம் அதைப் பெறுகிறது. தாவரம் உணவை உருவாக்கி ஆக்சிஜனை வெளியிடுகிறது.

**Supplement — oxygen's origin:** The oxygen released in plant photosynthesis comes from **water**, not directly from CO₂. Water splitting supplies electrons and hydrogen ions; it does not mean that leaves normally release hydrogen gas. The PDF's simple sequence questions compress overlapping biochemical reactions. [Reference: NCERT, Photosynthesis in Higher Plants](https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/kebo111.pdf).

### 3.2 Glucose, starch, sucrose and ATP

| Substance | Role to remember | Tamil cue |
|---|---|---|
| Glucose | A simple sugar used in metabolism; shown as food in the net equation | குளுக்கோஸ் — எளிய சர்க்கரை |
| Starch | Major storage carbohydrate in plants | ஸ்டார்ச் — சேமிப்பு |
| Sucrose | Main sugar transported over long distances in many plants | சுக்ரோஸ் — கடத்தல் |
| ATP | Immediate energy-transfer molecule used by cells | ATP — உடனடி ஆற்றல் பரிமாற்றம் |

**ATP = adenosine triphosphate; ADP = adenosine diphosphate.** “Energy currency” refers to ATP. It does not mean that ATP is the plant's principal long-term food store. Pyruvate is a product of glucose breakdown during glycolysis; it is not the answer to “energy currency.”

Carbohydrates contain carbon, hydrogen and oxygen. Plants can use products of photosynthesis to build fats and other organic substances. Making proteins also requires **nitrogen**, because proteins are built from amino acids containing nitrogen. Nitrogen is also required for nucleic acids such as DNA and RNA.

**Source connection:** Sugarcane is the PDF's example of a crop converting solar energy into chemical energy. No efficiency percentage is needed to answer its question.

### 3.3 Photosynthesis and respiration coexist

| Feature | Photosynthesis in green plants | Aerobic respiration |
|---|---|---|
| Overall purpose | Builds organic food using light | Releases usable energy from food |
| Simplified inputs | CO₂ and water | Glucose and oxygen |
| Simplified outputs | Carbohydrate and oxygen | CO₂, water and usable energy |
| Timing | Requires suitable light | Continues in living cells during day and night |

In daylight, both processes can happen together. A leaf's **net** gas exchange depends on their relative rates. Artificial light can support photosynthesis when conditions are suitable; “day only” is shorthand for the usual natural-light situation.

> **⚠️ Exam trap:** “Plants take in only CO₂” is false. Plants also use oxygen in respiration. Oxygen is a photosynthetic by-product, but it is not useless to the plant.

**Ecology connection:** Photosynthesis supports most food chains and supplies oxygen for aerobic life. Avoid “all life needs oxygen” or “sunlight is the energy source for absolutely every ecosystem”; anaerobic organisms and chemosynthesis-based systems are exceptions.

### 3.4 Worked models A — food and energy

**A1. A green plant receives water and minerals but is kept without light. Can mineral supply replace photosynthesis?**

1. Minerals supply elements needed for growth.
2. They do not supply the light energy needed for photosynthesis.

**Answer:** No. Stored food can support the plant temporarily, but minerals do not replace light-driven food production.

> **தமிழில்:** தாது உப்புகள் கிடைத்தாலும் ஒளி இல்லாமல் வழக்கமான ஒளிச்சேர்க்கை நடக்காது.
> **⚠️ Trap:** Fertiliser is not ready-made glucose.

**A2. Match energy currency, storage carbohydrate and transported sugar.**

1. Immediate cellular energy transfer identifies ATP.
2. Storage identifies starch; long-distance sugar transport identifies sucrose.

**Answer:** ATP — starch — sucrose, respectively.

> **தமிழில்:** உடனடி ஆற்றல்: ATP; சேமிப்பு: ஸ்டார்ச்; கடத்தல்: சுக்ரோஸ்.
> **⚠️ Trap:** Glucose is not the answer to every food-related question.

**A3. A plant is illuminated with suitable artificial light at night. Must photosynthesis stop?**

1. Identify the actual requirement: suitable light, not a clock time.
2. Check that water, CO₂ and functioning photosynthetic tissues are available.

**Answer:** No; photosynthesis can occur under suitable artificial illumination.

> **தமிழில்:** முக்கியம் ஒளி கிடைப்பது; பகல் நேரம் என்ற பெயர் மட்டும் அல்ல.
> **⚠️ Trap:** Do not turn the usual daylight condition into an absolute rule.

**A4. In a water-labelling experiment, which material supplies the oxygen released by a green plant?**

1. Separate the sources of carbon and released oxygen.
2. Carbon comes from CO₂; released oxygen comes from water splitting.

**Answer:** Water.

> **தமிழில்:** வெளியேறும் ஆக்சிஜனின் மூலம் நீர்.
> **⚠️ Trap:** Seeing “oxygen” in CO₂ does not identify the released gas's source.

**A5. A bacterium builds organic food from CO₂ using energy from oxidising an inorganic compound. Classify it.**

1. Making organic food from inorganic carbon identifies autotrophy.
2. Chemical oxidation, rather than light, identifies the energy source.

**Answer:** Chemoautotroph.

> **தமிழில்:** வேதிவினை ஆற்றலைப் பயன்படுத்தும் தற்சார்பு ஊட்ட உயிரி.
> **⚠️ Trap:** Autotrophy is broader than photosynthesis.

## 4. 🍃 Getting materials in and moving food | உறிஞ்சுதல் மற்றும் கடத்தல்

### 4.1 Root hairs, stomata and guard cells

**Root hairs** increase the surface available for absorption of water and dissolved mineral nutrients. These materials move through the plant in **xylem**.

**Stomata** are microscopic pores, commonly on leaf surfaces. **Stoma** is singular. A pair of **guard cells** regulates a pore's opening. CO₂ can enter through the pore, while oxygen and water vapour can leave. Gas movement depends on concentration differences; stomata are not one-way valves.

**Mesophyll** means the inner leaf tissue where much photosynthesis occurs. It contains many chloroplasts. **Epidermis** is the outer tissue; guard cells are specialised epidermal cells. **Veins** contain transport tissues.

> **தமிழில்:** வேர் தூவிகள் நீர் மற்றும் தாது உப்புகளை உறிஞ்சுகின்றன. இலைத்துளைகளின் திறப்பு–மூடலை காப்புச் செல்கள் கட்டுப்படுத்துகின்றன. இலைத்துளை என்பது துளை; காப்புச் செல் என்பது அதனைச் சுற்றியுள்ள செல்.

### 4.2 Xylem versus phloem

| Tissue/process | What it does | Do not confuse with |
|---|---|---|
| Xylem — சைலம் | Carries water and minerals, mainly from roots upwards | Food transport |
| Phloem — புளோயம் | Carries sugars and other organic solutes from sources to sinks | Water-only transport |
| Translocation | Movement of organic food through phloem | Transpiration |
| Transpiration | Loss of water vapour from aerial plant parts, mainly through stomata | Movement of sugar |

**Supplement — source and sink:** A **source** supplies sugar, such as a mature photosynthesising leaf. A **sink** uses or stores it, such as a developing fruit or growing root. Transport may be upwards or downwards in different phloem pathways; “food always travels down” is wrong.

**Other option words:** Osmosis is water movement across a selectively permeable membrane under an appropriate water-potential difference. Diffusion is net movement down a concentration gradient. Neither is the name for the whole long-distance food-transport process.

> **🔬 Process map: Two connected routes**
>
> 1. Root hairs absorb water and mineral ions.
> 2. Xylem carries them towards leaves and other tissues.
> 3. Photosynthetic tissues make sugars using CO₂ and light.
> 4. Phloem translocates organic food to tissues that use or store it.

### 4.3 Where photosynthesis happens

Leaves are called **food factories**, but other chlorophyll-containing parts can photosynthesise. In many cacti, the **green stem** performs most photosynthesis; leaves are modified into spines. Green branches can also photosynthesise.

Submerged aquatic plants can use **dissolved CO₂** in water; some also use bicarbonate. Floating or emergent leaves can obtain CO₂ from the atmosphere. Pond algae photosynthesise when they have suitable photosynthetic pigments and conditions.

> **📌 Remember:** Leaf is the usual organ; chloroplast is the plant-cell organelle; chlorophyll is the light-absorbing pigment. These are three different levels.

### 4.4 Worked models B — structures and transport

**B1. Sugar moves from a mature leaf to a growing root. Name the tissue and process.**

1. The transported substance is organic food.
2. Food moves through phloem by translocation.

**Answer:** Phloem; translocation.

> **தமிழில்:** உணவு புளோயம் வழியாக இடம்பெயர்கிறது.
> **⚠️ Trap:** A root destination does not make this xylem transport.

**B2. Which structure regulates the pore through which CO₂ enters a leaf?**

1. The pore is a stoma.
2. Its surrounding guard cells regulate its opening.

**Answer:** Guard cells.

> **தமிழில்:** துளையை இயக்குவது காப்புச் செல்கள்.
> **⚠️ Trap:** “Stomata” answers the pore question, not the regulating-cell question.

**B3. A cactus has spines instead of broad leaves. Which part usually makes most of its food?**

1. Look for green photosynthetic tissue.
2. In the typical cactus example, this is the stem.

**Answer:** Green stem.

> **தமிழில்:** கள்ளியின் பச்சைத் தண்டு ஒளிச்சேர்க்கை செய்கிறது.
> **⚠️ Trap:** Leaves are not the only possible photosynthetic organs.

**B4. A submerged water plant has no leaves exposed to air. Where can its CO₂ come from?**

1. CO₂ can dissolve in water.
2. A submerged plant can use this dissolved source.

**Answer:** Dissolved CO₂ in the surrounding water.

> **தமிழில்:** நீரில் கரைந்துள்ள CO₂ பயன்படுகிறது.
> **⚠️ Trap:** Do not apply this answer to every leaf of every aquatic plant.

**B5. A plant loses water vapour while sending sugar to a fruit. Name both processes.**

1. Water-vapour loss identifies transpiration.
2. Sugar movement to a fruit identifies translocation.

**Answer:** Transpiration and translocation, respectively.

> **தமிழில்:** நீராவி இழப்பு: நீராவிப்போக்கு; உணவு நகர்வு: உணவுக் கடத்தல்.
> **⚠️ Trap:** Similar-looking English words describe different substances and processes.

## 5. 🎨 Pigments, light and yellow leaves | நிறமிகளும் ஒளியும்

### 5.1 Pigment facts

| Pigment | Usual colour association | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| Chlorophyll — பச்சையம் | Green | Contains central magnesium, **Mg**. |
| Xanthophyll | Yellow | A carotenoid pigment. |
| Carotene | Orange/yellow-orange | Another carotenoid; distinguish from anthocyanin. |
| Anthocyanin | Red, purple or blue, depending on conditions | Commonly located in vacuoles; helps explain many flower/leaf colours. |

Red, purple or brown leaves can still contain chlorophyll: other pigments can **mask** its green colour. Do not confuse this with a white, chlorophyll-free patch of a variegated leaf.

### 5.2 Which light is useful?

Chlorophyll absorbs strongly in blue and red regions. Green light is less strongly absorbed, with much reflected or transmitted. **Some green light can still support photosynthesis.** The PDF labels sunlight “UV”; the normal teaching focus is visible light, not UV as a special requirement. [Reference: NCERT, Photosynthesis in Higher Plants](https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/kebo111.pdf).

**For the PDF's simple colour MCQs:** choose **red** for “maximum” among its listed options and **green** for “least effective.” Treat these as conventional simplified questions. Actual rates depend on wavelength, light intensity, tissue, pigments and other conditions; there is no universal ranking for every experimental setup.

> **தமிழில்:** சிவப்பு, நீல ஒளியை பச்சையம் நன்றாக உறிஞ்சுகிறது. பச்சை ஒளி குறைவாக உறிஞ்சப்படுகிறது; “பச்சை ஒளியில் ஒளிச்சேர்க்கையே இல்லை” என்று கூறக்கூடாது.

### 5.3 Chlorosis and look-alike terms

**Chlorosis** means yellowing associated with reduced chlorophyll. Nitrogen or magnesium deficiency can cause it. Yellowing alone does not prove which nutrient is deficient; disease, water stress and other deficiencies may also contribute.

| Term | Meaning | Distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Chlorosis | Loss/reduction of normal green colour | Nitrogen/Mg deficiency are possible causes. |
| Necrosis | Death of cells or tissue | Dead patches, rather than merely yellow tissue. |
| Etiolation | Pale, elongated growth under inadequate light | Not a synonym for all mineral deficiency. |
| Plasmolysis | Cell contents shrink away from the wall after water loss | An osmotic effect, not a pigment name. |

### 5.4 Worked models C — colour and diagnosis

**C1. A purple leaf contains chlorophyll. Can it photosynthesise?**

1. Visible purple colour can mask green pigment.
2. Chlorophyll and functioning cells can still capture light.

**Answer:** Yes, under suitable conditions.

> **தமிழில்:** ஊதா நிறம் இருந்தாலும் பச்சையம் இருக்கலாம்.
> **⚠️ Trap:** Visible colour alone does not prove chlorophyll is absent.

**C2. An MCQ asks for the central metal in chlorophyll: Fe, Ca, Mg or Zn.**

1. Recall the pigment-specific fact.
2. Magnesium is the central metal.

**Answer:** Mg.

> **தமிழில்:** பச்சையத்தின் மைய உலோகம் மெக்னீசியம்.
> **⚠️ Trap:** Do not transfer iron's association with haemoglobin to chlorophyll.

**C3. Which is least effective in the PDF's basic colour comparison: blue, red, green or yellow?**

1. Recall that chlorophyll absorbs blue and red strongly.
2. Much green light is reflected or transmitted.

**Answer:** Green, under the intended simplified comparison.

> **தமிழில்:** இக்கேள்வியின் எதிர்பார்க்கப்படும் விடை பச்சை.
> **⚠️ Trap:** “Least effective” does not mean “impossible.”

**C4. A leaf turns yellow. Can you diagnose magnesium deficiency from colour alone?**

1. Yellowing is a symptom called chlorosis.
2. Several deficiencies and stresses can cause the same symptom.

**Answer:** No. More evidence is needed to identify the cause.

> **தமிழில்:** மஞ்சள் நிறம் ஒரு அறிகுறி; அதுவே துல்லியமான காரணம் அல்ல.
> **⚠️ Trap:** A possible cause is not the only cause.

**C5. Match purple-blue flowers and yellow accessory pigment.**

1. Purple/blue commonly points to anthocyanins.
2. Yellow accessory carotenoid points to xanthophyll.

**Answer:** Anthocyanin; xanthophyll.

> **தமிழில்:** ஊதா/நீலம்: அந்தோசயனின்; மஞ்சள்: சாந்தோஃபில்.
> **⚠️ Trap:** Colour associations help recognition but do not replace a full pigment analysis.

## 6. 🔬 Testing photosynthesis through starch | ஸ்டார்ச் சோதனை

### 6.1 What iodine detects

**Iodine solution gives a blue-black colour with starch.** It is not a universal carbohydrate test. Glucose and sucrose are carbohydrates but do not give this starch result by themselves.

The link is: photosynthesis makes sugars; a leaf may convert some into starch. Under controlled conditions, newly accumulated starch supports the conclusion that photosynthesis occurred.

### 6.2 The PDF's dark/sunlight activity

1. Use two potted plants of the same kind.
2. Keep one in darkness for **72 hours** and the other in sunlight, as the source describes.
3. Test suitable leaves for starch. The expected comparison is little/no detectable starch in the dark-treated leaf and a positive result in the illuminated leaf.
4. Return the dark-treated plant to sunlight for **3–4 days**, as stated in the PDF, and test again.

**Destarching** means reducing previously stored starch before an investigation. In darkness, photosynthesis stops but respiration and transport continue using stored food. A fixed 72-hour period does **not** guarantee that every plant has lost every trace of starch; verify the starting condition.

> **தமிழில்:** இருளில் வைத்தால் புதிய ஒளிச்சேர்க்கை நடக்காது; சேமித்த உணவு பயன்படுத்தப்படும். பழைய ஸ்டார்ச்சைக் குறைப்பதே destarching. “மூன்று நாட்களில் எல்லா ஸ்டார்ச்சும் கட்டாயம் மறையும்” என்பது மிகைப்படுத்தல்.

### 6.3 Supplement — the complete leaf-test sequence

> **🔬 Process map: Preparing a leaf for iodine**
>
> 1. Heat the leaf briefly in hot/boiling water to kill it and make its tissues more permeable.
> 2. Place it in ethanol heated using a hot-water bath to remove chlorophyll.
> 3. Rinse the decolourised leaf in water to remove ethanol and soften it.
> 4. Spread it on a pale surface and add iodine solution.
> 5. Observe blue-black areas as evidence of starch.

**Why remove chlorophyll?** The green colour can hide the iodine result. **Why rinse?** Ethanol makes the leaf brittle; water softens it. Carry out practical work with teacher/laboratory supervision, eye protection and care with hot liquids. **Ethanol is flammable: keep it away from flames.** [Procedure reference: Practical Biology](https://practicalbiology.org/standard-techniques/testing-leaves-for-starch-the-technique.html).

### 6.4 Supplement — interpreting controlled experiments

| Investigation | Fair comparison | Expected interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Light requirement | Destarched leaf: opaque-covered area versus exposed area | Starch in exposed area supports the need for light. |
| Chlorophyll requirement | Previously mapped green and non-green areas of a suitable destarched variegated leaf | Starch mainly in green areas supports chlorophyll's role. |
| CO₂ requirement | Similar destarched plants with/without CO₂ removal, keeping other conditions comparable | Less/no new starch where CO₂ is unavailable supports its requirement. |

These are **interpretations**, not full laboratory instructions. CO₂-absorbing chemicals require supervised handling. A proper comparison changes the factor being investigated while keeping other relevant conditions similar.

> **⚠️ Exam trap:** A blue-black leaf proves starch is present. Without controlling existing starch and other conditions, it does not by itself prove exactly when that starch formed.

### 6.5 Worked models D — experiments

**D1. A glucose solution does not turn blue-black with iodine. Does this prove it has no carbohydrate?**

1. Iodine detects starch.
2. Glucose is a different carbohydrate.

**Answer:** No; absence of the starch result does not exclude glucose.

> **தமிழில்:** அயோடின் சோதனை எல்லாக் கார்போஹைட்ரேட்டுகளுக்கும் அல்ல.
> **⚠️ Trap:** Do not expand a specific test into a general one.

**D2. Why is ethanol used before iodine in the leaf test?**

1. A green leaf can conceal the colour change.
2. Ethanol removes chlorophyll so the result is easier to see.

**Answer:** To decolourise the leaf.

> **தமிழில்:** பச்சை நிறத்தை நீக்கி முடிவைத் தெளிவாகக் காண உதவுகிறது.
> **⚠️ Trap:** Ethanol is not the starch-detecting reagent.

**D3. Why destarch a plant before testing whether light is necessary?**

1. Existing starch might produce a positive result even without new photosynthesis.
2. Reducing and checking that reserve makes the comparison more meaningful.

**Answer:** To reduce the confounding effect of previously stored starch.

> **தமிழில்:** பழைய ஸ்டார்ச் புதிய முடிவைப் போலத் தோன்றாமல் இருக்க.
> **⚠️ Trap:** Destarching does not mean removing chlorophyll.

**D4. After a controlled test, an exposed leaf area turns blue-black while its opaque-covered area does not. What does this support?**

1. The tested areas differed in access to light.
2. Starch accumulated in the illuminated area under the test conditions.

**Answer:** Light was needed for the observed starch formation through photosynthesis.

> **தமிழில்:** ஒளி பெற்ற பகுதியில் ஸ்டார்ச் உருவானது ஒளியின் தேவையை ஆதரிக்கிறது.
> **⚠️ Trap:** This comparison does not independently test CO₂ or magnesium.

**D5. After 72 hours in darkness, a leaf still tests positive. Must iodine be faulty?**

1. Destarching time depends on plant condition and reserves.
2. Remaining starch is a possible explanation.

**Answer:** No. Check the plant's starting condition and the procedure before concluding reagent failure.

> **தமிழில்:** குறிப்பிட்ட நேரம் முடிந்தது மட்டும் முழுமையான destarching-ஐ உறுதிப்படுத்தாது.
> **⚠️ Trap:** A textbook duration is not a universal biological guarantee.

## 7. 🪴 Parasites and insectivorous plants | ஒட்டுண்ணி மற்றும் பூச்சியுண்ணி தாவரங்கள்

### 7.1 Parasitic nutrition

A **parasite** obtains resources from a living **host**, generally harming the host. Parasitic plants use specialised structures called **haustoria**; singular **haustorium**. These connect to host tissues to obtain resources.

**Cuscuta = dodder = Amarbel.** The PDF describes its yellow, twining growth and dependence on a host. For the basic question, identify it as a parasitic plant. Being a climber alone does not establish parasitism: a plant may use a tree only for support.

| Source example | What to remember | Detail priority |
|---|---|---|
| Cuscuta / dodder / Amarbel | Names for the same familiar parasitic example | Core |
| Viscum and Loranthus | Mistletoe examples; green hemiparasites can photosynthesise while taking host resources | Recognition |
| Orobanche, Hyobanche, Rafflesia | Examples of strongly host-dependent, non-photosynthetic parasites | Recognition |
| Western Australian Christmas tree, Nuytsia floribunda | Photosynthesises but parasitises neighbouring roots | Recognition |

**Holoparasites** depend on hosts for organic carbon; **hemiparasites** retain photosynthesis but obtain water/minerals from hosts. The source's blanket “all parasitic plants lack chlorophyll” idea is therefore wrong. Some Cuscuta species retain limited photosynthetic capability; use its host dependence as the identifying feature. [Reference: Natural History Museum, parasitic plants](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/parasitic-plants.html).

> **தமிழில்:** ஒட்டுண்ணி தாவரம் உயிருள்ள ஓம்புயிரியிடமிருந்து வளங்களைப் பெறுகிறது. எல்லா ஒட்டுண்ணிகளும் பச்சையம் இல்லாதவை அல்ல. மரத்தின் மீது ஏறுவது மட்டும் ஒட்டுண்ணித்தனத்திற்குச் சான்று அல்ல.

### 7.2 Insectivorous/carnivorous nutrition

Carnivorous plants are flowering plants with adaptations for trapping and digesting prey. They commonly live where available mineral nutrients, especially nitrogen, are scarce. **They photosynthesise for organic food and obtain extra nutrients from prey.** They do not need insects as a substitute for all sunlight-made carbohydrate.

| Plant | Trap | Distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Pitcher plant, e.g. Nepenthes | Modified leaf forms a pitfall containing digestive fluid | Prey falls/slips inside; the lid is not a snapping jaw. |
| Venus flytrap, Dionaea | Leaf trap closes when appropriately stimulated | This is the familiar snap trap. |
| Sundew, Drosera | Sticky glandular tentacles | Sticky trapping, not a pitcher. |
| Bladderwort, Utricularia | Small suction bladders | Captures tiny prey, often in water. |

**Spelling correction:** The PDF's “Dionea” should be **Dionaea**. “Dionaea” and “Venus flytrap” are not two separate plant types.

In the pitcher example, modified leaf tissue forms the pitcher; a lid sits over the opening. Slippery surfaces and, in some pitchers, downward-pointing hairs hinder escape. Digestive fluid helps break down prey, and nutrients are absorbed.

**Source correction:** The PDF says the pitcher lid closes after an insect enters. Do not transfer the Venus flytrap mechanism to a pitcher. Many pitcher lids help shelter the fluid from rain. [Reference: Indiana University, Nepenthes](https://greenhouse.biology.indiana.edu/features/carnivores/Nepenthes-sp.html).

> **🔬 Process map: Why a green plant traps prey**
>
> 1. Light and CO₂ support carbohydrate production.
> 2. Nutrient-poor soil limits available nitrogen and other minerals.
> 3. Modified leaves trap prey.
> 4. Digestion and absorption supply additional nutrients.

> **தமிழில்:** பூச்சியுண்ணித் தாவரம் ஒளிச்சேர்க்கை செய்து உணவைத் தயாரிக்கிறது. பூச்சிகளிடமிருந்து முக்கியமாக நைட்ரஜன் போன்ற தாதுச் சத்துகளைப் பெறுகிறது. குடுவைத் தாவரமும் வீனஸ் ஈப்பொறியும் ஒரே முறையில் பிடிப்பதில்லை.

### 7.3 Worked models E — hosts and prey

**E1. A yellow Cuscuta stem connects to another plant and obtains resources. Name the relationship.**

1. Resources come from a living host.
2. Cuscuta benefits at the host's expense.

**Answer:** Parasitism.

> **தமிழில்:** உயிருள்ள ஓம்புயிரியிடமிருந்து பெறுவதால் ஒட்டுண்ணி ஊட்டம்.
> **⚠️ Trap:** It is not feeding on dead matter.

**E2. A green plant parasitises another plant's roots. Is “green parasite” a contradiction?**

1. Photosynthesis can supply its carbon compounds.
2. Parasitic connections can still supply water and minerals.

**Answer:** No; a hemiparasite can remain photosynthetic.

> **தமிழில்:** பச்சையம் இருந்தாலும் பிற தாவரத்தின் நீர்/தாதுகளைப் பெறலாம்.
> **⚠️ Trap:** Do not define every parasite by absence of chlorophyll.

**E3. A pitcher plant photosynthesises. Why does it still catch insects?**

1. Photosynthesis makes carbohydrates.
2. Prey supplies mineral nutrients missing from poor soil.

**Answer:** Mainly to supplement nutrients such as nitrogen.

> **தமிழில்:** உணவு தயாரிப்பும் நைட்ரஜன் பெறுதலும் வேறு தேவைகள்.
> **⚠️ Trap:** “It cannot make any food” is wrong.

**E4. A question describes a leaf snapping shut. Choose pitcher plant or Venus flytrap.**

2. A snap trap matches Dionaea.

**Answer:** Venus flytrap.

> **தமிழில்:** வேகமாக மூடும் பொறி வீனஸ் ஈப்பொறியுடையது.
> **⚠️ Trap:** A pitcher lid is not a flytrap jaw.

**E5. An ordinary climber uses a tree for support. Is that enough to call it a parasite?**

1. Support is different from extracting host nutrients.
2. Look for an actual feeding connection and host dependence.

**Answer:** No; support alone is insufficient evidence.

> **தமிழில்:** ஆதரவுக்காக ஏறுதல் மட்டும் ஒட்டுண்ணித்தனம் அல்ல.
> **⚠️ Trap:** Judge the feeding relationship, not merely appearance.

## 8. 🍄 Saprotrophs and bread mould | சிதைவூண் ஊட்டம்

### 8.1 External digestion

**Saprotrophs** obtain nutrients from dead or decaying organic matter. Many fungi and bacteria do this. A fungus releases digestive enzymes onto the material, breaks complex substances into soluble products and absorbs them.

**Saprotrophic nutrition — சிதைவூண் ஊட்டம்.** The older term “saprophytic” appears in the PDF and exam options. Recognise the intended meaning, but do not classify fungi as plants. Modern classification separates them.

> **🔬 Process map: Feeding without swallowing**
>
> 1. A fungus grows on a suitable organic substrate.
> 2. It secretes enzymes outside its body.
> 3. Enzymes break down complex material into soluble substances.
> 4. The fungus absorbs the products.

**Substrate** means the material on which an organism grows. Bread, pickles, leather and cloth can be substrates for fungal growth; **they are not themselves saprotrophic organisms**. Mushrooms and bread mould are organism examples. Not all fungi are saprotrophs: some are parasites or mutual partners.

> **தமிழில்:** பூஞ்சை வெளியே நொதிகளைச் சுரந்து உணவைச் சிறிய கரையக்கூடிய பொருட்களாக உடைக்கிறது. பிறகு அவற்றை உறிஞ்சுகிறது. ரொட்டி பூஞ்சையின் உணவுத்தளம்; ரொட்டியே பூஞ்சை அல்ல.

### 8.2 The PDF's bread activity

The source describes moistening bread and leaving it in a warm place for **2–3 days**. Fluffy growth may appear. Fungal **spores** present in the environment can germinate when conditions are suitable. Many moulds grow as thread-like **hyphae**; a network is a **mycelium**.

The useful conclusion is that moisture, suitable warmth and food favour growth. It does not show that bread spontaneously creates life. Growth time varies; two days is not a guarantee.

For observation, use a teacher-prepared sealed sample or supplied photographs. Keep a mouldy sample closed; do not sniff, taste or handle its growth. It is unnecessary to culture mould yourself to learn the concept.

### 8.3 Worked models F — decomposition

**F1. A fungus secretes enzymes onto dead wood and absorbs dissolved products. Name its nutrition.**

1. The material is dead organic matter.
2. Digestion occurs outside the organism before absorption.

**Answer:** Saprotrophic nutrition.

> **தமிழில்:** இறந்த கரிமப் பொருளை வெளியே செரித்து உறிஞ்சுதல்.
> **⚠️ Trap:** This is not internal, holozoic digestion.

**F2. Is a pickle itself a saprotroph because mould can grow on it?**

1. Identify the growing organism: the mould.
2. Identify its substrate: the pickle.

**Answer:** No. The mould is the organism; the pickle is the substrate.

> **தமிழில்:** உணவுத்தளத்தையும் அதில் வளரும் உயிரையும் பிரித்தறி.
> **⚠️ Trap:** A list of spoiled materials is not a list of organisms.

**F3. Mould appears on moist bread. Did the bread create the fungus from nothing?**

1. Spores can already be present on the bread or arrive from its surroundings.
2. Suitable conditions allow germination and growth.

**Answer:** No; growth can arise from pre-existing fungal spores.

> **தமிழில்:** ஏற்கெனவே இருந்த வித்துகள் ஏற்ற சூழலில் வளர்கின்றன.
> **⚠️ Trap:** Visible appearance is not the same as the beginning of existence.

**F4. A fungus obtains nutrients from a living plant and damages it. Must it be a saprotroph?**

1. Its source is living host tissue, not dead matter.
2. The harmful feeding relationship is parasitic.

**Answer:** No; in this situation it is a parasite.

> **தமிழில்:** எல்லாப் பூஞ்சைகளுக்கும் ஒரே ஊட்டமுறை இல்லை.
> **⚠️ Trap:** “Fungus” is not synonymous with “saprotroph.”

**F5. Why can decomposition help replenish soil nutrients?**

1. Dead organic material contains nutrient elements.
2. Decomposers break it down and help return nutrients to usable cycles.

**Answer:** Decomposition recycles nutrients from organic remains.

> **தமிழில்:** இறந்த பொருள்களின் சத்துகள் மீண்டும் சுற்றுச்சூழலுக்குத் திரும்புகின்றன.
> **⚠️ Trap:** Recycling matter is different from creating new energy.

## 9. 🤝 Mutual partners | இருதரப்பு நன்மை உறவுகள்

### 9.1 Understand the exchange

The PDF uses **symbiosis** for mutually beneficial associations. In broader biology, symbiosis means a close association and can include other relationships; **mutualism** specifically means both partners benefit.

| Association | One partner provides | Other partner provides |
|---|---|---|
| Lichen: fungus + photosynthetic partner | Fungus provides structure and helps retain/acquire water and minerals | Alga or cyanobacterium provides photosynthetic carbon compounds |
| Mycorrhiza: fungus + plant root | Fungus helps the plant obtain mineral nutrients and often water | Plant supplies organic carbon to the fungus |
| Legume + rhizobia | Bacteria fix atmospheric nitrogen into usable compounds | Plant provides carbon compounds and a protected nodule environment |

**Lichen** is the association, not a name for an alga alone. The elementary answer is “alga and fungus”; some lichens contain cyanobacteria as the photosynthetic partner. [Reference: Australian National Botanic Gardens, What is a lichen?](https://www.anbg.gov.au/lichen/what-is-lichen.html).

**Mycorrhiza — பூஞ்சை–வேர் கூட்டுறவு.** Do not confuse a fungal partner with the nitrogen-fixing bacterial partner in a legume nodule.

**Rhizobium** is the standard textbook bacterial answer. “Rhizobia” is a broader collective term for several groups of legume-nodulating bacteria. Peas, gram/chickpea, moong/green gram and beans are the source's legume examples.

## 10. 🌱 Minerals, nitrogen and fertile soil | தாதுகள், நைட்ரஜன் மற்றும் மண் வளம்

### 10.1 Seventeen essential elements

Higher plants generally require **17 essential elements**. “Macro” and “micro” refer to the amount required, not whether an element matters.

| Group | Elements | Revision cue |
|---|---|---|
| Non-mineral macronutrients | Carbon **C**, hydrogen **H**, oxygen **O** | Mainly from CO₂ and water |
| Primary mineral macronutrients | Nitrogen **N**, phosphorus **P**, potassium **K** | **NPK** |
| Secondary mineral macronutrients | Calcium **Ca**, magnesium **Mg**, sulphur **S** | **Ca–Mg–S** |
| Micronutrients | Iron **Fe**, manganese **Mn**, zinc **Zn**, copper **Cu**, boron **B**, molybdenum **Mo**, chlorine **Cl**, nickel **Ni** | Eight; small required amounts |

Thus **9 macronutrients + 8 micronutrients = 17**. The PDF groups C, H, O, N, P and K as “primary”; for fertiliser/mineral classification, **primary macronutrients means N, P and K**. Chlorine is generally absorbed as chloride. [Reference: NC State Extension, plant nutrients](https://content.ces.ncsu.edu/extension-gardener-handbook/1-soils-and-plant-nutrients).

| Element | Useful connection | Avoid this mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Nitrogen | Amino acids, proteins and nucleic acids | More nitrogen is not always better. |
| Phosphorus | ATP and nucleic acids | Do not confuse P with potassium. |
| Potassium | Enzyme activity and regulation of water balance/stomata | Its symbol is **K**. |
| Magnesium | Central metal in chlorophyll | Iron is not the central atom. |

> **தமிழில்:** அதிக அளவில் தேவைப்படுவது பேரூட்டச் சத்து; குறைந்த அளவில் தேவைப்படுவது நுண்ணூட்டச் சத்து. குறைவாகத் தேவைப்பட்டாலும் அது அவசியமற்றது அல்ல. NPK என்பது நைட்ரஜன், பாஸ்பரஸ், பொட்டாசியம்.

### 10.2 Why atmospheric nitrogen is not immediately usable

About **78% of dry air** is nitrogen gas, N₂. Ordinary plant roots cannot simply absorb this gas and use it as protein nitrogen. N₂ has a strong bond; it must first be converted into biologically usable compounds.

**Nitrogen fixation — நைட்ரஜன் நிலைநிறுத்தம்:** Conversion of atmospheric N₂ into fixed nitrogen compounds. In biological fixation, the enzyme **nitrogenase** helps reduce N₂ to ammonia, which can form ammonium in water and be incorporated into organic compounds.

Rhizobia in legume root nodules perform this conversion. The PDF's wording “soluble N₂” is misleading: **fixation changes the chemical form; it is not merely dissolving the same gas.** [Reference: University of Minnesota Extension, soil biology](https://extension.umn.edu/natural-resources/conservation/agricultural-soil-and-water/soil-biology).

Plants mainly absorb inorganic nitrogen as **nitrate, NO₃⁻**, and **ammonium, NH₄⁺**. The PDF also lists **nitrite, NO₂⁻**; remember it chiefly as a nitrogen-cycle intermediate, not the normal main nitrogen supply to teach alongside nitrate and ammonium. [Reference: University of Minnesota Extension, plant-available nitrogen](https://blog-crop-news.extension.umn.edu/2019/04/will-soil-microbes-deliver-nitrogen-to.html).

### 10.3 Supplement — separate the four nitrogen processes

These distinctions explain the close options in **source Q54**. Learn the direction of change before bacterial names.

| Process | Change | Memory cue |
|---|---|---|
| Nitrogen fixation | Atmospheric N₂ → fixed nitrogen, biologically ammonia/ammonium | Brings atmospheric nitrogen into a usable chemical form |
| Ammonification | Organic nitrogen in remains/waste → ammonia/ammonium | Decomposition releases ammonium |
| Nitrification | Ammonium → nitrite → nitrate | Oxidation in two broad stages |
| Denitrification | Nitrate → gaseous nitrogen products, including N₂ | Returns nitrogen towards the atmosphere |

**Recognition only:** Nitrosomonas is a standard textbook example for ammonia oxidation; Nitrobacter for nitrite oxidation. They are not synonyms for Rhizobium. Plants then **assimilate** absorbed nitrogen by building it into amino acids and other organic substances.

> **🔬 Process map: Nitrogen into a plant**
>
> 1. Nitrogen-fixing microorganisms convert N₂ into fixed nitrogen compounds.
> 2. Soil transformations provide forms such as ammonium and nitrate.
> 3. Roots absorb available nitrogen, or a legume receives fixed nitrogen through its nodule association.
> 4. Plant cells incorporate nitrogen into amino acids, proteins and nucleic acids.
> 5. Decomposition and other microbial processes recycle nitrogen through soil and air.

> **தமிழில்:** வளிமண்டல N₂ அப்படியே புரதமாக மாறாது. நுண்ணுயிர்கள் அதை பயன்படும் சேர்மங்களாக மாற்றுகின்றன. தாவரம் அவற்றைப் பயன்படுத்தி அமினோ அமிலங்கள், புரதங்கள் போன்றவற்றை உருவாக்குகிறது.

### 10.4 Replenishing soil nutrients

Repeated cropping and removal of harvested material can deplete soil nutrients. **Manure** contributes organic material and nutrients; **fertilisers** supply specified nutrients. Their composition and effects differ.

Legumes in a cropping system can reduce nitrogen-fertiliser needs when effective fixation and residue management occur. They do not manufacture phosphorus or potassium and do not automatically eliminate every fertiliser requirement. Nutrient benefit to a following crop depends partly on what material remains and decomposes.

> **💡 Why is growing a legume useful?** Its bacterial partnership introduces fixed nitrogen. It is not simply “a plant putting back every nutrient that another crop removed.”

>
> - **Make:** CO₂ + water + captured light → organic food; oxygen released.

### B. Core Conceptual Bridge:
1. **From Plant Autotrophy to Animal Holotrophy (Next Chapter: `07 Nutrition in Animals`):**
   - In Chapter 06, you learned how autotrophic primary producers create organic matter from scratch using solar energy.
   - Chapter 07 examines the opposite end: heterotrophic animals that cannot build food, requiring an alimentary canal for ingestion, mechanical breakdown, enzymatic chemical digestion, nutrient absorption (villi), and egestion.
2. **Photosynthesis vs. Cellular Respiration:**
   - Photosynthesis stores energy in organic chemical bonds (6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂).
   - Respiration breaks those bonds to release ATP for metabolic work (C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + ATP).
   - Both processes occur simultaneously in plants during daylight, while only respiration continues at night.
