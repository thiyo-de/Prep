## 1. 🧭 Start here: what must agree?

Think of a sentence as a message about someone or something. **The boys play** tells us about *the boys*. **The boy plays** tells us about *one boy*. In the simple present, the verb changes because the subject changes.

| Term | Tamil meaning | Simple explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subject | எழுவாய் | Who/what the clause is about: **The boy** plays. |
| Verb | வினைச்சொல் | An action or state: the boy **plays/is** happy. |
| Agreement / concord | பொருத்தம் / இயைபு | Matching a finite verb to its subject's person and number. |
| Singular / plural | ஒருமை / பன்மை | One / more than one, as expressed grammatically. |
| Person | தன்மை, முன்னிலை, படர்க்கை | Speaker: I/we; listener: you; others: he/she/it/they. |
| Head of a phrase | தொடரின் தலைமைச் சொல் | The central word: **box** in “the box of pencils.” |
| Modifier / qualifier | விளக்கச் சொல் அல்லது தொடர் | Extra description: “of pencils.” |
| Finite verb | காலம் அல்லது பொருத்தம் காட்டும் வினை | The verb carrying tense/agreement: she **has** finished. |
| Auxiliary | துணை வினை | A helping verb: is, have, does, can. |
| Clause | எழுவாய்–வினை கொண்ட பகுதி | A unit such as “she works” inside a sentence. |
| Antecedent | சுட்டப்படும் முன்னுள்ள பெயர் | The noun a relative pronoun refers to: boys **who** run. |

### 1.1 The table you must know first

| Subject | Simple present | Be: present / past | Have / do: present |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | work | am / was | have / do |
| You — one or many | work | are / were | have / do |
| He, she, it, one boy | works | is / was | has / does |
| We, they, two boys | work | are / were | have / do |

**Noun -s and verb -s do different jobs.** *Boy → boys* usually makes a noun plural. *Work → works* makes a simple-present verb third-person singular. The PDF calls *work/go* “plural verbs”; use that only as classroom shorthand. **I work** is correct although I refers to one person.

Noun spelling from the PDF: boy/boys, leaf/leaves, baby/babies; child/children, tooth/teeth, foot/feet. Verb spelling: go/goes, write/writes, clarify/clarifies. Do not add *-s* to every word mechanically.

**Only the relevant finite verb changes:** she **has** completed / they **have** completed; she **does not work** / they **do not work**. After does, keep the main verb in its base form. Ordinary past forms usually do not change: I/he/they **worked**, **went**, **had**, **did**. Modals also do not take third-person *-s*: he/they **can work**.

> **தமிழில்:** “பக்கத்தில் பன்மைச் சொல் இருக்கிறது” என்பதற்காக வினையை மாற்றாதீர்கள். அந்த வினைக்கு உரிய எழுவாயைக் கண்டுபிடியுங்கள். “The box of pencils **is** here” — இருப்பது ஒரு பெட்டி; pencils என்பது பெட்டியை விளக்குகிறது.

### 1.2 Your answer-finding order

> **🔬 Process map: Find the controller**
>
> 1. Read the whole sentence and identify each finite verb.
> 2. Ask who/what belongs to **that verb**, not simply the nearest noun.
> 3. Bracket ordinary descriptive phrases: the box [of pencils].
> 4. Identify special structures: each, a number of, fractions, and, either/or, relative clauses.
> 5. Check person, number, tense and intended meaning.
> 6. Read the corrected sentence again; check its noun and pronoun forms too.

### 1.3 Group A — basic agreement and the true subject: five worked models

**A1. The girl ___ every evening. (read / reads)**

1. Subject = *the girl*: third-person singular.
2. This is a simple-present habit; add the third-person ending.

**Answer: reads.** Check: *The girls read*. **தமிழில்:** ஒரு பெண் என்பதால் reads.

**A2. You ___ ready, and I ___ ready too. (is/are; am/are)**

1. There are two clauses: *you* and *I* each need a verb.
2. You takes **are**, even when speaking to one person; I takes **am**.

**Answer: are; am.** Check: do not treat every singular pronoun like *he*. **தமிழில்:** You ஒருவரைக் குறித்தாலும் are; I உடன் am.

**A3. The colour of these shirts ___ attractive. (is / are)**

1. Bracket *of these shirts*. The head is singular *colour*.
2. Choose the verb for colour, not shirts.

**Answer: is.** Check: *The colours of this shirt are attractive*. **தமிழில்:** நிறம் தான் எழுவாய்; சட்டைகள் அல்ல.

**A4. The student ___ not ___ the instructions. (do/does; understand/understands)**

1. Singular student requires **does** in a present negative.
2. Does already carries agreement; the following main verb remains **understand**.

**Answer: does not understand.** Trap: *does not understands* marks the same agreement twice. **தமிழில்:** does வந்தால் அடுத்த வினை base form.

**A5. The list of books that the teachers recommend ___ on my desk. (is / are)**

1. Main clause: *the list … is on my desk*. List is singular.
2. Inside the description, *the teachers recommend* is a separate subject–verb pair.

**Answer: is.** Check both pairs independently: list–is; teachers–recommend. **தமிழில்:** நீண்ட வாக்கியத்தில் ஒவ்வொரு வினைக்கும் தனியாக எழுவாயைக் காணுங்கள்.

## 2. 👤 Each, every and indefinite pronouns

An **indefinite pronoun** refers to a person/thing without naming exactly who/what: somebody, nothing. The PDF's list is **someone, somebody, everybody, everything, something, anyone, anything, nobody, nothing**; also learn **everyone, anybody, no one**. These take singular agreement: *Nobody knows; everything is ready*.

**Something** is one word and singular. **Some things** contains a plural noun: some things **are** missing.

| Structure | Normal formal pattern | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Each/every + singular noun | Each boy **is**; every student **has** | Consider each individual. |
| Each/either/neither/one of + plural set | Each of the boys **is** | The selected individual controls. |
| Each X and each Y; every X and Y | Each boy and each girl **is** ready | Repeated/distributed individuality. |
| Plural subject + each | We each **have**; the boys each **are** | *We/boys* remains the subject. |

**Each** can stand alone: *Each is ready*. **Every** normally needs a noun: *Every student is ready*, not *Every is ready*. In modern grammatical terminology these noun-introducing words are **determiners**; the PDF uses the older classroom label “adjectives.”

**Either** = one or the other of two; **neither** = not either of two. For a larger set, **any/none** is normally clearer. But **any and none are not always singular**: *Any water is useful; any available rooms are suitable; none of the water is left; none of the rooms are occupied*. With *none of + plural*, singular can also emphasise “not one.” With *either/neither of + plural*, use singular as the formal practice default; plural occurs in actual usage. [Cambridge: none](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/no-none-), [Cambridge: agreement and either/neither](https://dictionaryblog.cambridge.org/2017/05/10/we-agree-but-she-agrees-the-importance-of-subject-verb-agreement/).

### 2.1 Everybody is … but they are

**Everybody has their book.** Everybody still takes **has**. *Their* can refer back to a person without specifying gender. In a new clause, **they know**, not *they knows*. Do not replace every *their* with *his*. [British Council: indefinite pronouns](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/indefinite-pronouns).

**Everyone** means every person. **Every one** means each individual member and can refer to things: *Every one of the films is interesting*. Not *everyone of the films*.

**Nothing but** means nothing except: *Nothing but books was available*. Nothing controls this construction. *Nobody but Meena was present* refers to a person. **But** can mean “however” as a conjunction, or “except” in these constructions.

> **தமிழில்:** Each என்பது ஒவ்வொருவராகக் கருதுகிறது. “We each have” என்றால் எழுவாய் We; each அல்ல. “Everybody has their ticket” என்பதில் has சரி; their இருப்பதால் have என்று மாற்றக் கூடாது.

### 2.2 Group B — five worked models

**B1. Somebody ___ waiting outside. (is / are)**

1. Somebody is an indefinite pronoun with singular agreement.
2. Choose **is**; we do not need to know the person's identity.

**Answer: is.** Check: somebody ≠ some people. **தமிழில்:** யாரோ ஒருவர் → is.

**B2. Each of the candidates ___ an identity card. (has / have)**

1. The set contains many candidates; **each** selects them individually.
2. Keep the set noun plural and the main verb singular.

**Answer: has.** Trap: *each of the candidate* also has a noun error. **தமிழில்:** பலரில் ஒவ்வொருவர் என்பதால் has.

**B3. The candidates each ___ an identity card. (has / have)**

1. The subject is now plural **candidates**.
2. Each adds distribution but does not replace that subject.

**Answer: have.** Check the difference from B2. **தமிழில்:** candidates எழுவாய்; அதனால் have.

**B4. Everyone ___ their own answer, and they ___ it independently. (write/writes; check/checks)**

1. Everyone takes singular **writes** in the first clause.
2. The second subject is **they**, so use **check**.

**Answer: writes; check.** Their/they can refer back without specifying gender. **தமிழில்:** இரண்டு clauses; இரண்டு எழுவாய்–வினை பொருத்தங்கள்.

**B5. None of the milk ___ left, but some of the bottles ___ empty. (is/are; is/are)**

1. Milk is uncountable; “not any milk” takes **is**.
2. Bottles are countable plural; some bottles **are** empty.

**Answer: is; are.** Trap: do not memorise “none/some always singular.” **தமிழில்:** milk அளவுப் பொருள்; bottles எண்ணக்கூடிய பன்மை.

## 3. 🔗 One of … and relative clauses

First separate **one of the students is** from **students who are**. A **relative clause** describes a noun using who/which/that. Its verb agrees with the noun that the pronoun represents.

- *Meena **is** one of the students who **work** here.* Main subject Meena; who refers to students.
- *Meena **is** the only student who **works** here.* Who refers to one student.
- *Meena is the only one of these students who **works** here.* The description singles out the only one.

**Only's position matters.** “She is **only one of** the students who work here” can mean she is merely one among several. It does **not automatically** force *works*. The PDF's “only one” shortcut needs this correction.

Who/which/that can be relative subjects. **Whom** is an object; do not decide a verb from whom automatically: *The students whom the teacher helps **are** ready* → teacher–helps; students–are. **Whose** shows possession: *the girl whose brothers **work*** → brothers controls work.

> **தமிழில்:** “one of …” என்று பார்த்தவுடன் எல்லா வினைகளையும் ஒருமையாக மாற்றாதீர்கள். Main clause-இல் one; relative clause-இல் who எந்தப் பெயரைக் குறிக்கிறது என்பதைப் பாருங்கள்.

### Group C — five worked models

**C1. One of the students ___ absent. (is / are)**

1. The subject head is **one**, selected from students.
2. There is only one finite verb to match.

**Answer: is.** Check: students stays plural after one of the. **தமிழில்:** மாணவர்களில் ஒருவர் வரவில்லை.

**C2. She is one of the players who ___ daily. (train / trains)**

1. Who describes the plural set **players** who train daily.
2. Choose **train**; she belongs to that set.

**Answer: train.** Trap: she controls *is*, not this relative verb. **தமிழில்:** தினமும் பயிற்சி செய்யும் வீரர்களில் அவளும் ஒருவர்.

**C3. She is the only player who ___ daily. (train / trains)**

1. Who now refers to singular **player**, specifically the only one.
2. Use third-person singular **trains**.

**Answer: trains.** Check the changed noun before who. **தமிழில்:** தினமும் பயிற்சி செய்யும் ஒரே வீரர்.

**C4. The books that the teacher ___ useful ___ expensive. (find/finds; is/are)**

1. Inside the relative clause, **the teacher finds** the books useful.
2. In the main clause, plural **books are** expensive.

**Answer: finds; are.** That is the object of finds here; it is not its subject. **தமிழில்:** ஆசிரியர் கருதுகிறார்; புத்தகங்கள் விலை உயர்ந்தவை.

**C5. “She is only one of the writers who work here.” Must work become works?**

1. *Only one of* can mean “merely one among the writers”; plural **work** is valid.
2. To mean the sole writer, say *She is the only writer who works here*.

**Answer: No automatic change.** Check meaning and placement of only. **தமிழில்:** only இருக்கும் இடம் பொருளை மாற்றும்; ஒரு சொல்லைப் பார்த்து விதி போடாதீர்கள்.

## 4. 🤝 Joined subjects: and, or and extra information

| Link | Agreement method | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| And: different people/things | Plural | Ravi and Meena **are** ready. |
| And: one person/combined idea | Singular when that meaning is clear | My friend and adviser **is** here. |
| Either…or; neither…nor; or/nor | Match the nearer coordinated subject in formal exercises | Neither the boys nor the girl **is** here. |
| Not only…but also | Usually match the nearer subject | Not only the girl but also the boys **are** here. |
| As well as / along with / with | Match the main subject, ignoring the addition | The girl, along with the boys, **is** here. |

The PDF's full list of additions is **as well as, with, along with, together with, besides, including, in addition to, accompanied by, unlike, rather than, except**. **Besides** = in addition to; **beside** = next to. These are not replacements for coordinating *and*.

**Repeated articles can signal two people:** *the secretary and treasurer is* usually one person holding two jobs; *the secretary and the treasurer are* usually two. But missing a second article does not prove one person: *his wife and mother were injured* normally means two people; *the principal and staff are waiting* also refers to several people.

**One combined idea:** *Slow and steady wins the race* is an established expression; *horse and carriage is a means of transport* treats the combination as one system. If the two items are discussed separately, plural agreement can be right.

**Excluded subject:** *The teachers, but not the principal, have agreed*. The positive subject teachers controls; the excluded principal does not. This differs from *not only…but also*, which includes both.

**Questions can reverse word order.** In *Is Ram or his friends present?*, the first coordinated subject is nearest the preceding verb, which explains the PDF's choice. Mixed-number coordination in questions can sound awkward. A clearer rewrite is *Is Ram present, or are his friends present?* “Always use the second subject” is not a safe universal rule.

> **தமிழில்:** “and” இருவரையும் சேர்க்கலாம். “along with” கூடுதல் தகவல் மட்டுமே. “either…or” என்றால் இணைக்கப்பட்ட இரண்டு எழுவாய்களில் வினைக்கு அருகிலுள்ளதைப் பாருங்கள்; வழியில் வரும் எந்தப் பெயரையும் அல்ல.

### Group D — five worked models

**D1. Ravi and his sister ___ ready. (is / are)**

1. Ravi and his sister are two distinct people joined by and.
2. The combined subject is plural.

**Answer: are.** Trap: do not match only the last singular noun. **தமிழில்:** இருவர் சேர்ந்து எழுவாய் → are.

**D2. My friend and adviser ___ arrived. (has / have), referring to one person.**

1. The question explicitly says the same person is both friend and adviser.
2. One person's two roles require singular agreement.

**Answer: has.** With two distinct people, *my friend and my adviser have*. **தமிழில்:** இரு பதவிகள்; ஒரே நபர்.

**D3. Neither the manager nor the workers ___ available. (is / are)**

1. Identify the coordinated subjects: manager / workers.
2. Workers is nearer the following verb and is plural.

**Answer: are.** Reverse the subjects → *Neither the workers nor the manager is available*. **தமிழில்:** வினைக்கு அருகிலுள்ள workers-க்கு are.

**D4. The manager, along with two assistants, ___ checking the files. (is / are)**

1. Along with introduces additional information, not an and-subject.
2. Singular manager controls the auxiliary.

**Answer: is.** Assistants is a distractor. **தமிழில்:** assistants கூடுதல் தகவல்; manager தலைமை எழுவாய்.

**D5. Not only the teachers but also the principal ___ agreed; the teachers, but not the principal, ___ signed. (has/have; has/have)**

1. First clause uses not only…but also: nearer principal → **has**.
2. Second clause excludes the principal: main subject teachers → **have**.

**Answer: has; have.** Trap: the two uses of but are different. **தமிழில்:** முதல் பகுதியில் இருவரும் சேர்க்கப்படுகிறார்கள்; இரண்டாவது பகுதியில் principal விலக்கப்படுகிறார்.

## 5. 🔢 Quantifiers, fractions and number expressions

**Quantifier — அளவு/எண்ணிக்கை காட்டும் சொல்:** some, most, many. First ask whether the expression refers to a mass, several items, or a count itself.

| Expression | Pattern | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Some/all/most/plenty of + mass | Singular | Most of the food **is** ready. |
| Some/all/most/plenty of + plural items | Plural | Most of the plates **are** ready. |
| Both, few, a few, the few, several, many | Plural when referring to countable items | Both books **require** care. |
| A number of / a large number of | Plural | A number of students **have** passed. |
| The number of | Singular count | The number of students **has** increased. |
| Many a/an + singular noun | Singular | Many a student **has** tried. |
| A great/good many + plural noun | Plural | A great many students **have** tried. |
| More than one + singular noun | Conventionally singular | More than one room **is** vacant. |
| More than two + plural noun | Plural | More than two rooms **are** vacant. |
| More plural nouns than one | Plural | More rooms than one **are** vacant. |

**Fractions and percentages:** *one-third/two-thirds of the pillar has rotted*; *one-third/two-thirds of the pillars have rotted*. The part refers to one whole object or several objects. Likewise, *90 percent of the students have passed*, but *the percentage of successful students is high*.

**Fraction spelling:** one-third; two-thirds; three-fourths. A numerator greater than one normally takes a plural denominator. That spelling does not by itself decide the verb.

**Majority and variety need meaning.** *A/the majority of voters support the change* focuses on people. *The majority is small* describes one numerical majority. *A variety of dishes were served* focuses on dishes; *the variety of dishes was impressive* focuses on diversity. Do not generalise that *the* makes every following expression singular.

**Corrections to pp.48–53:** use **many students**, **a great many students**, **a number of students**. Bare **a many students** is not the standard modern form. **The many** means the many people and normally takes a **plural** verb, not the singular rule printed in the PDF. [Oxford: many](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/many).

> **தமிழில்:** “A number of” = பலர்; “the number of” = அவர்களின் எண்ணிக்கை. பொருள் வேறுபடுவதால் வினையும் வேறுபடுகிறது. “All” என்பது “அனைத்தும்” அல்லது “அனைவரும்” எனப் பொருள்படும்; இரண்டுக்கும் ஒரே வினை விதி கிடையாது.

### Group E — five worked models

**E1. Some of the rice ___ cooked; some of the beans ___ uncooked. (is/are; is/are)**

1. Rice is a mass noun: **is**.
2. Beans is a plural count noun: **are**.

**Answer: is; are.** Check the noun after of for this quantifier construction. **தமிழில்:** அரிசி அளவாக; பீன்ஸ் பல பொருட்களாக.

**E3. Two-thirds of the wall ___ painted; two-thirds of the walls ___ painted. (is/are; is/are)**

**E5. “The many is ignored while the few prosper.” Correct it.**

1. The many means many people; it is not the phrase the number.
2. Use plural **are ignored**; *the few prosper* already agrees.

**Answer: The many are ignored while the few prosper.** This repairs a misleading PDF rule. **தமிழில்:** the வந்ததாலேயே ஒருமை ஆகாது.

## 6. 📏 Money, distance, duration and compound modifiers

When an amount is treated as **one total**, singular is common: *Five hundred rupees is enough; ten miles is a long distance; six years is a long time*. When separate objects are counted, plural applies: *Five coins are on the table*.

The PDF's claim “a plural noun preceded by a number takes a singular verb” is too broad. **Ten students are waiting** is correct. Meaning matters; a numeral is not a singular-verb switch.

Compare **a hundred-rupee note** and **a six-member committee**. Rupee/member is inside a compound modifier and stays singular; the head note/committee controls the verb. The PDF uses **six-man committee**; *six-member* is a neutral alternative with the same grammar.

With *five hundred rupees was/were distributed*, **was** is natural for one total sum. **Were** can reflect the treatment of separate currency units. The slide's plural choice is not the sole possible analysis. Prefer an unambiguous wording such as “A sum of five hundred rupees was distributed.”

> **தமிழில்:** “ஐந்நூறு ரூபாய் போதும்” என்றால் ஒரு மொத்தத் தொகை. “ஐந்து நாணயங்கள் உள்ளன” என்றால் தனித்தனி பொருட்கள். எண்ணைப் பார்த்து மட்டும் வினையைத் தேர்வு செய்யாதீர்கள்.

### Group F — five worked models

**F1. Ten kilometres ___ too far to walk today. (is / are)**

1. The sentence judges one total distance.
2. That measurement takes singular **is**.

**Answer: is.** Trap: kilometres looks plural, but distance is treated as one amount. **தமிழில்:** மொத்தத் தூரம் → is.

**F2. Ten runners ___ waiting near the gate. (is / are)**

1. Runners are ten separate people, not one measurement.
2. Use plural **are**.

**Answer: are.** Check: the numeral ten does not force singular. **தமிழில்:** பத்து நபர்கள் → are.

**F3. A five-rupee coin ___ on the floor. (is / are)**

1. Five-rupee describes the value of one coin.
2. Head coin is singular; use **is**.

**Answer: is.** Keep rupee singular inside five-rupee. **தமிழில்:** ஒரு நாணயம் தான் எழுவாய்.

**F4. Two five-rupee coins ___ missing. (is / are)**

1. The modifier remains five-rupee.
2. The head changes to plural coins, so choose **are**.

**Answer: are.** Check the head, not the value. **தமிழில்:** இப்போது இரண்டு நாணயங்கள்.

**F5. “Five hundred rupees were distributed.” Is were always the only correct form?**

1. Currency units may be viewed separately, which can support plural agreement.
2. A single sum can take singular: *Five hundred rupees was distributed*.

**Answer: No; the intended reading matters.** Clear rewrite: *A sum of five hundred rupees was distributed*. **தமிழில்:** தொகையாக எடுத்தால் was; மூலக் கேள்வியில் பொருள் தெளிவு தேவை.

## 7. 🧺 Noun forms: mass nouns, plural nouns and pairs

**Countable noun — எண்ணக்கூடிய பெயர்:** a chair, two chairs. **Uncountable/mass noun — தனி அலகுகளாக எண்ணாத பெயர்:** furniture. You can count pieces of furniture, but ordinary standard English does not say *three furnitures*.

### 7.1 The PDF's mass-noun list, repaired

These are normally mass nouns in the senses taught here: **furniture, scenery, luggage, advice, news, poetry, machinery, stationery, traffic, dirt, baggage, dust, information, work, evidence, equipment, bread, fuel, knowledge, pottery, crockery**. They normally take singular agreement: *The equipment is ready*.

**Business** is singular for activity/a business, but **businesses** can mean several companies. **Work** as effort is uncountable; *works* can mean artistic creations or a factory. *Fuels/breads/potteries* can occur for types or establishments; learn the ordinary meaning first rather than saying such forms can never exist.

The source also lists **ethics, athletics, classics, innings, gallows** here. They are not one uniform set of mass nouns. **Ethics/classics/athletics** can name fields; ethics can also mean moral principles. **Innings** is countable with unchanged singular/plural form: one innings **is**, two innings **are**. **Gallows** has specialised number usage; do not derive an “all -s words are uncountable” rule from it.

| Countable word | Related mass word | Correct way to count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chair / chairs | furniture | a piece / pieces of furniture |
| Scene / scenes | scenery | beautiful scenery; several views |
| Bag / bags | luggage, baggage | a piece / pieces of luggage |
| Poem / poems | poetry | two poems; a collection of poetry |
| Machine / machines | machinery | two machines; some machinery |
| Tool / tools | equipment | two tools; pieces of equipment |
| Slice / slices | bread | a slice / slices of bread |
| Item / items | advice, information, news | pieces of advice; items of information/news |

**Stationery** = writing materials; **stationary** = not moving. **Spectacle** can mean an impressive sight; **spectacles** meaning eyeglasses is a different use.

### 7.2 The PDF's plural list, separated by meaning

- **Pair-shaped objects:** scissors, tongs, spectacles, trousers, binoculars, pants, shorts → normally plural: *The scissors are sharp*. **A pair** of scissors **is**; **two pairs** **are**.
- **Plural people/animals/items:** cattle, police, gentry, clergy, vermin, goods, assets, savings, alms → usually plural in these uses. *The police are*; *a police officer is*. Say **five policemen/police officers**, not *five police* in this elementary counting pattern.
- **Bacteria** is the plural of bacterium; the singular bacterium takes is.
- **Wages** as earnings normally takes plural. The fixed expression *The wages of sin is death* has singular agreement; do not apply it to every result or salary sentence.
- **Poultry** as live birds can take plural; poultry as meat is mass and takes singular.
- **Artillery, cavalry, infantry** refer to weapons or military groups; usage depends on sense and variety of English. They are **not invariably plural**. *The artillery is positioned nearby* is valid for equipment/a unit.

The PDF's full list is retained above, but the heading “always plural” is corrected. Dictionary senses matter: [Collins: poultry](https://www.collinsdictionary.com/us/dictionary/english/poultry), [Cambridge: artillery](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/artillery), [Oxford: innings](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/innings).

## 8. 👥 Collective nouns: one unit or individual members?

**Collective noun — குழுப் பெயர்:** team, committee, jury. The PDF includes **jury, public, team, committee, government, audience, board, council, army, panel**. It also includes **fish**, but fish is better taught separately: one fish is; two fish are; fish as food is.

In British English, a collective noun can take singular agreement when the group is one unit, or plural when its members are in view. American English more often uses singular or explicitly says **team members**. Be consistent with the intended meaning and pronouns. The presence of *team* alone does not prove only one answer is possible. [Cambridge: group-noun agreement](https://dictionaryblog.cambridge.org/2017/05/10/we-agree-but-she-agrees-the-importance-of-subject-verb-agreement/).

**The jury was unanimous** treats it as one decision-making body. **The jury were divided in their opinions** focuses on individual members. *Unanimous* = ஒருமனதாக; *divided* = கருத்து வேறுபட்ட.

> **தமிழில்:** குழுவை ஒரே அமைப்பாகப் பார்க்கிறோமா, உறுப்பினர்களைத் தனித்தனியாகப் பார்க்கிறோமா என்பதைப் பாருங்கள். தெளிவில்லாத கேள்வியில் “இந்தச் சூழலில் இதுதான் ஒரே விதி” என்று மனப்பாடம் செய்யாதீர்கள்.

### Group H — five worked models

**H1. The committee ___ one official report as a single body. (has issued / have issued)**

1. “As a single body” explicitly tells us to view the committee as one unit.
2. Singular agreement fits that stated reading.

**Answer: has issued.** Check the meaning cue, not just the noun. **தமிழில்:** ஒரே அமைப்பாக அறிக்கை வெளியிட்டது.

**H2. The committee members ___ separate reports. (has written / have written)**

1. The head is plural **members**, not committee.
2. Each member wrote separately, so plural is unambiguous.

**Answer: have written.** Check: no collective-noun ambiguity remains. **தமிழில்:** உறுப்பினர்கள் தான் எழுவாய்.

**H3. In British usage: The jury ___ divided in their opinions. (was / were)**

1. “Their opinions” and “divided” focus on individual jurors.
2. Plural agreement matches this reading.

**Answer: were.** Clear alternative: *The jurors were divided*. **தமிழில்:** ஒவ்வொருவருக்கும் வேறு கருத்து.

**H4. The team ___ won the cup as one unit, and its captain ___ thanked everyone. (has/have; has/have)**

1. Team is explicitly treated as one unit with its → has.

**Answer: has; has.** Keep the unit reading and pronoun consistent. **தமிழில்:** அணி ஒரு அமைப்பு; கேப்டன் ஒருவர்.

## 9. 📚 Meaning matters: -s subjects, titles and classes of people

### 9.1 A final -s is not enough

**Mathematics, physics, economics, civics, statistics, politics** can name fields and take singular agreement. **Statistics** meaning numerical facts takes plural; **politics** meaning someone's political views can take plural. **Mathematics** meaning calculations can take plural in the usage taught by the PDF.

The PDF says a modifier makes these nouns plural. **Correction:** meaning controls; a modifier alone does not. *The physics of light is fascinating* still names a field. *His knowledge of mathematics is good* has head knowledge.

**Measles, mumps, rickets, AIDS** name conditions and take singular agreement. **Billiards, darts, draughts** as game names also take singular. The same word can have another sense: *The darts are sharp* refers to separate objects.

### 9.2 Names and “the + adjective”

- **Book titles** refer to one work: *The Arabian Nights is a collection*; *Gulliver's Travels is a book*. The source also names **The Three Musketeers, Great Expectations, Tom Jones**. Do not decide from a plural-looking word inside the title.
- **The Himalayas are**; **Mt. Everest is**. Use the number of the actual name, not a general claim that every mountain range is singular/plural.
- **The poor, the rich, the honest** refer to classes of people and take plural agreement. Do not generalise to every adjective: *the unknown is frightening* refers to an abstract idea.
- **The fruits of hard work are…** is ordinary plural agreement. The handwritten *The fruits of hardwork is success* is not a safe model. Rewrite **The fruit of hard work is success** or **The fruits of hard work are visible**.

> **தமிழில்:** சொல்லின் முடிவில் -s இருக்கிறதா என்பதைவிட அதன் பொருள் முக்கியம். Statistics ஒரு பாடமா, பல புள்ளிவிவரங்களா? Darts ஒரு விளையாட்டா, தனித்தனி அம்புகளா?

### Group I — five worked models

**I1. Physics ___ an interesting subject. (is / are)**

1. Physics names one field of study.
2. It takes singular agreement despite the final s.

**Answer: is.** Check: *the physics of sound is* also remains singular. **தமிழில்:** இயற்பியல் ஒரு பாடம்.

**I2. These statistics ___ a clear increase. (show / shows)**

1. These statistics refers to several numerical facts.
2. Plural subject → **show**.

**Answer: show.** Contrast: *Statistics is a subject*. **தமிழில்:** இங்கு புள்ளிவிவரங்கள் பல.

**I3. “Great Expectations” ___ on my shelf. (is / are)**

1. The quotation marks identify the title of one book.
2. One work takes singular agreement.

**Answer: is.** Expectations inside the title does not make the book plural. **தமிழில்:** தலைப்பில் பன்மைச் சொல் இருந்தாலும் ஒரு புத்தகம்.

**I4. The poor ___ support, and the government programme ___ assistance. (need/needs; provide/provides)**

1. The poor = poor people → need.

**Answer: need; provides.** Check each clause separately. **தமிழில்:** மக்கள் பலர்; திட்டம் ஒன்று.

**I5. Darts ___ popular here, but these darts ___ blunt. (is/are; is/are)**

## 10. 🔄 There, inversion, activities and it-clefts

**There is/are:** in formal written exercises, look after the verb: *There is a message; there are messages*. Introductory there does not mean “a singular person.” Colloquial *there's two people* occurs, but use **there are** in formal practice.

**Inversion — வழக்கமான சொல் வரிசை மாறுதல்:** *Outside the hotel are two shops* means *Two shops are outside the hotel*. The verb is before its subject, but agreement still applies.

**Activity as subject:** *Driving vehicles requires skill*. The whole activity **driving vehicles** is one subject; vehicles is its object. A single to-infinitive activity also commonly takes singular: *To read daily is useful*. Separate activities can be coordinated: *Reading and swimming are my hobbies*.

**It-cleft — ஒரு பகுதியை வலியுறுத்தும் அமைப்பு:** *It is the students who need help*. It controls **is**; plural students controls **need** in the relative clause. Not *It are the students*.

> **தமிழில்:** எழுவாய் எப்போதும் வினைக்கு முன்னால் மட்டும் வராது. “Outside the room are two chairs” என்பதில் two chairs தான் எழுவாய். “It is the boys who play” என்பதில் is மற்றும் play வெவ்வேறு எழுவாய்களுக்கு உரியவை.

### Group J — five worked models

**J1. There ___ one message for you. (is / are)**

1. The following noun phrase is one message.
2. Formal agreement requires singular is.

## 11. 💭 Unreal conditions: why “If I were” is different

For an unreal imagined present, formal English commonly uses **were** with I/he/she/it: *If I were a bird, I would fly; I wish she were here*. This is often called **irrealis/subjunctive were** — உண்மையல்லாத கற்பனை நிலைக்கான were.

It is **not** a rule that every verb after if/wish/as if/as though/suppose must be “plural.” Other verbs use appropriate tense/modal forms: *I wish I knew; I wish I had studied; if she comes, we will start*. Real past uncertainty can use **was**: *If he was at home yesterday, he heard the bell*.

Informal English also uses **was** for many unreal wishes. **Were** is the formal practice choice when a clearly unreal was/were question tests this contrast. Read the meaning instead of assuming all if-clauses are unreal. [British Council: wishes and hypotheses](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/wishes-hypotheses).

> **தமிழில்:** “நான் பறவையாக இருந்தால்…” என்பது கற்பனை; formal English-இல் were. “நேற்று அவர் வீட்டில் இருந்திருந்தால்…” என்பது கடந்த நிகழ்வைப் பற்றிய நிச்சயமின்மை; was வரலாம். if என்ற சொல் மட்டும் விடையை முடிவு செய்யாது.

### Group K — five worked models

**K1. If I ___ you, I would apologise. (was / were), formal unreal advice.**

1. I am not literally you; this is an imagined situation.
2. Formal unreal be uses were.

**Answer: were.** The subject I has not become plural. **தமிழில்:** உன் இடத்தில் நான் இருந்தால் என்ற கற்பனை.

**K2. I wish my sister ___ here now. (was / were), formal style.**

1. The wish implies she is not here now.
2. Formal unreal be permits were with singular sister.

**Answer: were.** Was is also found in informal usage; keep the style condition clear. **தமிழில்:** இப்போது இல்லாத நிலையை விரும்புதல்.

**K3. If she ___ free tomorrow, she will call. (is / are)**

1. This is a possible future condition, not “if I were” unreal advice.
2. Present clause uses ordinary singular she is.

**Answer: is.** Trap: if does not demand were. **தமிழில்:** நடக்கக்கூடிய நிபந்தனை; வழக்கமான பொருத்தம்.

**K4. I wish I ___ the answer yesterday. (knew / had known)**

1. Yesterday signals regret about a past situation.
2. Use past perfect for the earlier unreal knowledge.

**Answer: had known.** This is tense choice, not a “plural verb” question. **தமிழில்:** கடந்த கால வருத்தம் → had known.

**K5. “If he was at home yesterday, he heard the bell.” Must was become were?**

1. The speaker may be uncertain about a real past fact.
2. Was correctly describes that possible past situation.

**Answer: No automatic change.** Different meaning: *If he were at home now, he would hear the bell*. **தமிழில்:** உண்மையான கடந்த நிகழ்வு பற்றிய சந்தேகம், கற்பனைக்கு சமம் அல்ல.

## 12. 🎯 Turn the rules into an exam method

For an error question, write a tiny pair beside each finite verb: **prices–have; growth–is; countries–have**. First check grammar; then check whether a noun form, article, pronoun or preposition is also wrong. A damaged slide may contain **more than one error**, even if its question format expects one.

**Answer priority:** exact clause structure → head/antecedent → connector/quantifier pattern → person and number → tense/form → intended meaning. A remembered keyword shortcut comes after these checks.

Do not invent an error just because a question has four options. A coaching tick is not an official exam key. Where the PDF and accepted usage differ, the bank labels **PDF expectation**, **modern usage** or **source typo**. If you later obtain an actual exam question, compare its exact wording and final official answer key.

**Shortcuts after understanding:** mentally bracket ordinary of-phrases, but stop at fractions/quantifiers: *a box [of books] is* versus *half of the books are*. Never use “ignore everything after of” as a universal rule.

## 13. 🧠 Revision map and contrasts

> **🧠 Concept map: Four things to recognise**
>
> - **Controller:** head noun, pronoun or activity; each clause has its own.
> - **Construction:** and/or/additions; one of; a/the number; fractions.
> - **Noun meaning:** mass, plural, pair, collective, field, title or people.
> - **Verb form:** present -s, auxiliary, ordinary past, modal or unreal were.

| Compare | First answer | Second answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The boy / the boys | plays | play |
| Each of us / we each | has | have |
| Something / some things | is | are |
| Everyone / every one of the books | is | is — different meanings/spelling |
| A number of students / the number of students | have | has |
| Many students / many a student | are | is |
| More than one room / more rooms than one | is | are |
| Half the water / half the bottles | is | are |
| Scissors / a pair of scissors | are | is |
| Statistics as a field / statistics as figures | is | are |
| The poor / the unknown | are | is |
| It … the boys / the boys who … | is | play |

**Trap reminder:** I/you are not ordinary he/she/it forms; none is not always singular; the many is not the number; collective nouns depend on meaning; only one is not automatically the only one; if does not make every verb plural.

- “Most of the food is/are stale.” → **is**.

**p.45:**

- “Some books is/are needed.” → **are**.
- “Some money is/are needed.” → **is**.
- “Some of the richest (a) / business magnate (b) / live in Mumbai (c) / no error (d).” → **b, magnates**. The verb live already matches the intended people.

**p.46 — Rule IX:** all, few, several, both, many, various; handwriting adds **few, a few, the few**. All is not always plural; see the money example on p.47.

- “Both the books require/requires careful reading.” → **require**.
- “Few is/are ready to work.” → **are**, referring to people.

**p.47:**

- “Various departments was/were opened.” → **were**.
- “All the students agree/agrees to help him.” → **agree**.
- “All the money has/have been stolen.” → **has**. This contradicts the PDF's blanket all-is-plural wording; meaning decides.

**p.48 — Rule X(a):** many, majority, number, large numbers. These words cannot all be labelled always plural without examining the complete phrase.

- “Many student/students were/was invited to the party.” → **students; were**.
- “Majority of them agree/agrees to vote for him.” → **agree**, referring to people; normal edited wording: **A/The majority of them agree…**
- “The majority of students wish/wishes to attend the seminar.” → **wish** for the students-as-people reading.

**p.49 — Rule X(b):** source lists **a many, a number, a large number**. Correct the first to **a great/good many**, not bare a many in ordinary modern English.

- “A number of girls has/have passed.” → **have**.
- “A great many boys is/are sitting there.” → **are**.

**p.50 — Rule X(c):** the number takes singular, but the printed extension to **the many** is wrong.

- “The number of shops is/are increasing.” → **is**.
- “The number of students appearing in the exam has/have dropped.” → **has**.

**p.51 — Rule XI summary table:** many/number; a many/a number; many a; the many/the number; more than one; more than two; more persons than one. The repaired forms and agreements are in Section 5. Preserve the contrast **more persons than one are**, but reject “the many always singular” and bare “a many students.”

**p.52 handwriting:**

- “Many student/students was/were present.” → **students; were**.
- “A many student/students was/were present.” → repair **A great many students were present** or **Many students were present**.
- “A great many student/students was/were present.” → **students; were**.
- “Many a student/students was/were present.” → **student; was**.

**p.53 handwriting:** “Many was/were present.” → **were**; many stands for many people. “Many a person is” is correct; “many a people” is not the intended singular-person pattern.

**p.54:**

- “Many a girl/girls go/goes to college in time.” → **girl; goes**.
- “A good many girl/girls go/goes to college in time.” → **girls; go**.
- “Many a man have (a) / come to India form Canada (b) / to live here permanently (c) / no error (d).” → **a, has**. Source typo **form → from** in b also needs repair.
- “A great many student (a) / have been declared (b) / successful (c) / no error (d).” → **a, students**. The final purple s is a handwritten addition; **have** is correct for the plural result.

**p.55:**

- “More than one room/rooms is/are vacant.” → **room; is**.
- “More than two room/rooms is/are vacant.” → **rooms; are**.
- “More rooms/room than one is/are vacant.” → **rooms; are**.
- “The majority of the woman (1) / teachers are persuading (2) / the principle to consider their demands (3) / No Error (4).” → **1, women teachers**. Also correct **principle → principal** in part 3: a head of an institution, not a rule. The scan contains two errors.

**p.56 — Rule XII:** half of, one half of, two-thirds of, three-fourths of, a part of, a majority of, a variety of. Agreement follows the intended quantity/group reference, with meaning-sensitive majority/variety explained in Section 5.

- “Two-thirds of the pillar was/were ready.” → **was**.
- “Two-thirds of the pillars was/were ready.” → **were**.
- “A part of the work has/have been completed.” → **has**.

### Pages 57–67 — amounts and uncountable nouns

**p.57 — Rule XIII:** a quantity treated as one whole can take singular agreement. The slide's alternative wording “a plural noun preceded by a numeral takes a singular verb” is **not** a valid general rule; ten people **are** waiting.

- “Five-hundred rupees is/are a good amount.” → **is**, one sum. Normal spelling: **Five hundred rupees**.
- “Five-hundred rupees was/were distributed among the flood victims.” → the handwriting chooses **were**. **Was** is also possible when the money is viewed as one sum. Do not memorise “distributed always requires were.”

**p.58:**

- “Ten-miles is/are a long distance.” → **is**. Write **Ten miles**, without a hyphen in this noun phrase.
- “A hundred rupee note was/were lying on the road.” → **was**. Edited spelling: **a hundred-rupee note**.
- “A hundred rupees note was lying on the road.” → **rupees → rupee** inside the compound modifier.
- Handwriting: “A six men/man committee has/have been appointed.” → **a six-man committee has**; neutral alternative: **a six-member committee has**. The head committee is singular on the unit reading.

**p.59 — Rule XIV:**

- “It is not the faculty members but the president who decide/decides this issue.” → **decides**, the positive referent president.
- “The thief, and not his sons is/are responsible for the theft.” → **is**; punctuate the insertion: *The thief, and not his sons, is…*
- “The teachers but not the principal has/have decided to attend the seminar.” → **have**, teachers.

**p.60 — Rule XV:** the complete source list is **furniture, scenery, luggage, advice, news, poetry, machinery, stationery, traffic, dirt, baggage, dust, information, work, evidence, equipment, bread, fuel, business, ethics, athletics, classics, innings, gallows, knowledge, pottery, crockery**. Section 7 separates ordinary mass senses from countable/sense-dependent entries; the whole list should not be memorised as “all uncountable, always singular.”

“The luggage was/were lying at the station.” → **was**.

**p.61 handwriting:** furniture, not ordinary *furnitures*; chair/chairs; scene/scenes versus scenery, not ordinary *sceneries*; spectacle as a sight versus spectacles as eyeglasses; bag/bags versus luggage/baggage, not ordinary *luggages/baggages*.

**p.62 handwriting:** “Luggage is/are…” → **is**. “Pieces of luggage is/are…” → **are**. “A piece of luggage is/are…” → **is**. “A luggage” is not the ordinary standard count expression; use a bag or a piece of luggage.

**p.63 handwriting:** advice, not ordinary advices; information, not ordinary informations; poem/poems versus poetry; machine/machines versus machinery. **A lot of advice/information** is correct. Specialised meanings should not be confused with these everyday mass senses.

**p.64 handwriting:** tool/tools versus equipment; bread; **slices of bread**. The source crosses out equipments/breads for the ordinary intended meanings; **breads** can nevertheless mean different types of bread in an appropriate context.

**p.65:**

- “These days news is/are travelling faster.” → **is**, news.
- “The poetry of John Keats is/are very interesting.” → **is**, poetry.
- “Mathematics is/are a good subject.” → **is**, field of study.
- “The furniture of the room has/have been shifted.” → **has**, furniture.

**p.66:** “He has given advices” → **He has given advice**. “He has sold the furnitures” → **He has sold the furniture**. These corrections concern ordinary mass-noun forms, not a ban on every possible specialised plural sense of every listed noun.

**p.67:** “He has sold many pieces of the furniture” is grammatical when referring to specific furniture. “I have a lot of information to give you” is correct. Count the **pieces/items**, not *informations*.

### Pages 68–76 — plural forms, pairs and collective nouns

**p.70 handwriting:** wages is linked with an unfavourable result; fruits with a favourable result. “The fruits of hardwork is success” needs repair: **The fruit of hard work is success**, or **The fruits of hard work are visible**. **p.71 handwriting:**

- “The police is/are on alert.” → **are**.
- “The policeman is/are on alert.” → **is**.
- “The policemen is/are on alert.” → **are**.
- “I saw five policeman near the house.” → **five policemen / five police officers**.

**p.72:**

**p.73:**

- “A pair of binoculars has/have been sold.” → **has**.
- “Spectacles is/are available.” → **are**.

**p.74 — Rule XVII:** jury, public, team, committee, government, audience, board, council, army, fish, panel. Collective nouns can take unit/member readings; **fish** needs its own count/mass explanation rather than being treated as an ordinary committee-type collective.

“The team has/have tried the uniforms.” → source **have**, individual members trying uniforms in British usage. Clear wording: **The team members have tried on the uniforms**.

**p.75:** “The team has/have won the match.” → source **has**, team as a unit. The slide explains the member/unit contrast with p.74. British usage may also use plural team agreement; the context is a guide, not a claim that only one dialectal form can ever occur.

**p.76:** “The jury was unanimous in opinion” → singular unit reading. “The jury were divided in opinion” → individual-member reading. Both examples work in the intended British-style distinction.

### Pages 77–84 — fields, meanings and unreal wishes

**p.77 — Rule XVIII:** mathematics, politics, physics, economics, civics. **Correction:** a modifier does not automatically make these words plural. Check whether the word names a field, figures/calculations, or views.

- “Statistics is an interesting subject.” → correct, subject of study.
- “Statistics have indicated that the price of food grains is rising.” → correct, numerical facts. Price is the singular subject inside the that-clause.

**p.78 handwriting:** “Mathematics is/are an interesting subject.” → **is**. “The mathematics of the tax returns is/are available.” → source **are**, with mathematics meaning calculations. Use **The calculations for the tax returns are available** for the clearest wording; an *of*-phrase by itself does not force plural.

**p.79 handwriting:**

- “Politics is/are a dirty game.” → source **is**, political activity. This is the source's opinion used as a grammar sentence.
- “Politics is/are not my cup of tea.” → **is**, the activity/field; *not my cup of tea* means not something I like.
- “His politics is/are not good, so I have advised him to read Gandhi and Nehru.” → **are** for his political views. “Read Gandhi and Nehru” means read their writings.

**p.80 handwriting:** “His civics is/are good” is marked **are** for behaviour. **Do not learn that as a normal modern model:** civics ordinarily names a field and takes singular. To express the intended meaning, use **His civic sense is good** or **His civic behaviour is good**. “He is good at civics” is a normal sentence about the subject.

**p.81:** repeats the field/facts contrast for statistics and “Politics is/are a dirty game” → **is**. “Gandhiji's politics is/are good.” → **are**, political opinions/views in the source's intended meaning. The evaluative statement is example content, not a grammar rule.

**p.82:**

- “Mathematics is/are his favourite subject.” → **is**.
- “The mathematics of the tax-returns are/is flawless.” → source **are**, meaning calculations. Clear alternative: **The calculations in the tax returns are flawless**. Meaning, not merely the modifier, explains this usage.

**p.83 — Rule XIX:** measles, mumps, rickets; handwritten **AIDS**; billiards, darts, draughts. Conditions and game names can be singular despite ending in s.

- “Measles has/have side effects.” → **has** for agreement. Better medical wording would be “Measles can cause complications”; this chapter does not teach medical advice.
- “Billiards is/are an interesting game.” → **is**.

**p.84 — Rule XX:** I wish, as if, as though, suppose, if. The source's “always take plural verbs” explanation is wrong; formal **unreal were** is the relevant special construction.

- “If I was/were as doctor, I would help the poor.” → **were**, with **as doctor → a doctor**: *If I were a doctor, I would help the poor*.
- “He talks to me as if he was/were the Prime Minister.” → **were** for the formal unreal reading. Context may allow factual as-if comparisons elsewhere; as if is not an automatic plural-verb signal.

## 19. 🎯 10-Point Master SVA Quality Checklist

This diagnostic checklist tests whether you can spot and solve every major Subject–Verb Agreement pattern tested in SSC CGL/CHSL and RRB examinations:

| # | Diagnostic Standard | Key Formula / Rule | Exam Trap to Avoid | Status |
| :-: | :--- | :--- | :--- | :-: |
| **01** | **Head Noun Isolation** | Bracket all prepositional modifiers: `The box [of pencils] IS...` | Never match the verb to the nearest noun inside a prepositional phrase | [ ] |
| **02** | **Additive Phrase Invariance** | `as well as`, `along with`, `together with`, `accompanied by`, `in addition to`, `but not`, `rather than` → verb strictly matches the **First Subject** | Do not confuse additive phrases with compound `and` | [ ] |
| **03** | **Correlative Proximity** | `Either...or`, `Neither...nor`, `Not only...but also` → verb strictly matches the **Nearest Subject** | Do not make the verb plural automatically when one element is plural | [ ] |
| **04** | **Relative Clause Antecedent** | `One of the [Plural Noun] who [Plural Verb]` vs. `The ONLY one of the [Plural Noun] who [Singular Verb]` | Remember: `who/which/that` takes its number from the noun immediately preceding it | [ ] |
| **05** | **Distributive & Indefinite Pronouns** | `Each`, `Every`, `Either`, `Neither`, `One`, `Everyone`, `Someone`, `Nobody` → always **Singular** | `Each of the boys has` (not *have*); `Many a man was` (not *were*) | [ ] |
| **06** | **SANAM Variable Quantity** | **S**ome, **A**ll, **N**one, **A**ny, **M**ost → verb follows the noun in the `of`-phrase (Uncountable = Singular; Plural = Plural) | *None* can be singular or plural depending on context; formal SSC default prefers singular unless plural is forced | [ ] |
| **07** | **"A Number" vs. "The Number"** | `A number of [Plural]` = **Plural Verb** (means *many*); `The number of [Plural]` = **Singular Verb** (means *the figure/count*) | Never write `The number of students are...` | [ ] |
| **08** | **Uncountable Mass Nouns** | `Furniture`, `Advice`, `Information`, `Luggage`, `Baggage`, `Scenery`, `Machinery`, `Equipment`, `Poetry` → always **Singular** | Never add `-s` (no *furnitures*) and never use plural verbs | [ ] |
| **09** | **Pair Nouns vs. Single Unit** | `Scissors`, `Trousers`, `Spectacles`, `Pliers` → **Plural Verb**; `A pair of scissors` → **Singular Verb** | Look for the phrase `a pair of` which shifts the controlling head to *pair* | [ ] |
| **10** | **Collective Wholeness vs. Inverted Clauses** | Collective acting as 1 unit = **Singular**; divided/dissenting = **Plural**. Inverted sentences (`There is/are`, locatives) → verb matches the **post-verbal real subject** | `There are [a book and a pen]` vs. `There is [a book]`; `The jury was unanimous` vs. `The jury were divided` | [ ] |
