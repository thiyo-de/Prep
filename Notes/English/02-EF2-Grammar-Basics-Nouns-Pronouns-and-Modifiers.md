# 🏷️ PART 1: NOUNS & NUMBER (பெயர்ச்சொற்கள் & ஒருமை / பன்மை)

A **Noun** is a naming word (person, place, animal, thing, quality, or idea).

### 1. The 5 Classical Types of Nouns (மரபுவழி 5 வகைகள்)

```
                              NOUN CLASSIFICATION
                                       │
        ┌──────────────┬───────────────┼───────────────┬──────────────┐
        ▼              ▼               ▼               ▼              ▼
     PROPER         COMMON        COLLECTIVE        ABSTRACT       MATERIAL
   (சிறப்புப்)     (பொதுப்)         (குழுப்)         (பண்புப்)      (பொருட்பெயர்)
   Chennai, Ram   Boy, City      Team, Fleet      Honesty, Joy   Gold, Cotton
```

1. **Proper Noun (சிறப்புப் பெயர்):** Specific individual name (*Chennai, Suresh, India*). Always capitalized.
   * ⚠️ *Rule:* Never takes an article (*a/an*) unless used as a metaphor (*He is **a Newton** of our school* $\to$ Common noun usage!).
2. **Common Noun (பொதுப் பெயர்):** General name of a class (*city, boy, country, book*).
3. **Collective Noun (குழுப் பெயர்):** Name of a group considered as a single unit (*team, jury, herd, committee, fleet*).
   * ⚠️ *Rule:* Takes a singular verb when working unitedly; takes a plural verb when divided.
4. **Abstract Noun (பண்புப் பெயர்):** Feelings, concepts, or qualities you **cannot touch or see** (*honesty, childhood, bravery, poverty*).
   * Formed from Adjectives (*kind $\to$ kindness*), Verbs (*obey $\to$ obedience*), or Common Nouns (*child $\to$ childhood*).
5. **Material Noun (பொருட்பெயர்):** Raw matter or substance (*gold, iron, milk, wood, cotton*).

### 2. Modern Structural Classification: Countable vs. Uncountable

```
                            NOUN (MODERN ENGINE)
                                     │
                 ┌───────────────────┴───────────────────┐
                 ▼                                       ▼
          COUNTABLE NOUNS                         UNCOUNTABLE NOUNS
        (எண்ணக்கூடிய பெயர்கள்)                   (எண்ண முடியாத பெயர்கள்)
        • Individual units, items, ideas         • Masses, liquids, substances, qualities
        • Can take 'a/an' & plural '-s'          • Do NOT take 'a/an' or plural '-s'
        • Answers: "How many?"                   • Answers: "How much?"
        • Quantifiers: many, few, several        • Quantifiers: much, little, a little
```

> **💡 Understand: Meaning vs. Countability (Crucial Distinction)**  
> Do not confuse the *semantic category* of a noun with its *grammatical countability*. 
> - **Abstract nouns CAN be countable:** While some abstract qualities are uncountable (*honesty, patience, advice, information*), many abstract nouns are fully **countable**: *an idea / two ideas*, *a difficulty / several difficulties*, *a hope / many hopes*, *a suggestion / suggestions*, *a decision / decisions*.
> - **Material nouns can be partitioned:** *water* is uncountable, but *two glasses of water* is countable.
> - **Countability is a syntactic behavior**, not a rigid meaning box. [Cambridge: Countable and uncountable nouns](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/countable-and-uncountable-nouns)

### 3. Noun Cases (வேற்றுமை உருபுகள் — The Syntactic Roles of Nouns)
In SSC CGL Tier-II, understanding the **Case** of a noun tells you whether it commands the verb or receives the action:

| Case | Meaning & Role | Test / How to Find | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Nominative (Subjective)** | Acts as the **Subject** of the verb | Ask **Who?** or **What?** before the verb | ***Karthik** scored a century.* (Karthik = Nominative) |
| **2. Objective (Accusative)** | Acts as the **Direct Object** | Ask **Whom?** or **What?** after the verb | *The dog bit **the thief**.* (thief = Objective) |
| **3. Dative Case** | Acts as the **Indirect Object** | Ask **To whom?** or **For whom?** | *The teacher gave **Priya** a medal.* (Priya = Dative) |
| **4. Possessive (Genitive)** | Shows **ownership, origin, or relation** | Uses apostrophe **`'s`** or **`of`** | *This is **Ravi's** laptop.* (Ravi's = Possessive) |
| **5. Vocative (Address)** | Used to **call or address** someone directly | Set off by a comma | ***Suresh**, please listen to me.* (Suresh = Vocative) |

* ⚠️ **Noun in Apposition (அருகமை பெயர்ச்சொல் விதி):**
  When one noun follows another to describe or explain it, the second noun is in **Apposition** to the first and shares the exact same Case:
  * *Kabir (Nominative), **the great poet**, was loved by all.*
  * *They met Raman (Objective), **the chief auditor**.*

### 4. Noun Gender (பெயர்ச்சொல்லின் பாலினம் — Crucial SSC Pronoun Traps)

```
                            THE 4 NOUN GENDERS
                                     │
        ┌──────────────┬─────────────┴─────────────┬──────────────┐
        ▼              ▼                           ▼              ▼
    MASCULINE       FEMININE                    COMMON          NEUTER
     (ஆண்பால்)       (பெண்பால்)                 (பொதுப்பால்)    (ஒன்றன்பால்)
    Boy, King,     Girl, Queen,                Teacher, Baby,   Table, Book,
    Actor, Lion    Actress, Lioness            Doctor, Student  Ant, Infant
```

#### ⚠️ Exam Traps in Noun Gender & Pronoun Reference:

1. **Common Gender & Indefinite Pronoun Agreement (Singular "They" vs. Generic "His"):**
   * **Modern Standard English (Widely Accepted):** In standard contemporary English (Oxford, Cambridge, Merriam-Webster), singular **`they / their / them`** is completely standard when referring to a singular person of unspecified sex:
     * *Every student must submit **their** assignment.* (Standard, natural, gender-inclusive).
   * **Traditional Prescriptive Exam Tradition:** Older British grammar books and certain traditional competitive exam keys historically preferred generic masculine **`his`** (or *his or her*):
     * *Every student must submit **his** assignment.*
   * **Exam Strategy:** Recognize both. If an older exam question offers *his* as an option alongside grammatical errors (like pronoun case mismatches), understand that the examiner may follow traditional prescriptive style. However, singular *they* is standard English—as seen in question tags where *Everyone* takes *they*: *"Everyone was satisfied, **weren't they?***" (See EF3 Part 5). Never mark singular *their* as an illiterate error. [Cambridge: All or every](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/all-or-every)

2. **Neuter Gender for Lower Animals, Insects & Infants:**
   * Inanimate things, small creatures (*ants, flies*), and **human infants/babies whose sex is unknown or irrelevant** take the **Neuter Pronoun `It / Its`**, NEVER *he/she*:
   * ❌ *The baby was crying because he was hungry.*
   * ✅ *The baby was crying because **it** was hungry.*
   * ❌ *The ant dropped his food.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *The ant dropped **its** food.*

### 🔥 Top SSC Exam Trap #1: The Uncountable Noun Law

> **⚠️ Exam trap: The Uncountable Noun Law**  
> Uncountable Nouns can **NEVER take plural `-s` or `-es`**, and can **NEVER take `a` or `an`**!

SSC asks this in almost every shift:

| Incorrect in Exam (❌) | Correct English (✅) | Tamil Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Furnitures* | **Furniture** | மரச்சாமான்கள் |
| *Informations* | **Information** | தகவல்கள் |
| *Advices* | **Advice** | ஆலோசனைகள் |
| *Sceneries* | **Scenery** | இயற்கை காட்சிகள் |
| *Luggages / Baggages* | **Luggage / Baggage** | பயணப் பொதிகள் |
| *Poetries* | **Poetry** | கவிதைகள் |
| *Stationeries* | **Stationery** | எழுதுபொருட்கள் |
| *Hairs* | **Hair** (when referring to whole head) | தலைமுடி |
| *Works* (meaning labor) | **Work** | வேலை |
| *Breads* | **Bread** | ரொட்டி |

* **How to make them singular or plural?** Use measurement / counting words:
  * ❌ *He gave me an advice.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *He gave me **a piece of advice**.*
  * ❌ *I bought three furnitures.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *I bought **three items / pieces of furniture**.*

### 🔥 Top SSC Exam Trap #2: Deceptive Singular & Plural Nouns

#### Group A: Looks Plural (ends in `-s`), but takes a SINGULAR verb:
* **Academic Subjects / Sports / Diseases:** *Mathematics, Physics, Economics, Politics, News, Billiards, Measles, Rickets*.
  * ❌ *Mathematics are a tough subject.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *Mathematics **is** a tough subject.*
  * ❌ *The news are true.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *The news **is** true.*

#### Group B: Looks Singular (no `-s`), but takes a PLURAL verb:
* **Collective Groups:** *Cattle, People, Police, Gentry, Poultry, Clergy*.
  * ❌ *The cattle is grazing in the field.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *The cattle **are** grazing in the field.*
  * ❌ *The police has caught the criminal.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *The police **have** caught the criminal.*

#### Group C: Two-Part Items / Tools (Always PLURAL):
* *Scissors, Trousers, Spectacles, Binoculars, Tongs, Pliers, Pants*.
  * ❌ *Where is my spectacles?* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *Where **are** my spectacles?*
  * *(To make it singular, add "a pair of": A pair of scissors **is** on the table).*

### 🔥 Top SSC Exam Trap #2B: Nouns with Drastically Different Meanings in Singular vs. Plural
In Tier-II, SSC examiners love to test words that change their entire definition when an `-s` is added:

| Word (Singular) | Singular Meaning | Word (Plural) | Plural Meaning | Exam Context / Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wood** | மரம் / Timber | **Woods** | காடு / Forest | *He went for a walk in the **woods** (not wood).* |
| **Force** | பலம் / Physical strength | **Forces** | ராணுவம் / Armed personnel | *The government deployed security **forces**.* |
| **Good** | நன்மை / Benefit | **Goods** | சரக்குகள் / Merchandise | *The freight train carries **goods**.* |
| **Quarter** | கால் பங்கு / One-fourth ($1/4$) | **Quarters** | குடியிருப்புகள் / Lodgings | *The officer lives in government **quarters**.* |
| **Iron** | இரும்பு உலோகம் / Metal | **Irons** | விலங்குகள் / Chains or fetters | *The prisoner was bound in **irons**.* |
| **Pain** | வலி / Physical ache | **Pains** | சிரத்தை / Great care & effort | *He took great **pains** to complete the work.* |
| **Custom** | வழக்கம் / Social tradition | **Customs** | சுங்கவரி / Import duty tax | *He paid duty at the **customs** counter.* |
| **Spectacle** | காட்சி / Remarkable sight | **Spectacles** | மூக்குக் கண்ணாடி / Eyeglasses | *The sunset was a grand **spectacle**.* |
| **Abuse** | திட்டுதல் / Ill-treatment | **Abuses** | சமூக அவலங்கள் / Malpractices | *Social **abuses** must be eradicated.* |

### 🔥 Top SSC Exam Trap #2C: Plural of Compound Nouns (கூட்டுப் பெயர்ச்சொற்கள்)
To pluralize a compound noun, **always add `-s` to the Principal / Root word**, NEVER to the preposition or descriptive word:

| Singular Form | Incorrect Exam Trick (❌) | Correct Plural (✅) | Principal Word |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Father-in-law** | *Father-in-laws* | **Fathers-in-law** | Father |
| **Commander-in-chief** | *Commander-in-chiefs* | **Commanders-in-chief** | Commander |
| **Passer-by** | *Passer-bys* | **Passers-by** | Passer (வழிப்போக்கர்கள்) |
| **Looker-on** | *Looker-ons* | **Lookers-on** | Looker (வேடிக்கை பார்ப்பவர்கள்) |
| **Son-in-law** | *Son-in-laws* | **Sons-in-law** | Son |
| **Man-servant** | *Man-servants* | **Men-servants** | Both change to plural! |
| **Spoonful** | *Spoonsful* | **Spoonfuls** | Quantity suffix takes `-s` |

### 🔥 Top SSC Exam Trap #2D: Joint vs. Separate Possession Apostrophe Rules
* **Joint Possession (இருவருக்கும் பொதுவான உரிமை):** When two nouns jointly share/own one thing, put the apostrophe **ONLY on the second noun**:
  * ✅ *Ram and Shyam's father.* (Both are brothers $\to$ same father).
  * ✅ *Sita and Gita's joint account.* (One shared bank account).
* **Separate Possession (இருவருக்கும் தனித்தனி உரிமை):** When both nouns own separate things, put apostrophe on **BOTH nouns**:
  * ✅ *Ram's and Shyam's fathers.* (Two different fathers).
  * ✅ *Keats's and Shelley's poems.* (Two different poets writing separate poems).
* **Apostrophe with Indefinite Pronouns + "Else":**
  * When *else* follows *somebody, anybody, everyone*, put `'s` on **`else`**, not the pronoun:
  * ❌ *This is somebody's else book.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *This is **somebody else's** book.*

# 👤 PART 2: PRONOUNS & PRONOUN CASES (பிரதிப்பெயர்ச்சொற்கள்)

A **Pronoun** replaces a noun to avoid repeating names continuously.

### 1. Pronoun Case Matrix

| Person | Subject Case (செய்பவர்) | Object Case (பெறுபவர்) | Possessive (உரிமை) | Reflexive (தன்னைத்தானே) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **1st Person (பேசுபவர்)** | **I, We** | **me, us** | my / mine, our / ours | myself, ourselves |
| **2nd Person (கேட்பவர்)** | **You** | **you** | your / yours | yourself, yourselves |
| **3rd Person (மற்றவர்)** | **He, She, It, They** | **him, her, it, them** | his, her/hers, its, their | himself, herself, itself, themselves |

---

### 🔥 Top SSC Exam Trap #3: The 231 vs. 123 Etiquette Rule

When two or three personal pronouns appear together in a single sentence:

```
• Normal / Pleasant / Positive action ───►  Order: 2 - 3 - 1  (You, He, and I)
• Blame / Mistake / Confession of fault ───►  Order: 1 - 2 - 3  (I, You, and He)
```

1. **Normal / Good Actions (Rule 2-3-1: You $\to$ He $\to$ I):**
   * ❌ *I, you and he will complete the project.*
   * ✅ ***You, he and I** will complete the project.* (முதலில் நீங்கள், பிறகு அவன், இறுதியில் நான்).
2. **Mistake / Blame / Guilt (Rule 1-2-3: I $\to$ You $\to$ He):**
   * ❌ *You and I made the blunder.*
   * ✅ ***I and you** made the blunder.* (தவறை ஒப்புக்கொள்ளும்போது என்னை முதலில் நிறுத்த வேண்டும்).

---

### 🔥 Top SSC Exam Trap #4: Preposition + Object Pronoun Rule
Any pronoun placed **after a Preposition** must be in the **Object Form** (`me, him, her, us, them`), NEVER the Subject Form (`I, he, she, we, they`).

* ❌ *There is no disagreement between you and I.*
* ✅ *There is no disagreement between you and **me**.* ('Between' is a preposition $\to$ use 'me').
* ❌ *All students passed except he.*
* ✅ *All students passed except **him**.* ('Except' is a preposition $\to$ use 'him').

---

### 🔥 Top SSC Exam Trap #5: Relative Pronouns (Who vs. Whom)
* **Who (யார்):** Functions as the **Subject** (does the action). Replace mentally with *He/She*.
* **Whom (யாரை):** Functions as the **Object** (receives the action). Replace mentally with *Him/Her*.

* *Test:* *"The officer (who/whom) investigated the case received an award."*
  * Mental Check: *He* investigated? Or *Him* investigated? $\to$ **He** investigated!
  * Therefore: Use **Who** ✅ (*The officer **who** investigated...*).

---

### 🔥 Top SSC Exam Trap #6: Mandatory vs. Forbidden Reflexive Pronouns

#### Group A: Verbs that MANDATORILY require a Reflexive Pronoun (when no direct object is present):
> **📌 Remember: Verbs requiring Mandatory Reflexive Pronouns**  
> **avail, adapt, exert, reconcile, acquit, amuse, resign, avenge, absent**

* ❌ *He availed of the golden opportunity.*  
  *(Exam Error: 'avail' strictly demands a reflexive pronoun!)*
* ✅ *He **availed himself of** the golden opportunity.*
* ❌ *She absented from the office yesterday.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *She **absented herself from** the office.*

#### Group B: Verbs Commonly Used Without Reflexives in Standard English:
> **⚠️ Exam trap: Unnecessary Reflexive Pronoun Usage**  
> Verbs like **keep, stop, turn, qualify, bathe, move, rest** are typically used intransitively in standard English without a reflexive pronoun when the subject acts on themselves:

* ❌ *He kept himself away from bad company.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *He **kept away from** bad company.*
* ❌ *He qualified himself in the preliminary exam.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *He **qualified** in the preliminary exam.*

> **💡 The Nuance of "Hide":**  
> In school and exam notes, *hide* is often cited as an intransitive verb (*"She hid behind the curtain"*). However, *hide* can also take a reflexive object (*"She hid herself behind the curtain"*) when emphasizing deliberate physical concealment. Do not treat *hid herself* as an ungrammatical phrase. [Oxford Learner's Dictionary: hide](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.co.uk/definition/american_english/hide_1)

### 🔥 High-Yield Relative Clause Antecedent Rule
When a relative pronoun (*who, which, that*) connects clauses, the verb agrees with the **Antecedent (the noun right before it)**:

$$\mathbf{One\ of\ the\ +\ Plural\ Noun\ +\ who\ /\ which} \quad \longrightarrow \quad \mathbf{PLURAL\ VERB}$$
$$\mathbf{The\ ONLY\ one\ of\ the\ +\ Plural\ Noun\ +\ who\ /\ which} \quad \longrightarrow \quad \mathbf{SINGULAR\ VERB}$$

* *Case 1:* *"He is one of the players who **have** played brilliantly."*
  * Who played brilliantly? *The players* (Plural noun right before *who*) $\to$ Use **`have`**!
* *Case 2:* *"He is **the only one** of the players who **has** scored a century."*
  * Notice **"the only one"** $\to$ Focus shifts strictly to the single person $\to$ Use **`has`**!

### 🔥 Top SSC Exam Trap: The Mandatory "THAT" Rule (Replacing Who / Which)
In standard SSC grammar, you **must use "that"** instead of *who* or *which* after:
> **📌 Quick rule: Words that strictly require "THAT" (replacing Who / Which)**  
> **All, The same, The only, None, Nothing, Any, The little, The few, and Superlative Degrees**

* ❌ *All which glitters is not gold.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *All **that** glitters is not gold.*
* ❌ *This is the same man who deceived me.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *This is the same man **that** deceived me.*
* ❌ *He is the wisest man who ever lived.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *He is the wisest man **that** ever lived.* (Superlative degree!).

### 🔥 Reciprocal Pronouns: Each other vs. One another
* **Classification:** *Each other* and *one another* are **Reciprocal Pronouns** (பரஸ்பர பிரதிப்பெயர்ச்சொற்கள்), showing mutual action or relationship. (True *Distributive Pronouns* are *each, either, neither*).
* **Traditional Prescriptive Rule:**  
  * **Each other:** Recommended for **two** persons or entities (*The two brothers helped **each other***).
  * **One another:** Recommended for **more than two** persons or entities (*The five partners accused **one another***).
* **Modern Standard Usage:** Both Cambridge and the British Council observe that in contemporary standard English, *each other* and *one another* are frequently used interchangeably for two or more. Learn the traditional 2 vs. >2 distinction for competitive test questions, but recognize both as valid in broader English. [British Council: Reciprocal pronouns](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/reciprocal-pronouns)

# 🅰️ PART 3: ARTICLES IN DEPTH (சுட்டுச் சொற்கள் — A, AN, THE)

### 1. `A` vs. `An` (The Sound Law)
* Use **`An`** before **Vowel SOUNDS** (அ, ஆ, இ, ஈ, உ, எ, ஐ, ஒ...).
  * *an hour, an honest man, an heir, an MLA, an FIR, an umbrella*.
* Use **`A`** before **Consonant SOUNDS** (க, ச, ட, த, ப, ய, வ...).
  * *a university (யு), a European (யு), a union (யு), a one-rupee coin (வ)*.

---

### 2. When to Use "THE" (Definite Article)

| Category | Example | Note / Exception |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Unique Celestial Objects** | *The Sun, The Moon, The Equator* | Only one exists in nature. |
| **2. Mountain Ranges** | *The Himalayas, The Alps* | ⚠️ **Never use 'The' for single peaks** (❌ *The Mount Everest*; ✅ *Mount Everest*). |
| **3. Water Bodies** | *The Ganga, The Indian Ocean, The Red Sea* | Rivers, Seas, Oceans, Gulfs. |
| **4. Holy Books & Newspapers** | *The Gita, The Quran, The Hindu* | Renowned publications. |
| **5. Superlative Degree** | *The tallest boy, The best policy* | Always precedes superlative degree (*-est / most*). |
| **6. Musical Instruments** | *She plays **the** flute.* | When playing or referring to the instrument. |
| **7. Repeated Nouns** | *I saw a boy. **The** boy was crying.* | Becomes specific on second reference. |

---

### 3. Zero Article (Omission — எங்கு Article வரக்கூடாது?)
* ❌ Before **Languages:** *He speaks English* (NOT *the English*).
* ❌ Before **Sports:** *He plays cricket* (NOT *the cricket*).
* ❌ Before **Meals:** *We had dinner at 9 PM* (NOT *the dinner*).
* ⚠️ **The Primary Purpose Trap:**
  * When visiting *School, College, Hospital, Temple, Prison* for their **primary purpose**, do NOT use "the":
    * *Ravi went to **hospital**.* (அவன் நோயாளி — Patient).
    * *Ravi went to **the hospital** to visit a friend.* (பார்வையாளர் — Visitor).

---

### 4. Quantitative Determiners: Few vs. Little (எண்ணிக்கை vs. அளவு)

Determiners of quantity depend strictly on whether the modified noun is **Countable** or **Uncountable**:

| Determiner Group | Used With | Base Form (Negative Sense: Almost None) | "A" Form (Positive Sense: At least some) | "The" Form (Specific: All of the small quantity) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Countable Plural Nouns** | Friends, books, mistakes, students | **Few** (*அரிதாகவே சிலர் / கிட்டத்தட்ட இல்லை*) | **A few** (*சிலர் / குறைந்த எண்ணிக்கையில்*) | **The few** (*இருக்கின்ற அந்தச் சிலரும்*) |
| **Uncountable Singular Nouns** | Milk, water, money, hope, knowledge | **Little** (*கிட்டத்தட்ட இல்லை / துளியளவு*) | **A little** (*சிறிதளவு / போதுமான அளவு*) | **The little** (*இருக்கின்ற அந்தச் சிறிதளவும்*) |

#### Core Usage Examples & Exam Traps:
1. **Few (Negative sense = almost none):**
   * *He has **few** friends in the new city, so he feels lonely.* (நண்பர்களே இல்லை $\to$ negative).
2. **A few (Positive sense = at least some):**
   * *He has **a few** trustworthy colleagues who support him.* (சிலர் உள்ளனர் $\to$ positive).
3. **The few (All of the limited number):**
   * *He lost **the few** books he had borrowed from the library.* (இருந்த அந்தச் சில புத்தகங்களையும்).
4. **Little (Negative sense = almost none):**
   * *There is **little** hope of his recovery after the accident.* (நம்பிக்கையே இல்லை $\to$ negative).
5. **A little (Positive sense = at least some):**
   * *A little knowledge is a dangerous thing.* / *There is **a little** milk left for tea.* (சிறிதளவு உள்ளது).
6. **The little (All of the limited quantity):**
   * *He spent **the little** money he had earned.* (இருந்த அந்தச் சிறிதளவு பணத்தையும்).

> **📌 Remember: Question Tag Interaction with Few & Little**  
> Because **Few** and **Little** (without article *a*) carry a negative meaning, the sentence is grammatically negative $\to$ the Question Tag must be **POSITIVE**:
> * *Few students attended the lecture, **did they?*** (NOT *didn't they*).
> * *He showed little concern for his health, **did he?*** (NOT *didn't he*).

# 🎨 PART 4: ADJECTIVES VS. ADVERBS (விவரிக்கும் சொற்கள்)

| Feature | Adjective (பெயரடை) | Adverb (வினையடை) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **What does it describe?** | Describes a **Noun** or **Pronoun** | Describes a **Verb**, an **Adjective**, or another **Adverb** |
| **Answers the Question** | *What kind? Which one? How many?* | *How? When? Where? How often? How much?* |
| **Example 1** | *He is a **quick** runner.* | *He runs **quickly**.* |
| **Example 2** | *She spoke in a **polite** tone.* | *She spoke **politely**.* |

---

### Degrees of Comparison (ஒப்பீட்டு நிலைகள்)

```
Positive Degree (தனித்த நிலை)      ──►  Tall       Smart      Beautiful
Comparative Degree (இருவர் ஒப்பீடு) ──►  Taller     Smarter    More beautiful
Superlative Degree (அனைவரிலும்)    ──►  Tallest    Smartest   Most beautiful
```

* ⚠️ **No Double Comparatives / Superlatives:**
  * ❌ *He is more cleverer than his brother.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *He is **cleverer** than his brother.*
  * ❌ *This is the most cheapest option.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *This is the **cheapest** option.*

### 🔥 The Adverb Traps without `-ly`
* **Fast:** Functions as both an adjective and an adverb. **"Fastly" does NOT exist in English!**
  * ❌ *He drove very fastly.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *He drove very **fast**.*
* **Hard vs. Hardly:**
  * **Hard:** With great effort (*He worked **hard***).
  * **Hardly:** Almost not at all / negative word (*He **hardly** works* = அவன் வேலை செய்வதே இல்லை!).

---

### 🔥 Top SSC Exam Trap: Latin Comparative Adjectives (`-ior` முடிவுகள்)
Certain adjectives borrowed from Latin end in **`-ior`**. They strictly take the preposition **`to`**, NEVER *than*, and **never take *more* or *most***:
> **📌 Remember: Latin Comparatives strictly take "To" (Not "Than")**  
> **Superior, Inferior, Senior, Junior, Prior, Anterior, Posterior** (also the verb **Prefer / Preferable**)

* ❌ *He is more senior than me in service.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *He is **senior to** me in service.*
* ❌ *Coffee is more preferable than tea.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *Coffee is **preferable to** tea.* (No 'more', no 'than'!).

---

### 🔥 Top SSC Exam Trap: Non-Comparable Absolute Adjectives (முழுமையான பெயரடைகள்)
Words that denote an absolute state **cannot be compared or qualified with *more*, *most*, *very*, or *less***:
> **⚠️ Exam trap: Non-Comparable Absolute Adjectives (No Comparative/Superlative)**  
> **Unique, Perfect, Complete, Ideal, Universal, Dead, Supreme, Round, Circular, Eternal, Extreme**

* ❌ *This is the most unique architectural design.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *This is **a unique** architectural design.*
* ❌ *He achieved a more perfect score.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *He achieved **a perfect** score.*

---

### 🔥 Top SSC Exam Trap: Adverb Placement with "ENOUGH"
The word **`Enough`** changes its position depending on whether it modifies an adjective or a noun:
* **Adjective + Enough:** Placed **AFTER** the adjective (and requires **Positive Degree**):
  * ❌ *He is enough rich to buy the car.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *He is **rich enough** to buy the car.*
  * ❌ *He is wiser enough to understand.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *He is **wise enough** to understand.*
* **Enough + Noun:** Placed **BEFORE** the noun:
  * ✅ *He has **enough money** to buy the car.*

---

### 🔥 Top SSC Exam Trap: Negative Adverb Inversion (Tier-II Sentence Structure)
When a negative or restrictive adverb opens a sentence, it triggers **Subject-Verb Inversion** (Helping verb comes before the subject):
> **📌 Remember: Negative Adverbs triggering Inversion**  
> **Seldom, Rarely, Scarcely, Hardly, Barely, Neither, Nor, Little, Never**

* ❌ *Seldom we have seen such dedication.*
* ✅ *Seldom **have we seen** such dedication.* (Helping verb *have* inverts before *we*).
* ❌ *Little he knows about the conspiracy.*
* ✅ *Little **does he know** about the conspiracy.*

# 📍 PART 5: PREPOSITIONS (முன்னிடைச்சொற்கள்)

### 1. Prepositions of Time: AT vs. ON vs. IN
```
  AT (Exact time point) ──►  at 6:00 PM, at noon, at midnight
  ON (Days & Dates)     ──►  on Monday, on 15th August, on Diwali day
  IN (Broad durations)  ──►  in October, in 2027, in the morning, in winter
```

---

### 2. Crucial Preposition Pairs

| Pair | Rule | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Between vs. Among** | **Between:** for exactly **2** entities.<br>**Among:** for **more than 2** entities. | • *Divide the property **between** the two brothers.*<br>• *Distribute the books **among** all the students.* |
| **In vs. Into** | **In:** Already inside (static state).<br>**Into:** Motion toward inside (action). | • *He is sitting **in** the room.*<br>• *He jumped **into** the river.* |
| **On vs. Onto** | **On:** Resting on surface.<br>**Onto:** Movement landing onto surface. | • *The book is **on** the desk.*<br>• *The cat jumped **onto** the counter.* |
| **Since vs. For** | **Since:** Starting point in time.<br>**For:** Total duration of time. | • *I have lived here **since** 2018.*<br>• *I have lived here **for** 8 years.* |

---

### ⚠️ Preposition Redundancy (எங்கு Preposition வரக்கூடாது?)
Certain transitive verbs already contain the preposition's meaning:
* ❌ *He entered into the classroom.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *He **entered the classroom**.*
* ❌ *We discussed about the matter.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *We **discussed the matter**.*
* ❌ *She ordered for coffee.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *She **ordered coffee**.*
* ❌ *The army attacked on the fortress.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *The army **attacked the fortress**.*
* ❌ *He resembles to his father.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *He **resembles his father**.*

---

### 🔥 Top SSC Exam Trap: Tier-II Fixed Preposition Clashes (ஒரே வினைச்சொல் — மாறும் Preposition)

In Tier-II, questions test whether you pick the correct preposition based on the exact contextual meaning:

| Base Verb | Preposition 1 & Meaning | Preposition 2 & Meaning | Exam Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Die** | **Die of** $\to$ நேரடி நோய் (Disease) | **Die from** $\to$ பிற காரணம் (Cause / Overwork / Hunger / Wound) | • *He died **of** malaria (நோய்).*<br>• *He died **from** overwork / loss of blood.* |
| **Deal** | **Deal in** $\to$ வியாபாரம் செய்தல் (Trade / Business) | **Deal with** $\to$ ஒருவரோடு பழகுதல் / கையாளுதல் (Handle / Tackle) | • *He deals **in** garments (ஆடை வியாபாரம்).*<br>• *I know how to deal **with** dishonest people.* |
| **Agree** | **Agree with** $\to$ ஒரு மனிதருடன் உடன்படுதல் | **Agree to** $\to$ ஒரு திட்டம் / யோசனையை ஏற்றுக்கொள்வது | • *I agree **with** you.*<br>• *The committee agreed **to** the proposal.* |
| **Compare** | **Compare with** $\to$ சமமான இருவரை ஒப்பிடுவது | **Compare to** $\to$ உருவகமாக வேறுபட்ட ஒன்றை ஒப்பிடுவது | • *Compare Shakespeare **with** Milton.*<br>• *Life is compared **to** a stage (உருவகம்).* |
| **Blind** | **Blind in** $\to$ ஒரு கண்ணில் பார்வை இல்லாமை | **Blind to** $\to$ தன் பிள்ளையின் தவறுகளைக் காணாத நிலை | • *He is blind **in** the left eye.*<br>• *Parents should not be blind **to** children's faults.* |

# 🔗 PART 6: CONJUNCTIONS & FIXED PAIRS (இணைப்புச் சொற்கள்)

### Fixed Correlative Conjunction Pairs

> **📌 Remember: Fixed Correlative Conjunction Pairs**  
> If any of these fixed pairs are mismatched in an exam question, it is an **instant error**:

| Fixed Pair | Incorrect Exam Trick (❌) | Correct Exam Pattern (✅) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Either ... or** | *Either ... and / nor* | *Either accept the offer **or** resign.* |
| **Neither ... nor** | *Neither ... or* | *Neither Ram **nor** his friends came.* |
| **Not only ... but also** | *Not only ... and also* | *He is not only talented **but also** humble.* |
| **No sooner ... than** | *No sooner ... when / then* | *No sooner did he arrive **than** the rain began.* |
| **Hardly / Scarcely ... when** | *Hardly ... than / then* | *Hardly had I stepped out **when** it started raining.* |
| **Lest ... should** | *Lest ... will / may* | *Walk carefully **lest** you **should** fall.* |
| **Both ... and** | *Both ... as well as* | *Both Suresh **and** Ramesh attended.* |
| **Although / Though ... yet** | *Although ... but* | *Although he was poor, **yet** he was honest.* |

---

### 🔥 Top SSC Exam Trap: Correlative Conjunction Parallelism (சமச்சீர் இலக்கண விதி)
In SSC CGL Tier-II, *Not only ... but also* must connect **grammatically identical structures** (Noun with Noun, Verb with Verb, Preposition with Preposition):

* ❌ *He not only lost his luggage but also his passport.*  
  *(Analysis: 'Not only' is followed by a verb 'lost', but 'but also' is followed by a noun 'his passport' $\to$ Unbalanced!).*
* ✅ *He lost **not only his luggage but also his passport**.*  
  *(Analysis: Both are followed by nouns $\to$ Perfect symmetry!).*
* ❌ *She not only speaks English fluently but also French.*
* ✅ *She speaks **not only English but also French** fluently.*

---

### 🔥 Top SSC Exam Trap: Until vs. Unless (நேரம் vs. நிபந்தனை)
* **Until:** Refers to **Time (நேரம்)** $\to$ வரை / அதுவரை.
* **Unless:** Refers to **Condition (நிபந்தனை)** $\to$ செய்யாவிட்டால்.
* **Choose the intended meaning:** *until* marks an endpoint in time; *unless* means "except if". Neither creates a universal ban on negative clauses. For "wait for my return" and "work hard to avoid failure", use the positive clauses below:
  * ❌ *Wait here until I do not return.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *Wait here **until I return**.*
  * ❌ *Unless you do not work hard, you will fail.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *Unless you **work hard**, you will fail.*

* A negative endpoint is valid: *Wait until there is no traffic.* Read the meaning rather than deleting every negative. [Cambridge: until](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/until).

> **Stage 1 self-check:**
>
> 1. Spot the error: *"He is senior than me and more clever."*
> 2. Spot the error: *"He entered into the room and ordered for two cups of tea."*
> 3. Fill in: *"Hardly had the train arrived ______ the crowd surged forward."* (when / than)
>
> *Ready to proceed to drills when you can answer all 3 unaided in 30 seconds.*

# ⚡ PART 7: 10-SECOND RAPID RECALL & EXAM DRILLS

1. **Uncountable Rule:** *Furniture, advice, information, luggage, scenery* never take `-s` or `a/an`.
2. **Looks Plural, Verb Singular:** *Mathematics, Physics, News, Economics* take **Singular Verb**.
3. **Looks Singular, Verb Plural:** *Cattle, Police, People* take **Plural Verb**.
4. **Noun Shifts with `-s`:** *Wood* = timber, *Woods* = forest; *Force* = strength, *Forces* = military; *Quarter* = $1/4$, *Quarters* = houses.
5. **Compound Noun Plural:** Add `-s` to the Principal word (*Fathers-in-law*, *Commanders-in-chief*, *Passers-by*).
6. **Joint vs. Separate Possession:** Joint = *Ram and Shyam's father*; Separate = *Ram's and Shyam's fathers*.
7. **Pronoun Etiquette:** Positive = **2-3-1** (*You, he and I*); Blame/Fault = **1-2-3** (*I, you and he*).
8. **Antecedent Trap:** *"One of the boys who **have**..."* vs. *"The **only one** of the boys who **has**..."*.
9. **Mandatory That:** Use *that* instead of who/which after *all, the only, the same, none, nothing*, and superlatives.
10. **Preposition Pronoun Case:** After prepositions, use Object Pronouns (*Between you and **me***).
11. **Latin Adjectives:** *Senior, junior, superior, inferior, preferable* take **`to`** (never *than* or *more*).
12. **Absolute Adjectives:** No *more unique*, *most perfect*, or *very complete*.
13. **Enough Position:** Adjective + *enough* (*rich enough*), but *enough* + noun (*enough money*).
14. **Fixed Preposition Nuance:** Die **of** a disease; Die **from** a cause/wound; Deal **in** trade; Deal **with** people.
15. **Parallelism:** *Not only* and *but also* must sit right before matching parts of speech.

### 📝 Self-Check Practice (சுய பரிசோதனை வினாக்கள்)

1. **Spot the Error:**  
   * *"The commander-in-chiefs held a secret meeting in the government quarters."*
2. **Correct the Pronoun & Verb:**  
   * *"Rahul is one of those players who does not surrender easily."*
3. **Choose the correct Preposition:**  
   * *This brand is superior (than / to) that imported one.*
4. **Fix the Parallelism:**  
   * *"He not only visited Delhi but also Mumbai."*
5. **Resolve the Negative Trap:**  
   * *"Unless you do not run fast, you cannot catch the express train."*

---

#### 💡 Step-by-Step Solutions & Explanations (விடைகளும் விரிவான விளக்கங்களும்)

1. **Compound Noun Pluralization:**
   * ❌ *The commander-in-chiefs held a secret meeting...*
   * ✅ *The **commanders-in-chief** held a secret meeting in the government quarters.*
   * **Explanation:** In compound nouns formed with prepositions, pluralize the **principal/root noun** (*commander* $\to$ *commanders*), never the prepositional tail (*chiefs*). *Quarters* is correct for residential lodging.

2. **Relative Pronoun Antecedent Agreement:**
   * ❌ *Rahul is one of those players who does not surrender easily.*
   * ✅ *Rahul is one of those players who **do not** surrender easily.*
   * **Explanation:** The relative pronoun *who* refers back to its plural antecedent **players** (not Rahul). Since *players* is plural, the relative clause requires the plural verb **do not** (not *does not*). Contrast with *"Rahul is the ONLY one of those players who does..."*.

3. **Latin Adjective Comparative:**
   * ✅ *This brand is superior **to** that imported one.*
   * **Explanation:** Latin comparatives ending in `-ior` (*superior, inferior, senior, junior, prior*) strictly govern the preposition **to**, never *than*, and never take *more*.

4. **Parallelism with Correlative Conjunctions:**
   * ❌ *He not only visited Delhi but also Mumbai.*
   * ✅ *He visited **not only Delhi but also Mumbai**.*
   * **Explanation:** *Not only ... but also* must balance identical grammatical parts of speech. Placing *visited* before *not only* balances the proper noun *Delhi* with the proper noun *Mumbai*.

5. **Conditional Negative Redundancy (Unless):**
   * ❌ *Unless you do not run fast, you cannot catch the express train.*
   * ✅ *Unless you **run fast**, you cannot catch the express train.*
   * **Explanation:** *Unless* inherently means "except if / if ... not". Adding *do not* within the *unless*-clause creates an erroneous double negative.
