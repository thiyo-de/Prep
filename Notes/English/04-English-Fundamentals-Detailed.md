## 1. Words, Groups and Sentence Anatomy | சொற்களும் வாக்கிய உறுப்புகளும்

The title on page 1 of the source PDF is **Fundamentals of English Grammar**. Page 2 lists the 7 core building blocks: **Clause, Phrase, Sentence, Subject, Object, Subject complement, Object complement**.

```
                           THE SENTENCE TEAM
                                   │
        ┌──────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────┐
        ▼                                                     ▼
   WORD CLASS (சொல் வகை)                             SENTENCE FUNCTION (வாக்கியப் பணி)
   • What a word IS by nature                        • What job the word DOES in this sentence
   • e.g., Noun, Verb, Adjective                     • e.g., Subject, Direct Object, Complement
```

> [!NOTE]
> **Key Principle:** A word's **class** describes what kind of word it is; its **function** describes its job in that particular sentence. The same person can do different jobs, and the same word can have different grammatical functions.

### 1.1 PDF Page 3: Complete Word-by-Word Analysis

**PDF example:** *“I gave a book to her.”*

| Word | Word class | Job / grammatical function | Tamil Meaning & Reason |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---|
| **I** | Pronoun | Subject (S) | நான் — செயலைச் செய்பவர் |
| **gave** | Verb (Finite) | Verb (V) | கொடுத்தேன் — கடந்த கால முதன்மை வினை |
| **a** | Article / Determiner | Modifier of head noun | ஒரு — எண்ணக்கூடிய பெயர்ச்சொல் குறிப்பான் |
| **book** | Noun | Head of Direct Object (DO) | புத்தகம் — செயப்படுபொருள் |
| **to** | Preposition | Head of Prepositional Phrase | -க்கு / இடம் நோக்கிய இடைச்சொல் |
| **her** | Pronoun | Object/Complement of Preposition | அவளுக்கு — இடைச்சொல்லின் செயப்படுபொருள் |

#### Analytical Steps:
1. **Find the verb:** **gave** (Past tense of *give*).
2. **Who gave?** **I** $\to$ Subject.
3. **Gave what?** **a book** $\to$ Direct Object.
4. **To whom?** **to her** $\to$ Prepositional phrase expressing recipient.

> [!TIP]
> **Added Distinction:** Compare **I gave her a book**: **her** is the indirect object and **a book** the direct object in that double-object construction. In **to her**, modern clause analysis normally treats **her** as the preposition's object, although some school explanations call the recipient an indirect object in both patterns.

<!-- separate learning boxes -->

> **தமிழில்:** “I” என்பது pronoun என்ற சொல் வகையைச் சேர்ந்தது; இவ்வாக்கியத்தில் subject என்ற வேலையைச் செய்கிறது. சொல் வகையும் வாக்கியப் பணியும் ஒன்றல்ல.

### 2.1 What the Source Comparison Means vs. What Needs Correction

Page 4 gives the following shorthand; pages 5–7 expand it:

| PDF Concept | PDF Source Claim | Correct Standard Grammatical Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Phrase** | *“No sub verb combination”; “No meaning”* | A phrase does not have the finite subject-predicate structure of a clause. **It does have meaning.** (e.g., *“at home”* clearly expresses location). Verb phrases also contain verbs. |
| **Clause** | *“Sub verb combination”; “Meaningful / no meaning”* | A clause contains a subject and verb group. **A dependent clause has meaning**, but cannot stand alone as an independent sentence. |
| **Sentence** | *“Sub verb combination”; “Meaningful sense”* | A full declarative sentence contains at least one main clause with an expressed or understood subject (*“Go!”*). |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Source Definitions & Corrections:**
> - **PDF Page 5 Definition:** *“It is a group of words with no subject-verb combination. It doesn’t give a meaningful sense.”*  
>   **Correction:** Replace the final claim with **“It does not express a complete independent statement on its own.”** Never memorize “phrase = meaningless.” *“Will come”* is a verb phrase; *“under the tree”* is a location phrase.
> - **PDF Page 6 Definition:** *“It has a subject-verb combination. It may or may not give a meaningful sense.”*  
>   **Correction:** Both independent and dependent clauses have meaning! The distinction is **grammatical independence**, not absence of meaning.
> - **PDF Page 7 Definition:** *“It has a subject-verb combination. It always gives meaningful sense.”*  
>   **Standard Rule:** A complete clause-based sentence contains an independent main clause.

<!-- separate learning boxes -->

> **தமிழில்:** “அவன் வந்தால்” என்பதற்குப் பொருள் உள்ளது. ஆனால் என்ன நடக்கும் என்பதைச் சொல்ல இன்னொரு கூறு தேவை. எனவே சார்புக் கூறை (Dependent clause) “பொருளே இல்லாதது” என்று தவறாகப் படிக்க வேண்டாம்.

### 2.2 All Page 4 Introductory Examples

| PDF Source Example | Grammatical Breakdown & Exam Trap Warning |
| :--- | :--- |
| *“At home, at school”* | Two prepositional phrases: **at** + noun. Meaning: *வீட்டில், பள்ளியில்*. Zero finite verbs; acts as location adverbials. |
| *“He said that he would come.”* | Main clause (*He said*) embedding the subordinate content clause (*that he would come*). The inner clause has Subject (*he*) + Modal Verb Group (*would come*). |
| *“If he will comes, I will go.”* | **Exam Trap:** The PDF crosses out *will*. In ordinary future conditions, the condition clause takes the simple present: **“If he comes, I will go.”** Furthermore, modal *will* cannot take a third-person *-s* verb (*comes* is grammatically impossible). |
| *“I will meet you at home or at office.”* | **Or** coordinates two location phrases (*at home*, *at the office*). Standard edited English requires the article: **at the office**. |

> [!CAUTION]
> **⚠️ Critical Exam Trap (நிபந்தனை வாக்கியப் பொறி):**  
> Do not memorize the blanket shortcut *“Never use will after if”*. While **will** is incorrect in an ordinary future condition (*“If he comes…”*), **if** can introduce an indirect question (*“I wonder if he will come”*) or express polite willingness (*“If you will kindly wait…”*). Know the construction!

### 2.4 Five Worked Models: Independent and Dependent Clauses | வாக்கியக் கூறுகள்

| Model ID | Target Sentence | Clause Division & Analysis | Clause Classification |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **C1**<br>*(Pg 6)* | *“If he comes, I will go.”* | • **If he comes:** Has Subject (*he*) + Verb (*comes*). | **C4**<br>*(Added)* | *“Because it rained, she stayed.”* | • **Because it rained:** Introductory dependent clause (DC) marked with a comma.<br>• **she stayed:** Main independent statement (IC). | **DC** followed by **IC**.<br><br>> [!TIP]<br>> Position in the sentence does not alter clause dependence. |
| **C5**<br>*(Trap)* | *“Because it rained, although she had an umbrella.”* | • *Because it rained:* Dependent clause 1 (Reason).<br>• *although she had an umbrella:* Dependent clause 2 (Concession).<br>• Neither clause is independent! | ❌ **Fragment (Incomplete Sentence)**.<br>Needs a main clause (e.g. *“she got drenched”*). |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Page 6 Handwritten Annotation Decoded:**  
> The source notebook uses **DC / IDC**, **S / V**, tick marks for $\text{IC} + \text{DC}$, and a prominent cross beside **DC + DC**.  
> **Key Exam Rule:** A standalone sentence **must contain at least one Independent Clause (IC)**. However, two dependent clauses *can* be coordinated inside a larger sentence that possesses a main clause (*“I know [that she left] and [that he stayed]”*).

## 5. Subject, Object and Complements — Page 13 | எழுவாய், செயப்படுபொருள், மற்றும் நிரப்பிகள்

Page 13 establishes the 4 foundational argument roles that noun phrases occupy in English syntax: **Subject, Direct Object, Subject Complement, Object Complement**, illustrated with the **S-V-O-C Engine**.

```
                   THE S-V-O-C GRAMMATICAL ENGINE
                                 │
     ┌───────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┐
     ▼                           ▼                           ▼
  1. TRANSITIVE PATTERN       2. LINKING (COPULAR)        3. COMPLEX TRANSITIVE
     Subject + Verb + Object     Subject + Verb + Sub Comp   Subject + Verb + Obj + Obj Comp
     [Ram] [rang] [the bell]     [Ram] [became] [a writer]   [Circumstances] [made] [them] [enemies]
     (O is acted upon; S ≠ O)    (S = Complement identity)   (O = Complement identity)
```

### 5.1 The Four Core Sentence Roles

| Syntactic Role | Diagnostic Recognition Question | Structural Nature & Relationship | Added Exemplar |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Subject (S)** | Who/what performs the action or is described? | The anchor of the clause | **The child** laughed. |
| **Direct Object (O)** | Transitive Verb + whom/what? | The entity acted upon ($S \neq O$) | She opened **the door**. |
| **Subject Complement (SC)** | Who/what is the Subject renamed/described as? | Identity or attribute of Subject ($S = SC$) | She became **a doctor**. |
| **Object Complement (OC)** | Who/what is the Object renamed/described as? | Identity or attribute of Object ($O = OC$) | They named her **captain**. |

> **Double Object vs. Object Complement (Double-Check Test):**  
> • **Double Object ($V + IO + DO$):** *“She gave Ravi a prize.”* $\to$ *Ravi* $\neq$ *a prize*. Ravi is the recipient; the prize is the thing given.

### 9.2 The Handwritten -ly Shorthand & Crucial Exam Traps

The handwritten margin on page 19 notes shorthand regarding **-ly** endings.

> [!CAUTION]
> **The -ly Ending Trap (வினையடைப் பொறி):**  
> 1. **Not all -ly words are Adverbs:** Noun + *-ly* forms an **Adjective**!  
>    • *friend + ly* = **friendly** (Adjective: *“a friendly person”*)  
>    • *love + ly* = **lovely** (Adjective: *“a lovely day”*)  
>    • *brother + ly* = **brotherly** (Adjective: *“brotherly affection”*)  
> 2. **Adverbs without -ly:** Many common adverbs have no *-ly*: **fast, hard, late, well**.  
> 3. **The Hard vs. Hardly Semantic Trap:**  
>    • *“He works **hard**.”* = He exerts great effort (கடினமாக உழைக்கிறான் — Adverb).  
>    • *“He **hardly** works.”* = He does almost zero work (கிட்டத்தட்ட வேலையே செய்வதில்லை — Negative frequency adverb).  
> 4. **No Such Word as "Fastly":** *“Fastly”* ❌ does not exist in standard English. Always use **fast** ✅ for both adjective and adverb. [British Council: adverbials of manner](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/adverbials-manner)

<!-- separate learning boxes -->

> **தமிழில்:** -ly இல் முடியும் சொற்கள் எல்லாம் வினையடைகள் (Adverbs) அல்ல. Friendly, Lovely போன்றவை பெயரடைகள் (Adjectives). Fastly என்ற சொல் ஆங்கிலத்தில் இல்லை; வேகமாக என்பதற்கு “fast” என்றே கூற வேண்டும்.

## 11. Conjunctions: Joining Words, Phrases and Clauses — Page 21 | இணைப்புச் சொற்கள்

Page 21 designates conjunctions as **“Joining Words”**, stating that they join **words, phrases, clauses and sentences**.

```
                    CONJUNCTION CLASSIFICATION ARCHITECTURE
                                       │
        ┌──────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┐
        ▼                                                             ▼
   1. COORDINATING CONJUNCTIONS                               2. SUBORDINATING CONJUNCTIONS
   Connect grammatically equal elements                       Connect a dependent clause to a main clause
   • FANBOYS (For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So)                • because, if, although, since, that, unless
   • e.g. "Ram [and] Mohan" (Words)                           • e.g. "He said [that] he was busy."
   • e.g. "at home [or] at the office" (Phrases)              • e.g. "Stay [because] it rained."
   • e.g. "She revised, [but] he slept." (Clauses)
```

### 11.1 Five Worked Models (CO1 to CO5)

| Model ID | Source Sentence | Conjunction | Units Joined | Resulting Sentence Structure |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **CO1** | *“Ram and Mohan will complete the work.”* | **and** | Two Proper Noun Subjects (*Ram* + *Mohan*) | Simple Sentence with Coordinated Compound Subject |
| **CO2** | *“I will meet you at office or at home.”* | **or** | Two Prepositional Phrases (*at office* + *at home*) | Simple Sentence (Standard: *at the office or at home*) |
| **CO3** | *“He said that he was busy.”* | **that** | Connects main clause (*He said*) to content clause (*he was busy*) | Complex Sentence ($\text{IC} + \text{DC}$) |
| **CO4** | *“She revised, but he slept.”* | **but** | Two Independent Clauses (*She revised* + *he slept*) | Compound Sentence ($\text{IC} + \text{IC}$) |
| **CO5** | *“I know that she left and that he stayed.”* | **and** | Coordinates two Dependent Clauses (*that she left* + *that he stayed*) | Complex Sentence with Coordinated Subordinate Clauses |

> [!TIP]
> **Coordinated Dependent Clauses:**  
> Model **CO5** proves why the source's handwritten cross beside **DC + DC** on page 6 is not an absolute rule. Two dependent clauses *can* be joined by *and*, provided they are anchored to a governing main clause (*“I know...”*).

## 13. Same Word: Conjunction or Preposition? — Page 24 | சொல் மாற்றம்: இடைச்சொல்லா அல்லது இணைப்புச் சொல்லா?

| Word | As a Preposition (Word + Noun Phrase) | As a Conjunction (Word + Clause) | Diagnostic Rule & Tamil Meaning |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **after** | **CP1:** *“The dog ran **after** the cat.”*<br>• Followed by noun phrase *the cat*.<br>• Meaning: pursuit (பூனையைத் துரத்தி).<br>• Classification: **Preposition**. | **CP2:** *“He came **after** I had left.”*<br>• Followed by clause *I had left* (S + V).<br>• Meaning: time sequence (நான் சென்ற பிறகு).<br>• Classification: **Subordinating Conjunction**. | Preposition takes a noun/pronoun complement; Conjunction introduces a clause with its own finite verb. |

## 14. Preposition, Adverb or Particle? — Page 25 | முன்னிடைச் சொல், வினையடை அல்லது வினையடித் துகள்?

Page 25 addresses the critical distinction between a **Preposition**, an **Adverb**, and a **Phrasal Verb Particle**.

```
                      THE PARTICLE SEPARATION TEST
                                    │
        ┌───────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────┐
        ▼                                                       ▼
    PHRASAL VERB (Separable Particle)                       PREPOSITION (Inseparable Head)
    • He [switched off] [the fans]                          • The boat sailed [down the river]
    • He [switched] [the fans] [off]   ✅                   • *The boat sailed the river down  ❌
    • He [switched] [them] [off]       ✅                   • *The boat sailed it down         ❌
    ==> "off" is a PARTICLE, NOT a preposition!            ==> "down" is a PREPOSITION!
```

### 14.1 Master Diagnostic Breakdown (PA1 to PA6)

| Model ID | Source Sentence | Target Word | Grammatical Classification | Analytical Proof & Syntactic Behavior |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **PA1** | *“The students are sitting in the room.”* | **in** | **Preposition** | Followed directly by its noun-phrase complement *the room*. Together they form a prepositional phrase of location. |
| **PA2** | *“He came in and took some rest.”* | **in** | **Adverb / Particle** | Modifies *came* with no following noun complement. Expresses inward direction/location. *Some rest* is the direct object of *took*, not *in*. |
| **PA3** | *“The boat sailed down the river.”* | **down** | **Preposition** | Governs *the river*. Moving *down* to the end (*“sailed the river down”*) is unnatural/ungrammatical. |
| **PA4** | *“He sat down and talked to me.”* | **down** | **Adverb / Particle** | Completes the verb *sat* without any object complement. (Note: *to* in *talked to me* is a preposition governing *me*). |
| **PA5** | *“The plane took off.”* | **off** | **Particle** | Combines with *took* to form the intransitive phrasal verb *take off* (விமானம் புறப்பட்டது). Has no object. |
| **PA6** | *“He switched off the fans.”* | **off** | **Particle (Phrasal Verb)** | Forms the transitive separable phrasal verb *switch off*. Passing the Particle Separation Test confirms its status:<br>• *He switched the fans off* ✅<br>• *He switched them off* ✅ |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Source Annotation Correction (Page 25):**  
> In the source PDF, the handwritten margin marks **off** in *“switched off the fans”* as a **Preposition (Prep)** because it is immediately followed by a noun phrase (*the fans*).  
> **This is an error in school annotation!** *The fans* is the direct object of the two-word transitive phrasal verb **switch off**, NOT the prepositional object of *off*. When the object is a pronoun, English grammar **requires** separation: *“He switched them off”* ✅, never *“He switched off them”* ❌! [Cambridge: switch off](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/switch-off)

<!-- separate learning boxes -->

> **தமிழில்:** “Switch off the fans” என்பதில் fans என்பது switch off என்ற கூட்டு வினையின் செயப்படுபொருள் (Direct object). அதை “switch them off” என்று மாற்ற முடியும். ஆகவே off என்பதை preposition என அழைக்கக் கூடாது; அது phrasal verb-ன் particle (துகள்) ஆகும்.
