# ⚡ PART 1: VERBS & 4 VERB FORMS (வினைச்சொற்கள் & வடிவங்கள்)

The **Verb** is the heartbeat of a sentence. Without a finite verb, no sentence can exist.

### 1. The 4 Essential Verb Forms
Every English verb operates across 4 core forms that you must recognize on sight:

| Base / Present (V1) | Simple Past (V2) | Past Participle (V3) | Continuous / Participle (V4) | Tamil Meaning |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Go** | **Went** | **Gone** | **Going** | போ |
| **Write** | **Wrote** | **Written** | **Writing** | எழுது |
| **Do** | **Did** | **Done** | **Doing** | செய் |
| **See** | **Saw** | **Seen** | **Seeing** | பார் |
| **Eat** | **Ate** | **Eaten** | **Eating** | சாப்பிடு |
| **Sing** | **Sang** | **Sung** | **Singing** | பாடு |

> [!WARNING]
> **The V3 Golden Law:**
> **V3 can NEVER stand alone as the main verb of a sentence!** It strictly requires a helping verb (*has, have, had, is, was, been*):
> * ❌ *He written a letter yesterday.*
> * ✅ *He **wrote** a letter yesterday.* (Using V2 for simple past)
> * ✅ *He **has written** a letter.* (Using Helping Verb + V3)

### 2. Helping Verbs & Modals

```
                       HELPING VERBS (துணை வினைகள்)
                                    │
           ┌────────────────────────┴────────────────────────┐
           ▼                                                 ▼
PRIMARY HELPING VERBS                               MODAL AUXILIARIES
• Be forms: is, am, are, was, were, been            • can, could (ability)
• Do forms: do, does, did                           • will, would (future/polite)
• Have forms: has, have, had                        • shall, should (duty/advice)
                                                    • may, might (possibility/permission)
                                                    • must, ought to (necessity/obligation)
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The Modal Law:**
> A Modal is **ALWAYS followed directly by V1 (Base Form)**. Never add `-s`, `-ed`, or `-ing`:
> * ❌ *He can plays cricket.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *He can **play** cricket.*
> * ❌ *You must to go.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *You must **go**.*
> * ❌ *She should studied.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *She should **study**.*

### 🔥 3. The Master Classifications of Verbs (வினைச்சொற்களின் 7 முதன்மை வகைகள்)

In SSC CGL Tier-II, understanding verb taxonomy is essential for spotting errors in Voice, Narration, Subject-Verb Agreement, and Modifiers:

```
                               VERB TAXONOMY ENGINE
                                        │
    ┌───────────────────────────┬───────┴───────────────────┬───────────────────────────┐
    ▼                           ▼                           ▼                           ▼
1. FINITE vs NON-FINITE     2. TRANSITIVE vs            3. LINKING / COPULAR        4. CAUSATIVE VERBS
• Finite: bound by tense       INTRANSITIVE             • Sensory verbs             • Make + Bare Inf.
• Non-Finite:               • Transitive: needs Object    (look, taste, smell, feel)• Get + Person + to V1
  - Infinitive (to + V1)    • Intransitive: no Object   • Strictly takes ADJECTIVE, • Get + Thing + V3
  - Gerund (V-ing Noun)       (Cannot be made passive!)   NEVER an Adverb!          • Have + Person + V1
  - Participle (Adjective)  │                           │                           │
    └───────────────────────────┴─────────────┬─────────────┴───────────────────────────┘
                                              ▼
                             5. STATIVE vs DYNAMIC VERBS
                             6. REGULAR vs IRREGULAR VERBS
                             7. MARGINAL AUXILIARIES (Need / Dare)
```

#### A. Finite vs. Non-Finite Verbs (முற்றுவினை vs. எச்சவினை)
* **Finite Verb (முற்றுவினை):** Bound by the Number, Person of the Subject, and Tense. Every complete sentence **must possess at least one Finite Verb**:
  * *He **goes** to school.* (Singular/Present) / *They **go**.* (Plural) / *He **went**.* (Past).
* **Non-Finite Verb (எச்சவினை):** Not limited by subject person, number, or tense. Performs duties of Nouns, Adjectives, or Adverbs:
  1. **Infinitive (`to + V1`):** Verbal Noun (* **To learn** grammar requires patience*).
  2. **Gerund (`V1 + -ing`):** Verbal Noun (* **Smoking** is injurious to health*).
  3. **Participle (`V-ing` / `V3`):** Verbal Adjective (*The **crying** baby*, *The **broken** mirror*).

#### B. Transitive vs. Intransitive Verbs (செயப்படுபொருள் குன்றா vs. குன்றிய வினைகள்)
* **Transitive Verb (TV):** Passes the action directly to an **Object**. Answers **"What?"** or **"Whom?"**:
  * *Ravi **bought** a car.* (Bought what? $\to$ a car = Object).
  * *The police **arrested** the thief.* (Arrested whom? $\to$ the thief = Object).
* **Intransitive Verb (IV):** The action remains with the subject and **never passes to an object**:
  * *Birds **fly**.* / *The baby **smiled**.* / *He **died** peacefully.*
* ⚠️ **The 2 Crucial Tier-II Laws:**
  1. **Voice Law:** Intransitive verbs **CANNOT be transformed into Passive Voice** (because there is no object to become the new subject!).
  2. **Preposition Law:** Transitive verbs take their direct object **without any preposition**:
     * ❌ *We discussed about the project.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *We **discussed the project**.*
     * ❌ *He ordered for two cups of tea.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *He **ordered two cups of tea**.*

#### C. Linking / Copular Verbs (இணைக்கும் வினைகள் — #1 SSC CGL Tier-II Trap)
Verbs of state, appearance, and sensory perception do not show physical action; they link the subject to a subject complement:
> **be (is, am, are, was, were), seem, appear, look, taste, smell, sound, feel, become, turn, grow**

> [!WARNING]
> **The Golden Adjective Law for Linking Verbs:**
> Because a Linking Verb connects back to the **Subject (Noun/Pronoun)**, it **MUST be modified by an ADJECTIVE**, NEVER an Adverb!
>
> ❌ *The soup tastes deliciously.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *The soup tastes **delicious**.* (Adjective).  
> ❌ *She looks charmingly in that traditional saree.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *She looks **charming**.*  
> ❌ *The roses smell sweetly.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *The roses smell **sweet**.*  
> ❌ *I feel badly about the misunderstanding.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *I feel **bad**.*  
> ❌ *The trumpet sounded loudly.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *The trumpet sounded **loud**.*

#### D. Causative Verbs (பிறவினைச் சொற்கள் — Tier-II Heavyweight)
When the subject does not perform the action directly, but causes someone else or an agent to do it:

| Causative Verb | Construction / Formula | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **MAKE** (கட்டாயப்படுத்துதல்) | $\text{Subject} + \text{make} + \text{Person} + \mathbf{V1\ (Bare\ Inf.)}$ | *The teacher made him **rewrite** the essay.* |
| **GET** (தூண்டுதல் / ஏற்பாடு) | $\text{Active:} \quad \text{Subject} + \text{get} + \text{Person} + \mathbf{to\ +\ V1}$<br>$\text{Passive:} \quad \text{Subject} + \text{get} + \text{Thing} + \mathbf{V3}$ | • *I got the plumber **to repair** the leak.*<br>• *I got the leak **repaired** by the plumber.* |
| **HAVE** (பொறுப்பு ஒப்படைத்தல்) | $\text{Active:} \quad \text{Subject} + \text{have} + \text{Person} + \mathbf{V1}$<br>$\text{Passive:} \quad \text{Subject} + \text{have} + \text{Thing} + \mathbf{V3}$ | • *I had the mechanic **service** my car.*<br>• *I had my car **serviced**.* |
| **LET** (அனுமதித்தல்) | $\text{Subject} + \text{let} + \text{Person} + \mathbf{V1\ (Bare\ Inf.)}$ | *My father let me **drive** the car.* |

#### E. Stative vs. Dynamic Verbs (நிகழ்நிலை vs. செயல்நிலை வினைகள்)
* **Dynamic Verbs:** Physical or mental activities with a clear beginning and end (*eat, run, write, build*). They **can take Continuous (`-ing`) tenses**.
* **Stative Verbs:** Conditions, perception, feelings, or possession (*know, believe, belong, love, remember*). They **strictly forbid Continuous (`-ing`) tenses** in standard meanings.

#### F. Regular vs. Irregular Verbs (வழக்கமான vs. மாறுபட்ட வினைகள்)
* **Regular (Weak Verbs):** Form past and past participle simply by adding `-d` or `-ed` (*laugh $\to$ laughed $\to$ laughed*).
* **Irregular (Strong Verbs):** Undergo internal vowel mutations (*sing $\to$ sang $\to$ sung*) or remain identical in all forms (*put $\to$ put $\to$ put*, *broadcast $\to$ broadcast $\to$ broadcast* — ⚠️ *Never say "broadcasted"*!).

#### G. Marginal Auxiliaries / Semi-Modals (`NEED` and `DARE`)
* **When used as Modals / Auxiliaries:** In negative and interrogative sentences, they take **V1 (Bare Infinitive)** and **never add `-s`**:
  * ❌ *He needs not worry.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *He **need not worry**.* (Modal auxiliary usage).
  * ❌ *She dares not speak.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *She **dare not speak**.*
* **When used as Main Verbs:** They conjugate like regular verbs and take **Full Infinitive (`to + V1`)**:
  * ✅ *He **needs to work** harder.* (Main verb with 'to').
  * ✅ *He **does not dare to enter** the room.*

### 🔥 Top SSC Exam Trap: The Fatal Confusable Verb Matrix (குழப்பும் வினைச்சொற்கள்)
Tier-II examiners frequently set traps using verbs with similar pronunciation or spellings:

| Base Form (V1) | Past (V2) | Past Participle (V3) | -ing Form (V4) | Tamil Meaning | Nature of Verb |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Lie** | **Lay** | **Lain** | **Lying** | படு / சாய்ந்திரு (Recline) | **Intransitive** (செயப்படுபொருள் தேவையில்லை) |
| **Lay** | **Laid** | **Laid** | **Laying** | கீழே வை / முட்டையிடு (Put down) | **Transitive** (செயப்படுபொருள் கட்டாயம் வேண்டும்) |
| **Lie** | **Lied** | **Lied** | **Lying** | பொய் சொல் (Speak untruth) | Regular Verb |
| **Rise** | **Rose** | **Risen** | **Rising** | எழு / மேலெழு (Ascend) | **Intransitive** (*The sun rises*) |
| **Raise** | **Raised** | **Raised** | **Raising** | உயர்த்து / எழுப்பு (Lift something) | **Transitive** (*Raise your hand*) |
| **Fall** | **Fell** | **Fallen** | **Falling** | கீழே விழு (Drop down) | **Intransitive** (*Leaves fall*) |
| **Fell** | **Felled** | **Felled** | **Felling** | மரத்தை வெட்டி சாய்த்தல் (Cut down) | **Transitive** (*Fell the timber*) |

* ❌ *He laid on the grass yesterday to take rest.*  
  *(Analysis: Laying something down? No, he reclined! Past tense of 'recline lie' is **Lay**).*  
  $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *He **lay** on the grass yesterday to take rest.*
* ❌ *Please lie the baby on the bed carefully.*  
  *(Analysis: Placing something down requires Transitive verb **Lay**).*  
  $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *Please **lay** the baby on the bed carefully.*

### 🔥 Top SSC Exam Trap: Prepositional Phrases Mandatory for Gerund (`V4 / -ing`), NOT V1!
The standard rule says *to + V1 (Infinitive)*. However, in these fixed prepositional phrases, **`to` is a preposition**, so it strictly takes a **Gerund (`V4 / -ing`)**:
> **Looking forward to | Accustomed to | Used to | With a view to | Addicted to | Prone to | Averse to | In addition to**

* ❌ *I am looking forward to meet you.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *I am looking forward to **meeting** you.*
* ❌ *He went to Delhi with a view to secure a job.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *He went to Delhi with a view to **securing** a job.*
* ❌ *He is addicted to gamble.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *He is addicted to **gambling**.*

---

### 🔥 Top SSC Exam Trap: Bare Infinitive Rules (Active vs. Passive)
Verbs of perception and causation take a **Bare Infinitive (V1 without *to*)** in Active Voice, but a **Full Infinitive (*to + V1*)** in Passive Voice:
> **Make, Bid, Watch, Hear, See**

* **Active Voice (No 'to'):**
  * ❌ *The teacher made him to apologize.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *The teacher made him **apologize**.*
  * ✅ *I saw him **cross** the road.*
* **Passive Voice (Mandatory 'to'):**
  * ✅ *He was made **to apologize** by the teacher.*
* ⚠️ **Exception (`LET`):** *Let* never takes 'to', even in passive:
  * ✅ *He was let **go** (NOT was let to go).*

# 👑 PART 2: SUBJECT-VERB AGREEMENT (S-V-A) — #1 EXAM PRIORITY

> **The Fundamental Law of English:**
> * **Singular Subject $\longrightarrow$ Singular Verb**
> * **Plural Subject $\longrightarrow$ Plural Verb**

```
Nouns vs. Verbs S-Paradox:
• In NOUNS: adding '-s' makes it PLURAL      (Boy + s  = Boys)
• In VERBS: adding '-s' makes it SINGULAR    (Play + s = Plays)
```

$$\mathbf{He\ /\ She\ /\ It\ /\ Suresh} \quad \longrightarrow \quad \mathbf{is\ /\ was\ /\ has\ /\ plays\ /\ writes\ (-s\ verbs)}$$
$$\mathbf{They\ /\ We\ /\ You\ /\ Boys} \quad \longrightarrow \quad \mathbf{are\ /\ were\ /\ have\ /\ play\ /\ write\ (no\ -s)}$$

---

### 🔥 SVA Rule 1: The "First Subject" Conjunctions
When two subjects are joined by:
> **as well as, along with, together with, in addition to, accompanied by, with, like, unlike, rather than**

The verb **strictly agrees with the FIRST SUBJECT**:

* *Sentence:* *"The captain (S1), along with all his players (S2), (is/are) present."*
  * S1 = *The captain* (Singular).
  * Ignore *players*! The verb must agree with *captain*.
  * ✅ *The captain, along with all his players, **is** present.*
* *Sentence:* *"The teachers (S1), as well as the principal (S2), (was/were) invited."*
  * S1 = *The teachers* (Plural).
  * ✅ *The teachers, as well as the principal, **were** invited.*

---

### 🔥 SVA Rule 2: The "Nearest Subject" Conjunctions
When two subjects are joined by:
> **Either ... or | Neither ... nor | Not only ... but also | Or**

The verb **strictly agrees with the NEAREST SUBJECT** (the one right beside the verb):

* *Sentence:* *"Neither the teacher nor the **students** (was/were) present."*
  * Nearest Subject = *students* (Plural).
  * ✅ *Neither the teacher nor the students **were** present.*
* *Sentence:* *"Either the boys or the **teacher** (is/are) responsible."*
  * Nearest Subject = *teacher* (Singular).
  * ✅ *Either the boys or the teacher **is** responsible.*

---

### 🔥 SVA Rule 3: The "Indefinite Singular" Words
The following words look broad, but are grammatically **100% SINGULAR** and must take a **Singular Verb**:

> **Each, Every, Everyone, Someone, Anyone, No one, Somebody, Nobody, Anybody, Everything, Something, Nothing, Either, Neither, One of the**

* ❌ *Everyone are attending the session.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *Everyone **is** attending the session.*
* ❌ *One of my friends have a car.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *One of my friends **has** a car.* (Subject is "One", not friends!).
* ❌ *Each of the boys were rewarded.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *Each of the boys **was** rewarded.*

---

### 🔥 SVA Rule 4: "A Number of" vs. "The Number of"
* **A number of + Plural Noun** $\longrightarrow$ Takes a **PLURAL Verb** *(means 'many')*.
  * ✅ *A number of students **have** passed.*
* **The number of + Plural Noun** $\longrightarrow$ Takes a **SINGULAR Verb** *(means a specific count)*.
  * ✅ *The number of students **has** increased.*

---

### 🔥 SVA Rule 5: "Many a" vs. "More than one" (ஒன்றுக்கும் மேற்பட்ட ஆனால் ஒருமை)
In Tier-II, SSC examiners deliberately test expressions that feel plural in meaning but are **strictly singular in syntax**:

$$\mathbf{Many\ a\ /\ Many\ an\ +\ Singular\ Noun} \quad \longrightarrow \quad \mathbf{SINGULAR\ VERB}$$
$$\mathbf{More\ than\ one\ +\ Singular\ Noun} \quad \longrightarrow \quad \mathbf{SINGULAR\ VERB}$$
$$\mathbf{More\ than\ two\ /\ three\ +\ Plural\ Noun} \quad \longrightarrow \quad \mathbf{PLURAL\ VERB}$$

* ❌ *Many a soldiers were killed in the war.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *Many a **soldier was** killed in the war.*
* ❌ *More than one person were involved in the fraud.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *More than one person **was** involved in the fraud.*
* ✅ *More than **two persons were** involved in the fraud.*

---

### 🔥 SVA Rule 6: "Nothing but" + Plural Noun
When a sentence opens with **`Nothing but`**, the subject is *Nothing* (Singular). The plural noun that follows is merely the object of the preposition *but*:

$$\mathbf{Nothing\ but\ +\ Plural\ Noun} \quad \longrightarrow \quad \mathbf{SINGULAR\ VERB}$$

* ❌ *Nothing but evergreen trees were seen across the valley.*
* ✅ *Nothing but evergreen trees **was** seen across the valley.* (Subject is *Nothing*!).

---

### 🔥 SVA Rule 7: Collective Nouns — Unanimous vs. Divided
* **Unanimous / United (ஒரே முடிவில் இருக்கும்போது) $\longrightarrow$ SINGULAR VERB:**
  * ✅ *The jury **was** unanimous in its decision.*
  * ✅ *The committee **has** submitted its report.*
* **Divided / Conflict (கருத்து வேறுபாடு ஏற்படும்போது) $\longrightarrow$ PLURAL VERB & PRONOUN:**
  * ❌ *The jury was divided in its opinion.*
  * ✅ *The jury **were** divided in **their** opinions.* (உறுப்பினர்கள் தனித்தனியாக பிரிந்துவிட்டனர்).

---

### 🔥 SVA Rule 8: Plural Units of Distance, Money, and Time
* **Single Collective Whole (ஒற்றைத் தொகையாக / தூரமாகக் கருதும்போது) $\longrightarrow$ SINGULAR VERB:**
  * ✅ *Ten miles **is** a long distance to walk.*
  * ✅ *Fifty thousand rupees **is** a handsome salary.*
* **An amount can remain singular even when distributed:**
  * ✅ *Fifty thousand rupees **was** distributed among the flood victims.* (One total amount.)
  * ✅ *The fifty notes **were** distributed among the flood victims.* (Separate countable items.)
  * Do not choose the verb from *distributed* alone; identify the subject and its meaning. [University of Technology Sydney grammar reference](https://www.uts.edu.au/sites/default/files/HELPS_Grammar_review1_AUT14.pdf).

# ⏳ PART 3: THE 12 ENGLISH TENSES (காலங்கள்)

Tenses pin an action to a specific point on the timeline:

```
                            THE 12 TENSES MATRIX
                  Past (இறந்தகாலம்)  │ Present (நிகழ்காலம்)  │ Future (எதிர்காலம்)
Simple          : wrote              │ writes / write       │ will write
Continuous      : was/were writing   │ is/am/are writing    │ will be writing
Perfect         : had written        │ has/have written     │ will have written
Perfect Contin. : had been writing   │ has/have been writing│ will have been writing
```

---

### Key Exam Tense Rules

#### 1. Simple Present: Habits & Universal Truths
* Habitual actions: *He drinks coffee every morning.*
* Universal truths: *The sun rises in the east.*
* ⚠️ **The Narration Trap:** Even if the reporting verb is in the past, a scientific truth **never changes to past tense**:
  * ❌ *The teacher said that water boiled at 100°C.*
  * ✅ *The teacher said that water **boils** at 100°C.*

#### 2. Simple Past (V2) vs. Present Perfect (`has/have + V3`)
* **Simple Past (V2):** Used when a **specific past time marker** is mentioned (*yesterday, ago, last night, in 2020*):
  * ❌ *I have seen him yesterday.*
  * ✅ *I **saw** him yesterday.*
* **Present Perfect (`has/have + V3`):** Used when the action happened recently without a past time stamp, or connects past action to the present:
  * ✅ *I have completed my homework.*

#### 3. Past Perfect (`had + V3`): The "Earlier of Two Past Actions"
Use **had + V3** to look back from a past reference point to an earlier event. That reference may be in the same sentence or the surrounding context.

* *The train **had left** before we **reached** the station.* Past perfect emphasises the earlier departure.
* *I finished dinner before I left.* Simple past is also possible when the sequence is already clear.
* *I met him yesterday.* Use simple past for an ordinary finished event. Do not mark a standalone past-perfect sentence wrong without checking the surrounding past context.

[British Council: past perfect and context](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/b1-b2/past-perfect).

#### 4. Present Perfect Continuous: "Since" vs. "For"
Used for actions that started in the past and are **still continuing right now**:

$$\text{Subject} + \mathbf{has\ /\ have\ been\ +\ V4} + \text{since / for} + \text{time}$$

* **Since:** Point of origin (*since Monday, since 2015, since morning*).
* **For:** Duration of time (*for 3 hours, for 5 years, for a long time*).
* ❌ *I am living in Chennai for 5 years.*
* ✅ *I **have been living** in Chennai for 5 years.*

#### 5. Stative Verbs — The Continuous Tense Trap (நிகழ்நிலை வினைகள்)
Verbs of perception, cognition, emotion, and possession describe a state, NOT a dynamic action. **They can NEVER be used in the Continuous (`-ing`) form** in their standard meaning:
> **know, believe, understand, remember, recognize, see, hear, smell, taste, like, love, hate, want, prefer, belong, own, possess**

* ❌ *I am knowing the truth.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *I **know** the truth.*
* ❌ *She is loving chocolate ice cream.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *She **loves** chocolate ice cream.*
* ❌ *This car is belonging to me.* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *This car **belongs** to me.*
* ❌ *Are you remembering my name?* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *Do you **remember** my name?*

#### 6. Future Time Clauses (No "Will / Shall" in Time Clauses)
In ordinary future time/condition clauses (**when, as soon as, before, after, until, unless, if, provided**), normally use a present form rather than *will* merely to indicate future time. Present perfect is also possible: *After I have finished, I will call.* This rule does not cover indirect questions (*I wonder when he will arrive*) or *will* expressing willingness. [British Council explanation](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/b1-b2/past-perfect?page=39).

* ❌ *When the chief guest will arrive, the function will begin.*
* ✅ *When the chief guest **arrives**, the function **will begin**.*
* ❌ *As soon as I will receive the letter, I will notify you.*
* ✅ *As soon as I **receive** the letter, I **will notify** you.*

#### 7. The Subjunctive "It is high time" Trap (Tier-II Classic Trap)
When an action is already delayed and should have been done earlier, English uses the **Past Subjunctive (V2)**:

$$\mathbf{It\ is\ high\ time\ /\ It\ is\ time\ /\ It\ is\ about\ time\ +\ Subject} \quad \longrightarrow \quad \mathbf{V2\ (Past\ Simple)}$$
$$\mathbf{It\ is\ time\ +\ to} \quad \longrightarrow \quad \mathbf{V1\ (Infinitive)}$$

* ❌ *It is high time that we start preparing for the exam.*
* ✅ *It is high time that we **started** preparing for the exam.* (Subject *we* triggers **V2**!).
* ✅ *It is time **to start** our preparation.* (No subject $\to$ standard *to + V1*).

# 🎯 PART 4: CONDITIONALS (நிபந்தனை வாக்கியங்கள் — "If" Clauses)

Sentences that state: *"If this condition happens, that result will follow."*

There are 3 primary conditional patterns tested in SSC:

| Type | Name | If-Clause (Condition) | Main Clause (Result) | Example | Tamil Meaning |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0** | **Universal Fact** | `If + Simple Present (V1)` | `Simple Present (V1)` | *If you **heat** ice, it **melts**.* | பனிக்கட்டியை சூடாக்கினால் உருகும். |
| **1** | **Open / Real Future** | `If + Simple Present (V1)` | `will + V1` | *If you **study** hard, you **will clear** the exam.* | படித்தால், தேர்ச்சி அடைவாய். |
| **2** | **Imaginary Present** | `If + Simple Past (V2 / were)` | `would + V1` | *If I **were** a bird, I **would fly** to you.* | நான் பறவையாக இருந்திருந்தால், பறந்து வந்திருப்பேன் (கற்பனை). |
| **3** | **Impossible Past Regret** | `If + had + V3` | `would have + V3` | *If you **had informed** me, I **would have helped** you.* | நீ தகவல் சொல்லியிருந்தால், நான் உதவியிருப்பேன் (ஆனால் சொல்லவில்லை). |

---

### 🔥 Top SSC Exam Trap: Type 3 Conditional Pairing

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The formula is rigid. If one side has `had + V3`, the other side **must** have `would have + V3`:
>
> $$\mathbf{If\ +\ had\ +\ V3} \quad \longleftrightarrow \quad \mathbf{would\ have\ +\ V3}$$

* ❌ *If he had worked hard, he would pass.*
* ❌ *If he worked hard, he would have passed.*
* ✅ *If he **had worked** hard, he **would have passed**.*

---

### 🔥 Top SSC Exam Trap: Inverted Conditionals (Dropping "IF" in Tier-II)
In high-level Tier-II papers, SSC drops the word **"If"** and starts the sentence directly with the helping verb:

| Standard "If" Sentence | Tier-II Inverted Form (Without "If") | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *If you should need any assistance, contact me.* | **Should you need any assistance**, contact me. | Type 1 Inversion |
| *If I were the Prime Minister, I would help.* | **Were I the Prime Minister**, I would help. | Type 2 Inversion (Imaginary) |
| *If he had reached on time, he would have caught the train.* | **Had he reached on time**, he would have caught the train. | Type 3 Inversion (Past Regret) |

* ❌ *Had he worked hard, he would pass the exam.*
* ✅ *Had he worked hard, he **would have passed** the exam.*

---

### 🔥 Top SSC Exam Trap: The "BUT FOR" Construction
The phrase **`But for + Noun`** means *"If it had not been for..."* and takes the Type 3 conditional result:

$$\mathbf{But\ for\ +\ Noun} \quad \longrightarrow \quad \mathbf{would\ have\ +\ V3}$$

* *Example:* *"**But for his timely advice**, I **would have incurred** a massive loss."*  
  *(Tamil Meaning: அவனுடைய சரியான ஆலோசனை மட்டும் இல்லையென்றால், எனக்கு பெரும் நஷ்டம் ஏற்பட்டிருக்கும்).*

# 🏷️ PART 5: QUESTION TAGS (கேள்வி வால்கள்)

A mini-question attached to the end of a statement to seek confirmation or agreement:

```
• Positive Statement  ───►  Negative Tag  (is he?  ──► isn't he?)
• Negative Statement  ───►  Positive Tag  (can't he? ──► can he?)
```

### The 4 Golden Rules of Question Tags:
1. **Rule 1 (Tense & Verb Agreement):** Use the exact same auxiliary verb and tense from the sentence:
   * *Ravi is an engineer, **isn't he?***
   * *They were playing, **weren't they?***
2. **Rule 2 (No Helping Verb $\longrightarrow$ Use Do/Does/Did):**
   * *She writes well, **doesn't she?*** (Present singular $\to$ does).
   * *You like tea, **don't you?*** (Present plural $\to$ do).
   * *He came yesterday, **didn't he?*** (Past $\to$ did).
3. **Rule 3 (The "I am" Exception):**
   * Positive: *I am late, **aren't I?*** (NOT *amn't I* — *amn't* does not exist!).
   * Negative: *I am not late, **am I?***
4. **Rule 4 (Imperative & "Let's"):**
   * Requests/Orders: *Open the door, **will you?***
   * Suggestions with Let's: *Let's start the revision, **shall we?***
5. **Rule 5 (Negative Adverbs $\longrightarrow$ Positive Tag):**
   * Statements containing semi-negative words (**seldom, rarely, hardly, scarcely, barely, neither, little, few**) are treated as negative statements $\to$ their Question Tag must be **POSITIVE**:
   * ❌ *He seldom comes here, doesn't he?* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *He seldom comes here, **does he?***
   * ❌ *She has little knowledge, hasn't she?* $\longrightarrow$ ✅ *She has little knowledge, **has she?***
6. **Rule 6 (Indefinite Person Pronouns $\longrightarrow$ Tag Pronoun "THEY"):**
   * When subjects are **Everyone, Everybody, Someone, Somebody, Anyone, Nobody, None, No one**, the pronoun in the question tag is **`THEY`**, which demands a **PLURAL auxiliary verb**:
   * ❌ *Everyone was satisfied, wasn't he?*
   * ✅ *Everyone was satisfied, **weren't they?*** (*they* cannot take *was* $\to$ must use *were*!).
   * ❌ *Nobody turned up, didn't they?*
   * ✅ *Nobody turned up, **did they?*** (*Nobody* is already negative $\to$ tag must be positive!).
7. **Rule 7 (Inanimate Indefinite Pronouns $\longrightarrow$ Tag Pronoun "IT"):**
   * For **Everything, Something, Nothing, Anything**, the pronoun in the tag is strictly **`IT`**:
   * ✅ *Everything is fine, **isn't it?***
   * ✅ *Nothing was stolen, **was it?*** (*Nothing* is negative $\to$ tag is positive).

# ⚡ PART 6: 10-SECOND RAPID RECALL & EXAM DRILLS

1. **V3 Law:** V3 (*written, gone, eaten*) can never stand alone without a helping verb (*has/have/had*).
2. **Modal Law:** Modals (*can, will, should, must*) are strictly followed by **V1**.
3. **Confusable Verbs:** *Lie/Lay/Lain* (படு - Intransitive) vs. *Lay/Laid/Laid* (கீழே வை - Transitive).
4. **Gerund Trigger:** *Looking forward to, with a view to, accustomed to, addicted to* strictly take **`V4 (-ing)`**, NOT V1.
5. **Bare Infinitive:** *Make, bid, watch, hear, see* take bare infinitive in active (*made him run*), but *to + V1* in passive (*was made to run*).
6. **First Subject Rule:** *As well as, along with, together with* agree with **Subject 1**.
7. **Nearest Subject Rule:** *Either...or, Neither...nor* agree with the **Nearest Subject**.
8. **Many a / More than one:** *Many a + Sing. Noun* and *More than one + Sing. Noun* take a **Singular Verb**.
9. **Nothing but:** *Nothing but + Plural Noun* strictly takes a **Singular Verb**.
10. **Collective Divided:** When jury/committee members disagree, verb and pronoun become **Plural** (*The jury were divided in their opinion*).
11. **Past Time Marker:** *Yesterday, ago, in 2020* strictly take **Simple Past (V2)**, NOT *has/have*.
12. **Earlier Past Action:** **`had + V3`** looks back from a past reference point; simple past can work when the sequence is already clear.
13. **Stative Verbs:** Verbs of perception/thought (*know, believe, belong, love*) never take Continuous (`-ing`).
14. **High Time Rule:** *It is high time + Subject* takes **V2 (Past Simple)** (*It is high time we started*).
15. **Conditional 3 Inversion:** `If + had + V3` inverts to **`Had + Subject + V3`**, pairing strictly with **`would have + V3`**.
16. **Question Tag Pronoun:** *Everyone was present, **weren't they?***
