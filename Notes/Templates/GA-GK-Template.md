## 1. What “school level” means for these exams

**School science is a foundation for learning. It is not a blanket limit on SSC CGL General Awareness.** Use simple explanations while retaining the detail needed to answer relevant exam questions.

**தமிழில்:** பள்ளி அறிவியல் கருத்துகள் அடிப்படை. எளிமையாக விளக்குவது சரி; ஆனால் தேர்வுக்குத் தேவையான தகவல்களையும் ஆழத்தையும் குறைக்கக் கூடாது.

| Exam | Verified baseline and interpretation | How to prepare notes |
|---|---|---|
| RRB NTPC Graduate | CEN 05/2024 specifies **General Science and Life Science up to 10th CBSE** in CBT-1 and CBT-2. Its wider GA syllabus also includes scientific/technological developments and many non-science subjects. | Cover the relevant school-science concepts accurately, then their factual details, applications and PYQ distinctions. Do not apply the Class-10 wording to all of GA or to every RRB recruitment. |
| SSC CGL | The 2026 notice covers everyday scientific awareness and scientific research. It does not prescribe a Class-10-only science boundary. Section 13.10.5 describes Tier-I GA's standard as commensurate with graduation. | Use foundational concepts plus relevant factual depth and PYQ applications. This broad GA standard is not a prescribed B.Sc. Biology/Chemistry course. |
| SSC CGL Tier-II Paper-I | The GA syllabus covers broad general awareness, current events and everyday scientific understanding; it is not a separate specialist biology paper. | Include relevant Tier-II PYQs where available. Do not assume every Tier-II question requires advanced academic theory. |

**Official references:** [RRB NTPC Graduate CEN 05/2024, §§13.1–13.2](https://rrbcdg.gov.in/uploads/202405/Detailed%20CEN%2005-2024%20NTPC.pdf); [SSC CGL notice, §§13.10.2, 13.10.5 and 13.11.4](https://ssc.gov.in/api/attachment/uploads/masterData/NoticeBoards/Notice_of_adv_cgl_2025.pdf). The SSC URL contains `2025`, but the document inspected is headed **Combined Graduate Level Examination, 2026**; use the document heading when identifying it.

**Cycle rule:** These named notices are preparation baselines, not confirmation of 2027 rules. Recheck the applicable official notice before stating a future exam's syllabus, marking, timing or eligibility. Do not silently present the older RRB reference as the latest notification.

**Difficulty rule:** A familiar concept can still produce a difficult question through close options, combined facts, unfamiliar wording or time pressure. School-level content does not guarantee an easy question.

## 3. Language and depth requirements

- Write mainly in simple English. Explain unfamiliar scientific, historical, legal and economic terms at first use.
- Give important terms in Tamil and include a short Tamil explanation of each major concept, method and confusing distinction.
- Keep standard English exam terms beside Tamil meanings so that I can recognise them in questions.
- Start with meaning and a familiar example. Then explain the process, rule or relationship. Add exceptions only after the normal case is clear.
- Keep the depth **foundation → precise facts → connections/applications → PYQs → independent recall**.
- Do not repeatedly use “school level” as a reason to leave out relevant detail. Explain the exact boundary instead: “This test detects starch, not every carbohydrate.”
- Do not add advanced material merely to make the note longer. Every substantial addition needs an exam, syllabus or prerequisite reason.
- Make the notes easy to navigate using text-only explanations, compact tables, clear spacing and purposeful emojis, following Section 3A below.

**தமிழில்:** முதலில் பொருள், அடுத்து விளக்கம், அதன் பின் தேர்வுக்கான தகவல் மற்றும் பயன்பாடு. கடினமான சொல்லை விளக்காமல் பயன்படுத்த வேண்டாம். எளிய மொழியில் தேவையான ஆழத்துடன் கற்பிக்க வேண்டும்.

### 3A. Text-only layout and learning flow

**Do not add images to chapter notes. Do not search for, download, generate or create images, diagrams or image placeholders.** Spend the effort on accurate explanations and a clear learning sequence. Reading diagrams in a supplied PDF for source analysis is still allowed; explain the relevant information in words.

- Open with a short learning route: prerequisites, core ideas, applications, worked practice, revision and independent practice. State which sections to read at each stage and what the learner should be able to do before continuing.
- Teach each concept in the same order: **meaning -> familiar example -> how/why -> key fact -> Tamil recap -> common confusion -> short self-check**. Use only the parts that help; avoid repetitive labels for trivial facts.
- Introduce a technical term before using it in a harder explanation. Put basic functions before exceptions and combined questions.
- Keep supporting additions beside the concept they explain, labelled **Supplement**. Mark recognition-only details so beginners know what to prioritise.
- Use short paragraphs and numbered steps for methods. Use small comparison tables, preferably 3-4 columns; keep long explanations outside tables.
- Keep Tamil directly beside its English concept. Avoid splitting related explanations across distant sections.
- Use consistent heading levels, sequential numbering and blank lines around headings, tables and lists. Do not align text using spaces, tabs or wide ASCII diagrams.
- Use short written sequences for processes; retain the explanation of why each step follows the previous one.
- Give a clear stopping check after each learning stage. A learner who cannot explain the basics should repair that section before harder questions.
- Separate questions from answers. Label worked examples, independent questions, PDF answer guides and publisher-reported PYQs distinctly.
- Finish with a compact recall sheet and an honest progress log. Beginner-to-exam readiness requires successful unaided practice, not merely reading a long note.

**Use emojis as reading cues:** usually one at the start of a major heading or callout, not on every sentence or table cell. Keep their meaning consistent and include a text label:

- 🧠 **Understand / புரிந்துகொள்:** concept explanation.
- 🔬 **Test or process / சோதனை அல்லது செயல்முறை:** experiment or scientific method.
- 📌 **Remember / நினைவில் கொள்:** a concise fact or revision cue.
- ⚠️ **Exam trap / தேர்வில் குழப்பம்:** a distinction or misleading option.
- 📝 **Practice / பயிற்சி:** model questions or independent practice.
- 🔁 **Revise / மீள்பார்வை:** active recall and review.

Use these cues selectively. Emojis must not replace scientific terms, imply a PYQ is verified, reveal checkpoint answers or mark a topic mastered before an actual attempt. Keep filenames, formulas, source citations and question options free of decorative emojis.

## 4. Decide what extra information belongs in the chapter

For each gap in the supplied material, use the following filter. These are study priorities, not measured question-frequency bands.

| Priority | Include when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Core | It is necessary to understand the stated topic or directly supported by a relevant, traceable exam question. | Nutrient functions; a dated vitamin-solubility question |
| Supporting | It explains a core fact, resolves a common ambiguity in the material, or distinguishes plausible options. | Liver produces bile; gallbladder stores it |
| Optional | It provides useful context but has no demonstrated need for the current chapter's preparation. | A brief historical discovery anecdote, identified as a legend if appropriate |
| Omit or move | It is specialist detail, unrelated trivia, a duplicate or an unverified claim. | Detailed industrial machinery design in basic fibre notes |

Record substantial additions in a compact table:

| Added concept | Why needed for SSC CGL / RRB NTPC | Evidence type and reference | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Concept] | [Specific question skill, prerequisite or syllabus connection] | [Official syllabus / traceable PYQ / authoritative textbook] | [Core/supporting/optional] |

Do not describe a concept as “frequently asked”, “most repeated” or “high weightage” without an actual paper sample, dates and counts. A broad syllabus connection is not evidence that a specific question appeared.

## 5. Source analysis and factual accuracy

1. Inspect the supplied material before writing. For scanned PDFs, inspect page images; text extraction alone may omit diagrams or handwritten information.
2. Record the actual reviewed page range. Do not claim full coverage if pages were inaccessible or illegible.
3. Create a short source-coverage map. Combine repeated English/Tamil slides without dropping distinct concepts.
4. Separate **source content**, **added explanation**, **correction**, and **unresolved wording**.
5. Verify disputed facts using appropriate primary or authoritative sources: official notices, NCERT, responsible government departments, legislation, scientific institutions or original research as appropriate.
6. Use coaching sites or books for question attribution only with the appropriate provenance label. Check questionable scientific explanations independently.
7. Preserve the original question's page/reference when correcting it. If no option is fully correct, explain the defect; do not force a scientifically wrong answer to match the key.
8. Give units, dates, populations, geographic boundaries and exceptions where they affect the answer.
9. Avoid unsupported “all”, “only”, “always” and “never” claims. State what a negative test or missing observation can actually establish.
10. Link sources beside the relevant explanation. Do not invent citations, page numbers, exam shifts, question IDs or claims of verification.

## 6. Required chapter-note structure

Use the following sections, adapting their length to the chapter. Skip a section only when it is genuinely inapplicable; do not pad a short topic with empty headings.

### A. Header and scope

- English title + Tamil title.
- Branch, checklist code, target exams, supplied source and date updated.
- What the chapter covers and any important boundary.
- Review status: source reviewed, references checked, PYQ attribution status and unresolved gaps.

### B. Why this belongs in SSC/RRB preparation

- State the official syllabus connection without claiming an official chapter-wise weightage.
- List traceable PYQ connections, if available.
- Distinguish an evidence-backed question pattern from an author-created practice pattern.
- Explain prerequisites before adding harder facts.

### C. Vocabulary and foundation

| English term | Tamil meaning | Simple explanation or example |
|---|---|---|
| [Term] | [Meaning] | [Explanation] |

Give a short everyday analogy when useful. Explain its limit if taking it literally would create a misconception.

### D. Main concepts and factual tables

For each concept, give its meaning, purpose, explanation, relevant facts and Tamil recap. Use compact tables for comparisons and numbered written sequences for processes. Define symbols and units in formulas.

### E. Methods, experiments and sequences

When the topic includes a test or method, explain **how it is done**, not only its result:

1. **Purpose:** What is being tested or identified?
2. **Materials/reagent:** What is used?
3. **Method:** Numbered steps in the correct order.
4. **Observation:** What colour, precipitate or other change occurs?
5. **Inference:** What does that observation support?
6. **Reason:** Briefly explain why the method works, at useful exam depth.
7. **Limit/trap:** What does the test not establish?
8. **Tamil explanation:** Summarise the method and conclusion.

For example, starch notes must include **food sample → dilute iodine → observe blue-black colour → starch detected**, followed by the distinction between starch and other carbohydrates. Protein and fat tests should have similarly complete steps.

Exact drop counts or quantities should be attributed to the specific source procedure, not presented as universal. Include practical precautions proportionate to the materials involved; exam notes are not unsupervised laboratory instructions.

### F. Distinctions, exceptions and corrected statements

| Confusing pair / source statement | Accurate distinction | Reason or example | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Item] | [Correction] | [Explanation] | [Source/page/link] |

### G. Worked model questions

Follow the [Topic Explanation template](Topic%20Explanation.md): at least **five worked models per distinct teaching topic**, grouping closely related subtopics that use the same reasoning.

Progress from first understanding to basic application, variation, combined application, and a trap/exception. Do not label a simple exercise “advanced” merely because it is last.

For each model include:

- **Label:** Generated practice question.
- **Question and concept tested.**
- **Numbered reasoning and final answer.**
- **Why the other options fail**, if options are supplied.
- **Tamil explanation and a mistake to avoid.**

Keep guided examples separate from independent performance evidence.

### H. Supplied questions and PYQ connections

If the PDF includes questions, provide a page-referenced answer guide and explain defective items. A supplied question without exam attribution is **source practice**, even if it resembles a PYQ.

Use these provenance labels consistently:

| Label | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| Verified PYQ | Exact exam, cycle/year, actual test date, tier/stage and shift where applicable; a checkable original paper/response sheet or authoritative record. Record question ID/page when available. State whether the key is tentative or final. |
| Publisher-reported PYQ | A named publisher supplies the exam/date/shift, but the original official record has not been independently checked. Link the exact page and disclose that limit. |
| Source practice | A question appears in the supplied PDF/book without verifiable exam details. Give the source and page. |
| Generated practice question | A new question created for learning. Never attach invented exam details or imply it was actually asked. |

The exam cycle and the date it was held may differ. Record both accurately. Paraphrased or adapted questions must be identified as such; do not present modified wording as the original question.

| Exam/cycle | Test date, tier/stage, shift | Question focus or ID | Answer and reasoning | Source/link | Verification status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Details] | [Details] | [Concept] | [Explanation] | [Reference] | [Label] |

Explain what each PYQ adds to the learning scope. If no reliable PYQ was found, say so and provide clearly labelled practice instead. Do not claim complete PYQ coverage after checking a few examples.

### I. Revision and independent checkpoint

- Provide a compact recall sheet: concept → key fact → distinction.
- Give 10–15 fresh easy questions for an independent checkpoint, with answers and explanations separated from the questions.
- Include a 20-question batch when explicitly requested; keep its answer key separate.
- During interactive teaching, ask one question at a time unless the user requests a batch.
- Do not let the question itself reveal its answer unnecessarily.

### J. Sources, coverage gaps and progress

End with sources, date checked, source errors, unresolved facts and gaps in verified PYQ coverage. Keep completion evidence blank until the learner actually attempts the work.

## 7. Additional requirements by GA/GK branch

| Branch | Include where relevant | Check carefully |
|---|---|---|
| Biology / food / health | Structure-function pairs, nutrients, deficiencies, organisms, life cycles, tests, everyday applications | Disease versus symptom; cause versus association; dietary versus textile fibre; no personal diagnosis or supplement prescription |
| Chemistry | Elements/compounds/mixtures, names and formulas, properties, reactions, reagents, colours, uses | Conditions of reaction; chemical versus physical change; common name versus chemical name |
| Physics | Quantity, unit, instrument, law, formula, everyday application, simple calculation and written process explanation | Units, assumptions, direction and the difference between similar quantities |
| History | Chronology, places, people, events, causes and consequences; dated text tables where useful | BCE/CE; event date versus reign; source uncertainty; legend versus documented history |
| Art and culture | Art form, region, tradition, period, identifying feature, major associations | Similar names; shared regional traditions; awards and recognitions tied to dates |
| Geography / environment | Locations, directions, neighbouring regions, physical processes, climate, rivers, soils, ecosystems and conservation; explain spatial relationships in text | Geographic boundaries; source year of rankings; longest/largest with the exact measurement criterion |
| Polity | Constitutional terms, institutions, powers, functions, articles/schedules/amendments where relevant | Current law versus historical provision; constitutional body versus statutory body; office versus officeholder |
| Economics | Meaning of indicators, institutions, basic mechanisms, budgets and everyday examples | Nominal versus real; stock versus flow; base year and reporting period; proposed versus implemented policy |
| Static GK | Books/authors, awards, sports, organisations, days, instruments and discoveries | Category, edition, year, spelling, location and qualifying words such as “first” |
| Current affairs | What happened, who, where, event date, why relevant and the static-GK connection | Event date versus publication date; announcement versus implementation; source and “as of” date |
| Computers | Use the linked computer checklist and maintain one progress record | Do not duplicate a full computer course inside every GA chapter |

These are content checks, not a claim that every listed detail is needed in every chapter. Select them according to the topic, official scope and traceable questions.

## 8. Time-sensitive facts and historical PYQ answers

- Date-check officeholders, rankings, records, awards, schemes, economic figures, laws and scientific developments.
- Attach the edition/year and metric to rankings or “largest/highest” claims.
- Preserve what an old question asked at its exam date. If today's answer differs, show **exam-year answer** and **current position** separately with sources.
- Do not infer a fixed current-affairs month window or chapter weightage without evidence. Follow the existing plan and verified current guidance.
- Do not generalise NTPC rules to Group D, ALP, Technician or JE, or CGL rules to every SSC examination. Those exams can have different scopes.

## 9. Practice, evidence and revision

Use **concepts → worked models → unaided easy practice → relevant PYQs → timed practice → retention checks**.

- Reach **70% unaided on an easy set** before harder PYQs. Below that, repair the specific gaps and retest within the existing study blocks.
- Separate guided, independent, timed and retention results. Reading a note, recognising an answer or making a correct guess does not prove mastery.
- Review errors after **7 and 21 days** using the existing plan. Use fresh recall questions if there were no errors.
- Apply the plan's Green/Amber/Red rules. Do not mark Green based only on one checkpoint or generate a fictional score.
- Use the applicable exam's verified marking scheme for timed mocks. A short practice drill is not an official per-question time limit.
